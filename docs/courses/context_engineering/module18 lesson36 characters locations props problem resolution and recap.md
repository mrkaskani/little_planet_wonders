## Lesson 36 of 72 — Characters, Locations, Props, Problem, Resolution, and Recap

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes

# 1. Learning objective

- Combine episode-specific story information into one planning card.
- Select only the characters, locations, and props required.
- Define starting state, problem, resolution, participation, and recap.
- Separate episode context from permanent series context.
- Create an Episode Context Card.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Permanent project documents explain who the characters are and how the world works. An Episode Context Card explains what is happening now.

Without a clear episode card, temporary information may be confused with permanent identity. A character may wear rain clothing in one episode and accidentally keep it forever. A prop may move locations without tracking. A temporary emotional state may be mistaken for the character’s normal personality.

The Episode Context Card gathers the current learning, story, visual, audio, and continuity needs before scene breakdown begins.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Episode-specific context

Information true for this episode: learning goal, weather, selected characters, active props, current clothing, temporary problem, and emotional change.

## 2. Character selection

Use only the characters needed for learning, story, safety, and emotional support. Extra characters increase dialogue and visual complexity.

## 3. Location selection

Choose locations that naturally support the goal and problem. Too many location changes consume time and complicate continuity.

## 4. Prop selection

List every important prop and its starting state. Decorative props belong only when they support location identity or composition.

## 5. Starting state

Record what characters know, feel, wear, hold, and intend before the story changes.

## 6. Main problem

State the problem in one concrete sentence.

## 7. Resolution

Describe the final visible state and how the learning goal creates or supports it.

## 8. Participation moments

Record where the child is invited to observe, answer, repeat, or move.

## 9. Audio plan summary

Identify dialogue, narration, ambience, Foley, essential effects, music, and silence needs at episode level.

## 10. Continuity plan

Track temporary states that must continue: prop holder, clothing, weather, time, knowledge, and emotional change.

## 11. Final recap

Record the exact learning and emotional lines planned for the ending.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Episode Context Card — *The Red Ball Finds a Home*

**Characters:** Lumi, Pip, Auntie Noura

**Location:** Rainbow Garden

**Props:** Red ball, red basket, blue basket

**Starting state:** The ball is clean and dry on the path. Baskets are beside the storage bench. Lumi and Pip are calmly putting items away.

**Problem:** The red ball is not in its basket.

**Resolution:** Lumi matches the red ball with the red basket and places it inside.

**Participation:** Choose which basket matches.

**Temporary knowledge change:** Pip learns to compare one color at a time.

**Final recap:** “Red ball. Red basket. They match.”

**Emotional ending:** “We looked again and found its home.”

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Create an Episode Context Card using the approved project documents.

Include:
- learning and emotional goals;
- target vocabulary;
- only required characters;
- character starting and ending knowledge;
- starting and ending emotion;
- selected locations;
- weather and time;
- important props and wardrobe;
- familiar beginning;
- main problem;
- attempts;
- helpful discovery;
- resolution;
- participation moments;
- exact final recap;
- episode-level audio summary;
- continuity plan.

**Permanent context must not be rewritten**  
Character identity, world rules, safety rules, visual style, voice identity, and series promise remain unchanged.

**Output requirements**  
Produce one concise but complete planning card for scene breakdown.

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> Use all characters, many locations, lots of props, several problems, and decide the ending later.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Extra characters increase complexity.
- Many locations consume time and weaken continuity.
- Props are not tracked.
- Several problems compete with the learning goal.
- An undefined ending prevents proper setup and continuity.
- Temporary and permanent context may become confused.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Use Lumi, Pip, and Auntie Noura in the Rainbow Garden. Include only the red ball, red basket, and blue basket as active props.

Define the starting holder, location, and state of each prop. State one problem: the red ball is outside its basket. Define the final visible resolution before scene planning: the ball is inside the red basket, the path is clear, and the characters feel reassured.

Include one participation moment and the exact recap line. Track Pip’s new understanding as an approved episode knowledge update.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Using every recurring character.
2. Changing locations without need.
3. Leaving prop state undefined.
4. Writing scenes before defining resolution.
5. Confusing temporary emotion with personality.
6. Adding audio planning too late.
7. Forgetting ending knowledge.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

What is episode context?

### Answer

Information that is true for the current episode rather than permanently true for the series.
### Question 2

Why limit characters?

### Answer

To preserve clarity, pacing, and visual simplicity.
### Question 3

Why define resolution early?

### Answer

It guides setup, story beats, and continuity.
### Question 4

What should the continuity plan track?

### Answer

Props, wardrobe, weather, time, knowledge, emotion, and unresolved details.
### Question 5

Should episode context rewrite permanent identity?

### Answer

No.
