# 1. Learning objective

- Prepare each final dialogue line for exact voice generation.
- Create clear pronunciation guidance for names, learning words, and difficult phrases.
- Select the correct emotional reference without replacing the final line.

- Create a TTS Direction Sheet.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

The final dialogue script is not ready for speaking-video production until each line has been prepared for pronunciation and performance. A line can be grammatically correct but still fail because a character name is pronounced differently, the target learning word is rushed, a pause falls in the wrong place, or the emotional delivery becomes too intense.

For children under five, pronunciation must be especially clear because the episode may be teaching the word itself. Emotional direction must also remain proportional. A mild concern line should not sound frightened, and a happy line should not become loud or breathless.

An emotional voice reference preserves identity and general delivery style. It does not replace the exact final line. This lesson creates the direction package that connects the approved script to clean exact dialogue generation.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Exact final line

The exact final line is the locked wording the character will speak. Every article, repeated word, pause, and pronunciation matters. Do not direct a line that may still be rewritten.

## 2. Pronunciation preparation

Mark syllable divisions, word stress, vowel clarity, difficult consonants, invented names, culturally specific names, and important educational vocabulary.

## 3. Learning-word priority

Target vocabulary should remain easy to hear. It may receive slight emphasis or a small pause around it, but should not be stretched unnaturally.

## 4. Emotional reference selection

Choose the approved reference that best matches the line’s emotional function: neutral-friendly, happy-gentle, curious, mild concern, reassuring, singing, or bedtime narration.

## 5. Reference versus imitation

The new line should preserve identity and emotional style, not copy the reference’s exact rhythm when the wording requires different timing.

## 6. Pace

Define the overall speed and the speed of key phrases. Preschool speech should usually remain moderate or slightly slow, with clear word boundaries.

## 7. Pause placement

Pauses may support thinking, meaning, participation, or emotional comfort. A pause should have a purpose and should not split a phrase unnaturally.

## 8. Emphasis

Emphasize only the most important word or phrase. Too much emphasis makes the line theatrical and may confuse the learning focus.

## 9. Breath

Plan a natural breath when a line is longer. Avoid audible gasping, breathless delivery, or a breath that interrupts the target word.

## 10. Maximum intensity

Use the Character Voice Bible to define the strongest allowed pitch, energy, pace, and volume. The direction must remain inside that range.

## 11. Listening context

The performance should consider what happens immediately before and after the line. A reply may need a small listening pause before speaking.

## 12. Approval readiness

A line is ready for exact audio generation only when wording, pronunciation, emotion, pace, pauses, emphasis, and reference selection are all approved.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Final line

> “The red ball belongs in the red basket.”

## Pronunciation direction

- **Red:** clear short vowel; do not rush.
- **Belongs:** stress the second syllable lightly.
- **Basket:** clear first syllable; avoid swallowing the final consonant.

## Emotional direction

Happy-gentle, not excited.

## Selected reference

Lumi — happy-gentle reference.

## Pace

Medium-slow.

## Pause

A small natural pause after “ball.”

## Emphasis

Gentle emphasis on the final phrase “red basket.”

## Maximum intensity

No shouting, no pitch jump, no exaggerated smile in the voice.

This direction gives the voice system enough guidance while preserving natural speech.

Prepare a TTS Direction Sheet for one exact preschool dialogue line.

**Permanent context**

[Line]

- pronunciation for every difficult or important word;
- selected emotional reference;
- emotional purpose;
- pace;
- pauses;
- emphasis;
- breath;
- energy;
- volume;
- maximum intensity;
- what happens before and after the line.

**Elements allowed to change**  
Line-specific pause, emphasis, breath, and emotional shade.

**Elements that must remain unchanged**  
Exact wording, character identity, accent, pronunciation rules, vocal age, and approved emotional limits.

Clear vocabulary, comfortable pace, no shouting, no breathless delivery, no exaggerated emotion, and no swallowed words.

Produce a complete direction sheet ready for exact clean dialogue generation.

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- “Happy” is too broad.
- No emotional reference is selected.
- Pronunciation is not defined.
- Pace, pauses, emphasis, and breath are missing.
- Maximum intensity is not protected.
- The learning words may be rushed or unclear.
- The performance context before and after the line is missing.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Use Lumi’s approved happy-gentle emotional reference. Preserve her warm childlike identity, medium-high comfortable pitch, moderate energy, and clear articulation.

Say the exact line: “The red ball belongs in the red basket.”

Use a medium-slow pace. Add a small pause after “ball.” Keep “red” clear both times and place gentle emphasis on “red basket.” Do not shout, rush, stretch the words, add laughter, or increase pitch dramatically.

The line follows a short listening moment and ends with a calm look toward the basket.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Directing a line that may still change.
2. Choosing a reference only because it sounds attractive.
3. Overemphasizing every learning word.
4. Adding unnatural pauses.
5. Making curiosity sound like constant questioning.
6. Making happiness sound loud.
7. Forgetting what happens immediately before the line.
8. Skipping pronunciation review.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create three TTS Direction Sheets.

For each line:
1. Confirm exact wording.
2. Mark difficult and learning words.
3. Choose one emotional reference.
4. Define pace and pauses.
5. Define one emphasis point.
6. Add breath direction if needed.
7. Define maximum intensity.
8. Add before-and-after context.
9. Review all three for voice consistency.
