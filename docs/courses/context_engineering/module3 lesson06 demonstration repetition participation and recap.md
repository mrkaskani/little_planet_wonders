# Module 3

## Lesson 6 of 72 — Demonstration, Repetition, Participation, and Recap

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Turn a learning goal into a complete child-friendly learning pattern.
- Show concepts through visible action before giving explanations.
- Use varied repetition and participation without pressure.
- Create a reusable Learning Pattern Template.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

A learning goal on a planning page does not automatically become understandable on screen. Children need a sequence that makes the idea visible, names it clearly, repeats it with purpose, lets them participate, applies it to the story, and closes with a concise recap. If explanation comes before demonstration, or if the final recap introduces new information, the episode becomes harder to follow.

This lesson converts the Learning Goal Card into an actual creative pattern that can shape scenes, shots, dialogue, sound, and editing.


## Recurring example series

Throughout these lessons, examples come from **Lumi and Pip’s Little Garden**, a gentle animated series for children approximately two to five, designed primarily for ages three to four.

- **Lumi:** a curious four-year-old child who learns by observing, asking questions, and trying again.
- **Pip:** a small teal garden bird who notices patterns and invites audience participation.
- **Auntie Noura:** a calm adult guide who models safe behavior without taking over every problem.
- **Main locations:** the Rainbow Garden, the Cozy Workshop, and the Little Pond Path.
- **Visual identity:** rounded 2D cut-paper shapes, soft texture, readable silhouettes, simple backgrounds, warm daylight, and stable eye-level cameras.
- **Audio identity:** short dialogue, warm voices, restrained music, gentle Foley, quiet observation space, and no sudden loud sounds.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Demonstrate before explaining

Let the child see the red ball, the blue basket, and the red basket before hearing a long explanation. Concrete evidence should carry much of the meaning.

## 2. Learning beat

A learning beat is a short unit where one concept is shown, named, or practiced. It should contain one dominant idea and enough time to observe it.

## 3. Introduction

Present the concept in a familiar context. The first mention should be simple: “A red ball.”

## 4. Guided demonstration

Characters show how to notice or use the concept. Lumi compares the ball with one basket at a time.

## 5. Purposeful repetition

Repeat the key idea through multiple channels: word, visual placement, gesture, sound cue, and action. Keep the concept stable while the story state changes.

## 6. Participation

Invite the child to identify, point, repeat, compare, or predict. Prepare the visual choices, ask one question, pause, confirm, and continue.

## 7. Application

Use the concept to solve the story problem. The ball goes into the red basket because the colors match.

## 8. Recap

Repeat the primary learning idea after the solution. Do not introduce a new rule or several unrelated facts at the end.

## 9. Caregiver takeaway

Optionally suggest a safe everyday extension: “You can look for red objects together.” It should not require purchasing materials.


---

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

### Learning pattern for the red-ball episode

1. **Introduce:** Lumi sees the ball. “A red ball.”
2. **Demonstrate:** She holds it beside the blue basket. “Red and blue do not match.”
3. **Repeat with variation:** She holds it beside the red basket. “Red and red match.”
4. **Participate:** “Which basket matches the red ball?”
5. **Apply:** Lumi places the ball inside the red basket.
6. **Recap:** “Red ball. Red basket. They match.”
7. **Reassure:** “You looked carefully.”

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Turn the following preschool learning goal into a complete learning pattern.

[Goal]

Create:
1. a visible introduction;
2. a guided demonstration;
3. three purposeful repetitions using different forms;
4. one participation moment with visible preparation and a pause;

6. one concise recap;

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> Explain the color red several times and ask the child lots of questions.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- It prioritizes explanation instead of visible demonstration.
- “Several times” does not define purposeful variation.
- Many questions may interrupt comprehension.
- No application or recap is defined.
- The prompt does not protect observation and response time.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Show one red ball clearly. Name it once. Compare it with one blue basket, then one red basket. Repeat “red” through naming, comparison, the child’s participation question, the matching action, and the recap. Ask only one main question after both choices are visible. Keep the camera stable and music quiet during the pause. Apply the answer by placing the ball in the red basket.

---

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Explaining before showing.
2. Repeating identical dialogue mechanically.
3. Using several participation questions in one beat.
4. Adding unrelated facts during application.
5. Introducing new vocabulary in the recap.
6. Making the caregiver takeaway complicated or commercial.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

Why demonstrate before explaining?

### Answer

Visible action gives concrete meaning to the words.

### Question 2

What is purposeful repetition?

### Answer

The concept returns through connected forms that reinforce meaning.

### Question 3

What should the application do?

### Answer

Use the learning concept to solve or advance the story.

### Question 4

Should the recap introduce a new lesson?

### Answer

No. It should reinforce the primary goal.

### Question 5

What must happen before a question?

### Answer

The relevant object or choices must be visible and understandable.
