# Module 28

## Lesson 58 of 72 — Visual, Motion, Identity, Safety, and Continuity Review

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Review generated shots systematically rather than by general impression.
- Separate visual quality, motion, identity, prop, camera, safety, audio, and continuity findings.
- Select the last usable frame and identify boundary problems.
- Approve, reject, or request local repair with specific evidence.
- Create a Shot Review Sheet.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

A generated clip may look attractive while still being unusable. The character may drift slightly, the ball may duplicate, the camera may move, the mouth may speak silently, the hand may pass through the prop, or the final frame may not match the next segment.

Review must be systematic. The reviewer should compare the output with the approved first frame, last frame, prompt, Continuity Ledger, and safety rules.

The result should be a clear decision: approve, trim, locally repair, regenerate, or reject. Vague feedback such as “make it better” does not help.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. First-frame match

Confirm that the clip begins from the approved state or from a frame close enough to trim safely.

## 2. Last-frame match

Confirm that the intended action reaches the approved ending state.

## 3. Identity review

Check face, hair, body proportions, clothing, colors, accessories, and voice identity where relevant.

## 4. Motion review

Check path, speed, balance, contact, missing action, extra action, frozen action, and unnatural acceleration.

## 5. Prop review

Check duplication, disappearance, holder, hand, scale, material, orientation, and state.

## 6. Camera review

Check shot size, perspective, shake, zoom, reframing, screen direction, and background geometry.

## 7. Mouth and speech review

For non-speaking shots, mouth stays controlled. For speaking shots, words and lip timing match.

## 8. Safety review

Check copyable behavior, frightening motion, flashing, sudden intensity, unsafe distance, and adult supervision.

## 9. Continuity review

Compare entering and exiting state with neighboring shots and the ledger.

## 10. Final usable frame

The actual last frame may be distorted. Select the last stable usable frame and update the boundary if approved.

## 11. Trim versus regenerate

Small unwanted motion at the beginning or end may be trimmed. Identity, missing action, prop, camera, or safety failures normally require repair or regeneration.

## 12. Specific feedback

Report exact frame range, failed element, expected behavior, and recommended local action.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Review finding

The pickup action succeeds, but during the final half-second:

- Lumi’s left hand slides through the ball.
- The camera pushes in slightly.
- Her mouth opens.
- The ball remains correctly held until just before the final frame.

## Decision

Trim to the last stable frame before the hand distortion.

## Boundary update

Use the selected stable frame as the approved end boundary.

## Follow-up

Recheck the next segment against the updated boundary. No full regeneration is required if the trim preserves duration and continuity.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Review a generated preschool shot against:
- approved first frame;
- approved last frame;
- motion or speaking prompt;
- Continuity Ledger;
- Character, Location, Prop, Camera, Audio, and Safety Bibles.

Report:
- first-frame match;
- identity;
- motion;
- prop;
- camera and background;
- mouth and audio;
- safety;
- continuity;
- last usable frame;
- decision;
- exact correction.

Do not approve based only on attractiveness.

**Output requirements**  
Produce a completed Shot Review Sheet with evidence-based approval, trim, repair, regeneration, or rejection.

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> The clip looks mostly good. Fix the weird parts.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- No reference comparison is identified.
- “Mostly good” does not state whether it is usable.
- “Weird parts” is not actionable.
- Identity, prop, camera, safety, and continuity are not separated.
- No frame range is identified.
- No decision or local-repair plan exists.
- The last usable frame is not selected.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Review the clip against the approved boundaries and ledger.

At 2.8–3.1 seconds, Lumi’s left hand passes through the ball and her mouth opens in a non-speaking shot. The camera also moves slightly closer.

The action is correct before 2.8 seconds. Select the last stable frame before the distortion. Approve with trim if the selected frame still matches the intended ending state. Otherwise regenerate only the pickup segment with explicit hand-contact, closed-mouth, and locked-camera constraints.

---

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Reviewing only the middle of the clip.
2. Ignoring first and last boundaries.
3. Approving small identity drift.
4. Trimming without updating continuity.
5. Calling failures “weird.”
6. Regenerating everything unnecessarily.
7. Skipping safety review.
8. Failing to record the decision.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

Should an attractive clip be approved automatically?

### Answer

No.
### Question 2

What is the last usable frame?

### Answer

The final stable frame suitable for editing or chaining.
### Question 3

When can trimming be enough?

### Answer

When the approved action and continuity remain intact before the defect.
### Question 4

What failures usually require regeneration?

### Answer

Identity, prop, missing action, camera, safety, or major continuity failures.
### Question 5

What makes feedback useful?

### Answer

Specific element, frame range, expected result, and correction.
