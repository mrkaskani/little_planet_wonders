**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes

# 1. Learning objective

- Review final speaking shots for word accuracy and believable mouth timing.
- Detect missing, added, repeated, delayed, or visually unsupported words.
- Separate audio errors from visual lip-sync errors.
- Choose the correct repair: trim, audio regeneration, speaking-shot regeneration, or replacement coverage.
- Create an Audio-Picture QC Sheet.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

A speaking shot can appear generally synchronized while still missing one important word, opening the mouth too early, closing too late, or using the wrong facial emphasis. For educational content, a missing target word is a serious failure.

Quality control must compare the exact script, locked audio, and final picture together. The reviewer should not judge lip sync only by general visual impression.

Some problems come from the audio master. Others come from the generated mouth performance. The repair must match the source. Replacing the soundtrack under an old speaking clip usually does not solve the problem.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Script-audio-picture comparison

Review the exact written line, approved audio, and visible mouth movement together.

## 2. Missing-word audio error

If the locked audio itself lacks the word, regenerate the audio and then the speaking shot.

## 3. Missing-word picture error

If the audio contains the word but the mouth does not visibly articulate it, regenerate or replace the speaking shot.

## 4. Added or repeated word

Any unapproved word or repetition requires audio and picture review. The final content must match the script exactly.

## 5. Onset timing

The mouth should prepare naturally and begin with the voice, not noticeably before or after.

## 6. Offset timing

The mouth should settle after the final spoken sound, not continue moving.

## 7. Pause timing

Silent pauses should show listening, thinking, or natural held expression rather than random mouth movement.

## 8. Facial emphasis

The visible expression should support the meaning and emotional reference without exaggeration.

## 9. Head and body synchronization

Nods, turns, or gestures should align with meaning, not with every syllable.

## 10. Coverage repair

A cutaway to the learning object or listener may hide a short imperfect mouth section only when the exact audio remains clear and the edit is honest and understandable.

## 11. Regeneration threshold

Missing target vocabulary, identity drift, severe mouth distortion, or incorrect timing normally requires regeneration.

## 12. Final whole-line review

Watch the line at normal speed several times. Slow review may help diagnose, but approval must be based on normal viewing.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Exact line

> “The red ball belongs in the red basket.”

## Audio check

All words are present and correctly pronounced.

## Picture problem

The mouth visibly skips “belongs in” and jumps from “ball” to “the red basket.”

## Diagnosis

Audio is correct. Speaking-shot lip performance is incorrect.

## Correct repair

Regenerate the speaking shot from the same locked audio and approved image. Emphasize natural articulation through the middle phrase without exaggerated mouth movement.

## Incorrect repair

Replace the audio or stretch the picture without regenerating the mouth.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Review one final preschool speaking shot for audio-picture quality.

Compare:
- exact script;
- locked clean audio;
- visible mouth movement;
- expression;
- head and body performance;
- edit timing.

Check:
- missing words;
- added words;
- repeated words;
- pronunciation;
- mouth onset and ending;
- phrase timing;
- pauses;
- target-vocabulary visibility;
- expression and gesture;
- identity;
- child clarity.

Identify whether the error comes from script, audio, picture, or edit. Recommend the smallest correct repair.

**Important rule**
Do not replace final audio under an incompatible mouth performance.

**Output requirements**
Produce an Audio-Picture QC Sheet with an approval or repair decision.

# 7. Weak prompt

> The lip sync is close enough. Hide the bad parts with music and quick cuts.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Music cannot correct mouth timing.
- Quick cuts may hide missing educational words.
- Exact script and audio are not compared.
- The error source is not diagnosed.
- Target vocabulary may become unclear.
- The final quality standard is reduced to general impression.
- Child comprehension is ignored.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Compare the exact script, locked audio, and visible mouth movement word by word.

Identify whether the problem exists in the audio or picture. Regenerate audio and the speaking shot when the audio is wrong. Regenerate the speaking shot when the audio is correct but mouth timing is wrong.

Use a cutaway only when it preserves the exact clear audio, does not hide important learning information, and maintains natural continuity. Do not use music or fast cutting to disguise a failed educational line.

---

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Approving general mouth movement.
2. Checking audio without picture.
3. Checking picture without script.
4. Using music to hide failure.
5. Stretching incompatible video.
6. Using a cutaway over important visual learning.
7. Skipping normal-speed review.
8. Failing to regenerate after line changes.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

What three sources must be compared?

### Answer

Script, locked audio, and picture.
### Question 2

What if audio lacks a word?

### Answer

Regenerate audio and the speaking shot.
### Question 3

What if audio is correct but the mouth skips a phrase?

### Answer

Regenerate or replace the speaking shot.
### Question 4

Can music fix lip sync?

### Answer

No.
### Question 5

When can a cutaway help?

### Answer

When exact audio remains clear and important learning is not hidden.
