**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Locations are not decorative backgrounds. They control action possibilities, safety, camera choices, ambience, prop storage, lighting, entrances, exits, and continuity. If the workshop shelf changes position in every episode, the pond rail disappears, or characters travel instantly without a defined fantasy rule, visual and story continuity break.

Recurring locations also help young children orient themselves. Recognizable landmarks, stable colors, repeated sounds, and familiar safe zones reduce cognitive load and make learning objects easier to notice.


## Recurring example series

Throughout these lessons, examples come from **Lumi and Pip’s Little Garden**, a gentle animated series for children approximately two to five, designed primarily for ages three to four.

- **Lumi:** a curious four-year-old child who learns by observing, asking questions, and trying again.
- **Pip:** a small teal garden bird who notices patterns and invites audience participation.
- **Auntie Noura:** a calm adult guide who models safe behavior without taking over every problem.
- **Main locations:** the Rainbow Garden, the Cozy Workshop, and the Little Pond Path.
- **Visual identity:** rounded 2D cut-paper shapes, soft texture, readable silhouettes, simple backgrounds, warm daylight, and stable eye-level cameras.
- **Audio identity:** short dialogue, warm voices, restrained music, gentle Foley, quiet observation space, and no sudden loud sounds.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Location purpose

Every recurring location should support certain story and learning functions. The Rainbow Garden supports colors and nature; the Cozy Workshop supports shapes and making; the Little Pond Path supports calm observation and water-safety behavior.

## 2. Stable layout

Define major landmarks, entrances, exits, safe zones, storage areas, and object positions. Camera angles may vary, but the spatial relationship should remain consistent.

## 3. Child-readable landmarks

Use a small number of memorable features: yellow gate, blue watering station, red storage basket, sunflower sign. Landmarks help children and creators understand orientation.

## 4. Safe spaces and boundaries

Record rails, gates, adult-only shelves, tool storage, walking paths, seating, and supervision points. Safety features must not disappear in generated images.

## 5. Transportation and movement

Define how characters travel: walking with adults, short garden paths, supervised neighborhood travel, or approved fantasy transitions. Avoid unexplained dangerous travel.

## 6. Nature behavior

Weather, plants, insects, animals, and water should follow the World Rules. Nature can create gentle change but should not become threatening without a carefully reviewed purpose.

## 7. Lighting and time

Create approved day, late-afternoon, indoor, rainy, and calm night variations while preserving location identity and child comfort.

## 8. Ambience identity

Each location should have a recognizable but restrained sound profile. The garden may include soft birds and leaves; the workshop has room tone and gentle material sounds; the pond path has water and distant insects.

## 9. Camera-friendly views

Plan exterior, interior, wide, reverse, activity, close demonstration, entrance, and safety-boundary views. These references support consistent first and last frames later.

## 10. Location continuity state

Track weather, time, open or closed gates, moved props, wet or dry surfaces, decorations, and temporary damage. Temporary state must not silently become permanent.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

### Rainbow Garden location identity

- Yellow gate on the west side.
- Curved central path.
- Blue watering station near the herb beds.
- Red storage basket beside the low bench.
- Sunflower sign facing the entrance.
- Adult-supervised tool shed remains closed during ordinary child scenes.
- Soft morning daylight and gentle leaf ambience.

If a shot shows the red basket on the opposite side, removes the gate, adds a deep pond, or changes the path into stairs, it conflicts with the Location Bible.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Design a recurring preschool location using the approved World Rules and Series Bible.

Include:
- story and learning purpose;
- stable layout;
- three to five memorable landmarks;
- entrances, exits, and walking paths;
- safe and supervision-required areas;
- recurring props and storage;
- palette and lighting variations;
- weather behavior;
- ambience identity;
- camera-friendly reference views;
- temporary-state fields;
- elements allowed to change;
- elements that must remain unchanged;
- child-safety and continuity review criteria.

Do not create a visually crowded environment. Keep the main action area readable on a small screen.

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> Create a beautiful magical garden with many flowers, ponds, bridges, moving decorations, animals, toys, lights, and surprises everywhere.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- The scene is visually crowded and lacks one learning focus.
- Multiple ponds, bridges, animals, and moving objects create safety and continuity problems.
- No stable layout or landmarks are defined.
- The location purpose and camera views are missing.
- “Surprises everywhere” conflicts with predictability and comfort.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Design the Rainbow Garden as a recurring, readable preschool location. Use one curved path, three rounded flower beds, a yellow entrance gate, a blue watering station, a low bench, a red storage basket, and a sunflower sign. Keep the background simple and reserve the central path for character action.

Include one clearly separated pond path only outside the main garden area, protected by a gate and viewing rail. Use soft daylight, minimal background motion, gentle leaf ambience, and stable eye-level camera views. Preserve landmark positions across all reference images.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Designing a location only as a beautiful wide image.
2. Changing layout between views.
3. Removing barriers in later shots.
4. Adding too many landmarks.
5. Using weather only for drama.
6. Failing to track moved props or wet surfaces.
7. Creating camera angles that cannot match during shot continuity.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create one full Location Card and outlines for two additional recurring locations.

For the full card:
1. State purpose.
2. Draw or describe the layout.
3. Choose five landmarks.
4. Define entrances, exits, safe areas, and restricted areas.
5. List recurring props and storage.
6. Define palette, lighting, weather, and ambience.
7. List six camera-friendly views.
8. Add temporary-state fields and review criteria.

Then assemble the World Rules and location cards into World Bible — Draft 1.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

Why are locations more than backgrounds?

### Answer

They control action, safety, camera, sound, props, and continuity.

### Question 2

What makes a landmark useful?

### Answer

It is memorable, stable, and helps orientation without crowding the frame.

### Question 3

Why track temporary state?

### Answer

Weather, wetness, moved props, and open gates should not change silently.

### Question 4

What is a camera-friendly view?

### Answer

A planned reference angle that supports recurring shots and continuity.

### Question 5

Should safety barriers be treated as optional decoration?

### Answer

No. They are permanent location context.
