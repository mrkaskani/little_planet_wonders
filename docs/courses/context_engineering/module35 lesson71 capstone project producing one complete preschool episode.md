# Module 35

## Lesson 71 of 72 — Capstone Project Producing One Complete Preschool Episode

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Apply the complete course workflow to plan, produce, review, and deliver one short preschool episode.
- Use authoritative project context rather than improvising identity, safety, story, or production rules.
- Create every required planning, dialogue, reference, shot, continuity, audio, review, and delivery artifact.
- Diagnose failures locally and preserve approved work.
- Produce a Capstone Episode Production Package suitable for human review and future reuse.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

The previous lessons taught individual systems: audience design, safety, series identity, characters, world, visuals, sound, story, dialogue, prompts, keyframes, speaking and non-speaking shots, continuity, editing, review, delivery, and retrospective.

The capstone combines those systems into one controlled production. The main challenge is not generating one attractive clip. It is preserving meaning and identity across an entire episode while keeping the experience understandable, emotionally safe, physically safe, educationally clear, and consistent.

A complete episode also reveals problems that isolated exercises cannot. A participation pause may disappear in editing. A target word may become unclear under music. A prop may drift across shots. A character may appear calm in one scene and suddenly overreact in another. The capstone therefore requires planning and review at every boundary.

The student should complete one short episode rather than attempting a large film. A two-to-four-minute episode is sufficient to demonstrate the full workflow.


## Recurring example series

The capstone continues the same preschool series:

# Lumi and Pip’s Little Garden

- **Lumi:** a curious four-year-old child with warm brown skin, a dark curly bob, coral overalls, a cream shirt, yellow boots, and a leaf-shaped hair clip.
- **Pip:** a small rounded teal-blue bird with a cream belly, orange beak, sky-blue scarf, large expressive eyes, gentle hops, and short calm flights.
- **Auntie Noura:** a patient adult guide with warm medium-brown skin, dark hair in a low bun, a sage-green gardening apron, a plum shirt, and calm reassuring movement.
- **World:** Sunseed Garden, including the Rainbow Garden, Cozy Workshop, and Little Pond Path.
- **Visual identity:** rounded 2D cut-paper design, soft texture, simple facial features, clear silhouettes, stable eye-level camera, warm light, and uncluttered backgrounds.
- **Audio identity:** warm voices, short sentences, restrained music, clear ambience, gentle Foley, and protected quiet space.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Capstone scope

Choose one primary learning goal, one supporting concept, one emotional skill, one main location, two or three characters, and a limited prop set. A smaller scope makes quality and continuity review possible.

## 2. Authoritative context

Begin from the latest approved Series Bible, Child-Safety Bible, Emotional-Safety Guide, Character Bibles, World Bible, Location Bible, Prop Bible, Wardrobe Bible, Visual Bible, Color Bible, Lighting Bible, Camera Guide, Voice Bibles, Music Bible, and Sound-Effects Bible.

## 3. Episode foundation

Create the Episode Learning Context and Episode Context Card before writing scenes. Define the learning goal, emotional goal, vocabulary, characters, location, props, problem, resolution, participation, recap, and continuity update.

## 4. Story and script

Use the approved preschool story structure: familiar beginning, discovery, small problem, safe attempts, helpful discovery, participation, application, emotional resolution, recap, reassurance, and calm ending.

## 5. Dialogue lock

Write exact final dialogue. Add pronunciation and emotional direction. Generate clean dialogue, review every word, approve it, and lock it before producing speaking shots.

## 6. Shot classification

Every shot must be classified as speaking, non-speaking controlled action, multi-action, complex performance, environment, or two-character staging.

## 7. Keyframe control

Create approved first and last frames. Last frames should normally be edited from approved first frames so that identity, camera, lighting, location, and props remain stable.

## 8. Segment chaining

Divide complex movement into A→B, B→C, and C→D units. Reuse stable boundary frames rather than asking one generation to perform the whole sequence.

## 9. Continuity ledger

Update state after every important boundary. Track character pose, expression, wardrobe, prop holder, location, weather, lighting, camera, knowledge, emotion, ambience, and unresolved state.

## 10. Picture and audio post-production

Edit for orientation, action readability, participation, reaction, and recap. Then assemble dialogue, ambience, Foley, effects, music, and the final mix without destroying quiet space.

## 11. Human review

Complete child-safety, educational, developmental, lip-sync, audio-picture, continuity, and final-mix reviews. The capstone is not approved only because the student likes it.

## 12. Local regeneration

When something fails, identify the smallest repairable unit. Preserve approved neighboring frames, audio, prompts, shots, and continuity.

## 13. Delivery and retrospective

Create captions, metadata, archive, final review package, retrospective, canon update, and next-episode handoff.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Suggested capstone episode

# The Yellow Watering Can

**Primary learning goal:** Identify yellow and match a yellow object with a yellow storage marker.

**Supporting concept:** Tools return to their safe storage place.

**Emotional skill:** Ask a trusted adult for help when an object is too high.

**Characters:** Lumi, Pip, Auntie Noura

**Location:** Cozy Workshop

**Props:** Yellow watering can, yellow shelf marker, blue storage box

**Problem:** The yellow watering can is on an adult-height shelf.

**Safe story solution:** Lumi stops, remains on the floor, and asks Auntie Noura for help. Auntie Noura lowers the watering can to the activity table. Lumi and Pip compare the yellow marker and return the can safely.

**Participation:** Choose which storage marker matches the yellow watering can.

**Recap:** “Yellow can. Yellow marker. They match.”

This episode practices color matching while reinforcing safe help-seeking. It avoids showing climbing as an attractive behavior.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Plan and execute one complete preschool animation episode as a capstone project.

**Permanent context**  
Use the latest approved project Bibles and references. Do not redesign characters, locations, props, voices, safety rules, or series identity.

**Episode scope**
- one primary learning goal;
- one limited supporting concept;
- one emotional skill;
- one main location;
- two or three characters;
- a small prop set;
- one mild visible problem;
- two or three attempts at most;
- one primary participation moment;
- one safe visible resolution;
- one concise recap.

Complete the project in this order:

1. Episode Learning Context.
2. Episode Context Card.
3. Complete Story Structure.
4. Final Dialogue and Narration.
5. Pronunciation and TTS direction.
6. Clean dialogue approval and lock.
7. Scene, Shot, and Segment Cards.
8. Shot classification.
9. Reference selection.
10. Approved first and last frames.
11. Speaking, non-speaking, multi-action, performance, environment, and two-character plans.
12. Continuity Ledger.
13. Shot generation and review.
14. Failure-specific local regeneration.
15. Picture edit.
16. Dialogue alignment.
17. Ambience, Foley, and effects.
18. Music editing.
19. Final mix.
20. Lip-sync, child-safety, educational, and continuity review.

**Child-audience requirements**
Maintain clear language, one visual focus, slow readable action, optional participation, physical safety, emotional safety, sensory comfort, accessibility, and caregiver trust.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Make a full preschool episode about colors. Generate all the scenes, add voices and music, and fix anything that looks wrong.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- The learning goal is too broad.
- Characters, location, problem, and resolution are undefined.
- No authoritative context is identified.
- Dialogue is not written or locked.
- Shot workflows are not classified.
- First and last frames are missing.
- Safety and educational review are postponed until after production.
- “Fix anything” does not define diagnosis or local regeneration.
- Continuity, captions, delivery, and archiving are absent.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Produce a two-to-four-minute episode titled *The Yellow Watering Can*.

Teach one goal: identify yellow and match a yellow object with a yellow storage marker. Support one emotional skill: ask a trusted adult for help when an object is too high.

Use Lumi, Pip, Auntie Noura, the Cozy Workshop, one yellow watering can, one yellow marker, and one blue nonmatching marker. Do not show climbing.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Choosing a capstone that is too long.
2. Adding several learning goals.
3. Generating scenes before locking dialogue.
4. Using temporary audio for speaking shots.
5. Skipping first and last frames.
6. Treating every shot as the same workflow.
7. Accepting attractive continuity drift.
8. Regenerating entire scenes for local failures.
9. Removing pauses during editing.
10. Skipping final human review or archive.

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Complete the Capstone Episode Production Package.

Recommended work order:

1. Select or use the suggested capstone episode.
2. Complete all episode-foundation documents.
3. Write and lock dialogue.
4. Build the entire scene, shot, and segment breakdown.
5. Create or specify all required references.
6. Prepare first and last frame plans.
7. Write production prompts for every shot.
8. Create a Continuity Ledger.
9. Perform shot-level review and local-repair planning.
10. Create picture, sound, music, and mix plans.
11. Complete final safety, educational, continuity, and delivery reviews.
12. Write the retrospective and next-episode handoff.

A student may use planned or simulated outputs when media generation is unavailable, but every decision and review must still be completed.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

What is the main capstone goal?

### Answer

To combine every course system into one controlled complete episode.
### Question 2

How many primary learning goals should the episode have?

### Answer

One.
### Question 3

When should speaking shots be generated?

### Answer

After exact clean dialogue is approved and locked.
### Question 4

What happens when one segment fails?

### Answer

Repair or regenerate the smallest failed unit.
### Question 5

What must happen before delivery?

### Answer

Picture, audio, safety, educational, continuity, caption, and final approval reviews.
### Question 6

Can an attractive but inconsistent shot be approved?

### Answer

No.
### Question 7

What should the capstone produce besides a final video?

### Answer

The complete planning, review, continuity, archive, retrospective, and handoff package.
