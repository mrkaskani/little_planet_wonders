# Module 10

## Lesson 19 of 72 — Prop Identity, Ownership, Safe Use, and Current State

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Design props as trackable story objects.
- Define ownership, holder, location, safety, and state.
- Prevent prop disappearance, duplication, and transformation.
- Create a Prop Bible and Prop Continuity Sheet.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Props are central to many preschool stories because learning is often concrete. Balls, baskets, crayons, watering cans, blocks, cards, and tools make concepts visible.

Props also create common continuity failures. A ball changes color, a basket changes size, an object jumps between hands, a tool appears without supervision, or a prop disappears between frames. A Prop Bible defines identity and safe use. A Prop Continuity Sheet tracks the current story state.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Prop identity

Define shape, color, material, texture, size, markings, and scale relative to characters.

## 2. Story function

A prop should have a clear purpose: learning object, recurring tool, emotional object, or location landmark.

## 3. Ownership and access

Record owner, shared status, storage location, and who may use it. Ownership should not justify coercive sharing.

## 4. Current holder

Track which character holds the prop, which hand or wing holds it, and when it changes holder.

## 5. Location state

Record where the prop begins, moves, and ends. A prop should not teleport unless the world explicitly allows it.

## 6. Physical state

Track clean, dirty, dry, wet, full, empty, open, closed, intact, broken, folded, or unfolded.

## 7. Safe use

Define age-appropriate handling, supervision, and forbidden uses.

## 8. Prop transformation

If a prop changes through the story, define the exact approved transformation. Do not allow random color, shape, or material changes.

## 9. Prop and keyframe continuity

First and last frames must show the intended holder, orientation, and state. Shared boundary images help preserve the prop.

## 10. Recurring sound identity

Important props may have a stable Foley identity: soft ball bounce, basket placement, watering-can pour.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Red ball identity

- Rounded soft fabric ball.
- Warm red surface.
- One small cream stitched circle.
- Fits comfortably in Lumi’s two hands.
- Stored in the red basket.
- Safe for gentle rolling and carrying.
- Not thrown indoors.
- Current state in the episode: clean, dry, found on the garden path.
- Current holder changes: path → Lumi → red basket.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Create or review a recurring prop using the approved Series, Character, Location, and Safety Bibles.

Define:
- identity shape, color, material, markings, and scale;
- story function;
- ownership and access;
- normal storage;
- safe use and supervision;
- forbidden use;
- recurring sound identity;
- permanent features;
- temporary states.

For the current shot, define:
- starting holder and hand;
- starting location and state;
- one primary action;
- ending holder, location, orientation, and state;
- elements that must remain unchanged.

**Output requirements**  
Produce a Prop Bible entry and a shot-level continuity record.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Lumi finds a red ball, throws it, catches it, changes it into a blue ball, gives it to Pip, and it disappears.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Several complex actions are combined.
- Color transformation is unexplained.
- Holder transitions are unclear.
- Throwing may conflict with safe indoor or garden behavior.
- The prop disappears without story logic.
- Starting and ending states are missing.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

The red ball begins on the garden path, clean and dry. Lumi approaches and picks it up with both hands. In the next segment, she holds it at chest height and shows it. In the final segment, she places it inside the red basket.

Preserve the ball’s warm red color, cream stitched circle, size, material, and orientation. Track holder and location at every segment boundary. Do not throw, transform, duplicate, or remove the ball.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Treating props as decorative.
2. Failing to define normal storage.
3. Changing color or material accidentally.
4. Allowing props to teleport.
5. Forgetting which hand holds the prop.
6. Combining several prop actions in one segment.
7. Ignoring sound identity.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create a Prop Bible for one recurring object.

1. Define identity and scale.
2. Define function, owner, and storage.
3. Define safe and forbidden use.
4. Define recurring sound.
5. List permanent and temporary states.
6. Create continuity records for three connected segments.
7. Check holder, hand, orientation, and state at every boundary.

---

# 14. Expected exercise result

A Prop Bible and a three-segment Prop Continuity Sheet.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

Why track current holder?

### Answer

To prevent impossible hand and prop changes.
### Question 2

What is normal storage?

### Answer

The approved location where the prop usually belongs.
### Question 3

What is prop state?

### Answer

Conditions such as clean, wet, open, full, or damaged.
### Question 4

Should a prop change color without explanation?

### Answer

No.
### Question 5

Why define Foley identity?

### Answer

Recurring material sounds improve audio continuity.
