**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes

# 1. Learning objective

- Understand the difference between voice identity, emotional reference, and exact final dialogue.
- Use the correct audio source for speaking-character video.
- Prevent timing, pronunciation, and lip-sync failures caused by temporary audio.
- Create an Emotional Reference Review Sheet.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

A common production mistake is using an emotional sample or temporary line to generate a speaking shot, then replacing the audio later. This often creates poor lip synchronization, missing words, unnatural pauses, and incorrect mouth movement.

Voice identity tells us who is speaking. Emotional reference tells us how the character generally delivers a type of emotion. Exact final dialogue tells us what is spoken and precisely how long the performance lasts.

The speaking-character video must be based on the exact approved final dialogue audio, not a reference clip, placeholder narration, or mixed soundtrack.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Voice identity

The permanent vocal characteristics: vocal age, pitch range, warmth, accent, pace tendencies, articulation, and maximum intensity.

## 2. Emotional reference

A reusable style example for neutral, happy, curious, concerned, reassuring, singing, or narration delivery.

## 3. Exact final dialogue

The approved final line with exact words, pronunciation, timing, pauses, breath, emotion, and duration.

## 4. Temporary dialogue

A placeholder line used during planning. It must not be used for final speaking-video generation.

## 5. Timing control

The final audio determines when syllables, pauses, breaths, and emotional changes occur.

## 6. Pronunciation control

The exact audio must be checked word by word, especially names and learning vocabulary.

## 7. Clean speaking input

Use dialogue only. Music, ambience, Foley, and effects belong on separate tracks and are added later.

## 8. Approval order

Write final dialogue → review meaning → add pronunciation → choose emotion and reference → generate exact audio → review every word → approve and lock → generate speaking video.

## 9. Change management

If the line changes after approval, regenerate the exact dialogue and speaking video. Do not keep old mouth movement.

## 10. Review responsibility

A human must compare script, clean audio, and final video. Tools cannot replace careful word-by-word and frame-by-frame review.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Incorrect workflow

1. Generate a speaking video using the line: “The ball goes here.”
2. Rewrite the script to: “The red ball belongs in the red basket.”
3. Replace the audio during editing.

Result:
- Mouth movement does not match.
- Video duration is too short.
- Pauses are missing.
- Emotional emphasis is wrong.

## Correct workflow

1. Final line: “The red ball belongs in the red basket.”
2. Review vocabulary and pronunciation.
3. Choose Lumi’s happy-gentle reference.
4. Generate clean exact dialogue.
5. Confirm every word.
6. Lock audio.
7. Generate speaking video from that exact audio.
8. Add ambience, Foley, and music later.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Prepare an exact-dialogue package for one speaking-character shot.

**Permanent context**  
Character Voice Bible, selected emotional reference, dialogue style, pronunciation guide, and child-audience requirements.

**Exact final line**  
[Line]

Define:
- selected emotional reference;
- pronunciation;
- pacing;
- pauses;
- breathing;
- emotional emphasis;
- maximum intensity;
- clean-audio requirements.

Then create a review checklist for:
- missing words;
- added words;
- repeated words;
- mispronunciation;
- incorrect timing;
- incorrect emotion;
- unstable character voice;
- excessive speed or volume.

**Important production rule**  
The exact approved dialogue audio must be locked before the final speaking-character video is generated.

**Output requirements**  
Produce a complete exact-dialogue review package.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Use Lumi’s happy voice sample to animate any line, then replace the audio with the final script during editing.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- The sample does not contain the final words.
- Mouth timing will not match.
- Final line duration may differ.
- Pauses and breaths may be wrong.
- Pronunciation cannot be verified.
- Replacing audio later does not repair lip movement.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Use Lumi’s happy-gentle reference only to guide identity and emotional delivery.

Generate the exact final line, “The red ball belongs in the red basket,” as clean dialogue with approved pronunciation, comfortable pace, one small pause after “ball,” and gentle emphasis on “red basket.”

Review every word, approve the performance, and lock the audio. Generate the speaking-character video from this exact audio. Add music, ambience, Foley, and effects later on separate tracks.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Using placeholder audio for final video.
2. Treating reference audio as final dialogue.
3. Mixing music into lip-sync input.
4. Changing script after video generation.
5. Reviewing only general emotion.
6. Failing to check every word.
7. Keeping a speaking clip after dialogue changes.
