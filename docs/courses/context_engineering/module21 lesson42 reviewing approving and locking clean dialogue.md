# Module 21

## Lesson 42 of 72 — Reviewing, Approving, and Locking Clean Dialogue

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Review exact generated dialogue word by word.
- Detect missing, added, repeated, mispronounced, rushed, or emotionally incorrect speech.
- Separate clean dialogue from music, ambience, Foley, and effects.
- Create a clear approval and locking process.
- Produce a Locked Dialogue Package and Dialogue Review Sheet.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

A speaking-character shot inherits every strength and weakness of its dialogue audio. If one word is missing, the video cannot pronounce it correctly. If the line contains music or effects, the lip-sync process may respond to sounds that are not speech. If the emotion is wrong, the final facial performance may also be wrong.

Approval must therefore happen before speaking-video generation. The reviewer compares the script, direction sheet, candidate audio, and voice identity. The approved file becomes the dialogue master. It should not be changed casually during editing.

For children under five, the reviewer must also check comfort: no sudden loudness, rushed vocabulary, excessive breath, unstable pitch, or frightening delivery.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Word-by-word review

Follow the script while listening. Confirm every word is present, in order, and spoken once unless repetition is written.

## 2. Missing word

A missing article, adjective, or learning word can change meaning. Do not assume the child will infer it.

## 3. Added word

Voice generation may add filler, interjections, or repeated sounds. Unwritten additions must be rejected unless the script is formally changed and reapproved.

## 4. Repeated word

Unplanned repetition can confuse learning and affect lip timing.

## 5. Pronunciation accuracy

Compare difficult words with the approved guide, not personal memory alone.

## 6. Voice identity review

Check vocal age, pitch range, warmth, accent, articulation, and energy against the Voice Bible.

## 7. Emotional review

The emotion should support the line and remain within maximum intensity.

## 8. Pace and pause review

Confirm the line gives children enough time to understand. Check that pauses are natural and do not split meaning.

## 9. Clean dialogue

The file contains one voice only. No music, ambience, Foley, sound effects, applause, room conversation, or other character voices.

## 10. Audio comfort

Check sudden volume changes, harsh consonants, excessive high frequency, distortion, clipping, and uncomfortable breaths.

## 11. Candidate comparison

Generate or record several candidates when necessary. Compare them against the same criteria rather than accepting the first result.

## 12. Approval and lock

Once approved, record version, line, character, emotion, duration, and approval status. The locked file becomes the only speaking-video source.

## 13. Change control

Any wording or pronunciation change creates a new version. Regenerate audio and the speaking shot rather than replacing audio under old mouth movement.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Review a generated preschool dialogue candidate against the approved script and direction sheet.

Check:
- every word;
- order;
- missing words;
- added words;
- repeated words;
- pronunciation;
- vocal identity;
- emotion;
- pace;
- pauses;
- emphasis;
- breath;
- volume;
- clipping or distortion;
- unwanted background audio.

**Decision rules**
- Reject if wording differs.
- Reject if target vocabulary is unclear.
- Reject if identity or emotion is wrong.
- Reject if music, effects, ambience, or another voice is present.
- Approve only when all required criteria pass.
- Lock the approved version before speaking-video generation.

**Output requirements**  
Produce a completed Dialogue Review Sheet and Locked Dialogue Package record.

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> The audio sounds good enough. Use it for lip sync and fix any problems later in editing.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- General impression replaces word-by-word review.
- Missing or repeated words may be overlooked.
- Editing cannot repair incorrect mouth movement.
- Mixed audio may enter the speaking workflow.
- Voice identity and pronunciation are not checked.
- No approval or version lock exists.
- Later changes may break duration and timing.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Compare the candidate with the exact script word by word. Confirm every word is present once, in the correct order, with approved pronunciation.

Check Lumi’s vocal age, pitch, warmth, pace, emotion, pauses, volume, and maximum intensity. Confirm the file contains one clean voice only and no music, ambience, Foley, effects, distortion, or other speakers.

Reject any failure. Approve and lock only the strongest passing candidate. Use that locked file as the speaking-video source. If the line later changes, create a new audio version and regenerate the speaking shot.

---

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Accepting the first candidate.
2. Reviewing emotion but not words.
3. Ignoring small added words.
4. Leaving music in the file.
5. Assuming editing can fix timing.
6. Failing to record duration.
7. Overwriting the approved master.
8. Changing the script after locking without versioning.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create a Dialogue Review Sheet for one line.

1. Write the exact script.
2. List every word in the review table.
3. Review three imagined or real candidates.
4. Record word, pronunciation, voice, emotion, pace, and cleanliness findings.
5. Reject at least one candidate for a specific reason.
6. Approve one candidate.
7. Create the Locked Dialogue Package record.
8. Write the change-control rule.
