# Module 10

## Lesson 20 of 72 — Wardrobe Identity and Temporary Clothing Changes

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Define clothing as part of character identity.
- Create approved wardrobe variations without losing recognition.
- Track clothing state across scenes.
- Complete a Wardrobe Bible.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Clothing supports recognition, world logic, weather, activity, culture, and continuity. It can also create many generation failures: colors change, sleeves disappear, patterns move, footwear changes, accessories multiply, or a wet outfit becomes dry in the next shot.

A Wardrobe Bible defines core clothing and approved variations. It also tracks temporary state such as wet, dirty, clean, damaged, rolled sleeves, open coat, or removed gloves.

Wardrobe choices should remain comfortable, respectful, safe, and appropriate to the character’s activity.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Core wardrobe

The default outfit that supports recognition. Lumi’s coral overalls, cream shirt, and yellow boots form her core identity.

## 2. Wardrobe variation

A planned change for weather, activity, celebration, sleep, or cultural event. The variation should preserve key identity cues.

## 3. Color continuity

Approved palette relationships remain stable. A raincoat may use yellow and coral accents rather than introducing unrelated colors.

## 4. Silhouette continuity

A variation should not completely change the character’s recognizable body shape unless carefully approved.

## 5. Activity suitability

Clothing must support safe movement and context. Avoid long loose items near tools, water, or active movement.

## 6. Temporary clothing state

Track wetness, dirt, paint marks, damage, open or closed fasteners, rolled sleeves, missing items, and current accessories.

## 7. Change procedure

A wardrobe change should happen through a clear scene transition or visible action. Clothing should not change between connected shots without explanation.

## 8. Cultural respect

Culturally specific clothing requires accurate, respectful design and appropriate human review. Do not use cultural dress as costume decoration without context.

## 9. Wardrobe and speaking video

Avoid collars, scarves, or accessories that obscure the mouth, neck, or face during lip-synced performance.

## 10. Wardrobe continuity package

Create front, side, back, three-quarter, sitting, and action references for each approved outfit.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Lumi rain variation

**Preserved identity:**
- Dark curly bob
- Leaf hair clip
- Coral color accents
- Yellow boots
- Childlike proportions

**Approved changes:**
- Soft yellow raincoat with coral trim
- Cream shirt remains underneath
- Overalls partially visible
- Small rounded hood resting behind the head
- No dangling cords

**Temporary state:**
- Raincoat wet on shoulders
- Boots slightly wet
- Hair remains mostly dry under supervision

The outfit communicates weather without redesigning Lumi.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Design or review an approved wardrobe variation for [CHARACTER].

**Permanent context**  
Character Identity Sheet, visual style, color palette, safety rules, cultural-respect rules, and current location.

**Reason for variation**  
[Weather, activity, celebration, bedtime]

Preserve:
- face, hair, body proportions, and identity colors;
- recognizable silhouette;
- age-appropriate comfort;
- safe movement;
- cultural accuracy where relevant.

Define:
- garment pieces;
- colors and materials;
- footwear;
- approved accessories;
- safety limitations;
- current clean, wet, dirty, open, closed, or damaged state;
- elements that may change;
- elements that must remain unchanged.

**Output requirements**  
Create consistent reference views and a Wardrobe Bible entry.

**Review criteria**  
The character must remain immediately recognizable.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Give Lumi a completely new fashionable outfit, different hair, jewelry, long scarf, high shoes, and many patterns for the rainy scene.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- The outfit redesigns the character instead of adapting her.
- Hair change breaks identity.
- Jewelry and patterns add clutter.
- A long scarf may create safety and motion problems.
- High shoes are unsuitable for garden movement.
- The approved palette and silhouette are ignored.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Create Lumi’s approved rain outfit. Preserve her dark curly bob, leaf hair clip, coral identity color, childlike proportions, and yellow boots.

Add a soft yellow raincoat with coral trim, rounded shapes, simple fasteners, and no dangling cords. Keep patterns minimal. Use safe flat boots and a hood that does not obscure the face. Track wetness on the coat and boots across connected scenes.

Do not add jewelry, long scarves, high shoes, new hairstyle, complex patterns, or unrelated colors.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Redesigning the character for every episode.
2. Using unapproved colors.
3. Forgetting footwear.
4. Changing clothing between connected shots.
5. Adding accessories that obscure the face.
6. Ignoring wet, dirty, or damaged state.
7. Using cultural clothing without context or review.

7. Plan six reference views for each approved outfit.
8. Review a three-shot sequence for clothing state continuity.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

Why is clothing part of identity?

### Answer

It supports recognition, silhouette, color, and continuity.
### Question 2

Can a weather outfit change?

### Answer

Yes, but it should preserve key identity cues.
### Question 3

Why track wetness?

### Answer

It must remain consistent across connected scenes.
### Question 4

Why avoid face-obscuring accessories?

### Answer

They reduce emotional readability and lip-sync quality.
### Question 5

What requires cultural review?

### Answer

Culturally specific clothing and context.
