# Module 27

## Lesson 56 of 72 — Continuity Ledger and Approved Changed State

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Track continuity across segments, shots, scenes, and episodes.
- Record character, prop, wardrobe, location, weather, light, knowledge, emotion, and audio state.
- Distinguish temporary state from approved changed state.
- Prevent accidental generation differences from becoming canon.
- Create a Continuity Ledger.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Continuity is the memory of the production. Without a ledger, a ball may move between hands, a coat may become dry, a gate may open, a character may forget a lesson, or a location may change layout without explanation.

The ledger records what is true at each boundary and what must continue. It also records deliberate approved changes. For example, Pip learning to compare colors may become approved character knowledge. A random change to Pip’s scarf color should not.

Continuity is not only visual. Audio ambience, music state, dialogue status, emotional tone, and knowledge also continue across cuts.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Continuity ledger

A structured record of current state at every production boundary.

## 2. Character state

Pose, orientation, expression, gaze, mouth, hands, wardrobe, dirt, wetness, and knowledge.

## 3. Prop state

Holder, hand, location, orientation, material, color, open or closed, full or empty, wet or dry.

## 4. Location state

Time, weather, light, open or closed gates, moved furniture, decorations, wet surfaces, and safety boundaries.

## 5. Camera continuity

Shot size, screen direction, eyeline, and spatial geography.

## 6. Audio continuity

Ambience perspective, dialogue completion, music state, Foley action, and silence.

## 7. Emotional continuity

Characters should not jump between emotional states without a visible cause.

## 8. Knowledge continuity

Characters remember approved learning unless the story intentionally revisits it at a different level.

## 9. Temporary state

A current condition that may disappear after the scene: wet boots, held ball, concerned expression.

## 10. Approved changed state

A deliberate change that continues forward: learned vocabulary, repaired object, relationship growth, new recurring location feature.

## 11. Unresolved state

An item the story has not completed yet. It must be carried forward until resolved.

## 12. Continuity authority

The approved ledger overrides accidental generated variation.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Create or update a Continuity Ledger for the current production boundary.

Track:
- every character’s visual, emotional, and knowledge state;
- every important prop’s holder, hand, location, orientation, and condition;
- location, weather, lighting, gates, surfaces, and safety boundaries;
- camera geography, screen direction, and eyelines;
- dialogue, ambience, Foley, effects, music, and silence;
- temporary state;
- unresolved state;
- approved changed state;
- requirements for the next boundary.

**Important rule**  
Do not store accidental generated variation as approved context.

**Output requirements**  
Produce a complete ledger entry and identify any continuity conflicts.

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> Keep the next shot similar to the previous one.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- “Similar” does not identify required matches.
- Character hands and prop holder are not tracked.
- Wardrobe, weather, light, and background state are undefined.
- Knowledge and emotion are omitted.
- Audio continuity is omitted.
- Temporary and approved changed states are not separated.
- Accidental drift could be accepted.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Use the approved Continuity Ledger entry.

The next shot begins with Lumi holding the red ball in both hands at waist height, cream stitched circle facing the camera. Her expression remains curious. Core wardrobe is dry and unchanged.

The Rainbow Garden remains in warm morning light. Yellow gate closed. Red basket left, blue basket right. Garden ambience continues.

Do not change prop holder, hand occupancy, ball orientation, wardrobe, light, gate, basket positions, or emotional state until the shot’s planned action begins.

---

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Tracking only clothing.
2. Forgetting hands and prop orientation.
3. Treating emotion as independent per shot.
4. Failing to track ambience and music.
5. Updating permanent context from drift.
6. Leaving unresolved objects behind.
7. Using vague continuity language.
8. Not recording approval.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create ledger entries for five boundaries in the red-ball episode:

1. Before discovery.
2. After pickup.
3. During participation.
4. After placement.
5. Episode ending.

Track all visual, emotional, knowledge, prop, location, camera, and audio state. Mark approved changed state at the end.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

What is a Continuity Ledger?

### Answer

A structured record of current state across production boundaries.
### Question 2

What is approved changed state?

### Answer

A deliberate change authorized to continue.
### Question 3

Should accidental scarf-color drift be stored?

### Answer

No.
### Question 4

What nonvisual states need tracking?

### Answer

Emotion, knowledge, dialogue, ambience, music, and silence.
### Question 5

What is unresolved state?

### Answer

A story or prop condition not yet completed.
