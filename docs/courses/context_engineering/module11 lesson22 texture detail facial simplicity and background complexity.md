---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

A scene can become difficult for a young child to follow even when the composition is technically correct. Fine patterns, many props, moving decorations, complex textures, and detailed shadows may compete with the learning object.

Texture can make a visual world feel warm and tactile, but it must not obscure shape. Facial detail can make a character expressive, but too much detail can become unstable or uncanny. Background complexity should change according to story function. A location may contain detail in an establishing shot, but a participation or learning shot should reduce competing information.

The Visual Style Bible turns the medium selected in Lesson 21 into practical density rules.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Visual hierarchy

Visual hierarchy determines what the viewer notices first, second, and third. Important characters, objects, and actions should receive stronger contrast or placement than decoration.

## 2. Texture scale

Texture should be large and subtle enough to survive small-screen viewing. Very fine grain may flicker or look like noise during motion.

## 3. Material consistency

Paper should behave like paper, clay like clay, and fabric like fabric. Avoid a prop switching from matte paper to glossy plastic between shots.

## 4. Detail zones

Allow more detail in calm establishing views and less detail in learning, participation, emotional, or speaking shots.

## 5. Facial simplicity

Use a limited stable set of features. Eyes, eyebrows, and mouth should communicate clearly without wrinkles, pores, complex teeth, or highly realistic skin.

## 6. Mouth clarity

The mouth must remain visible and flexible for speech. Avoid patterns, scarves, props, or shadows that obscure the lower face.

## 7. Background complexity budget

Set a maximum number of active background elements. A background may contain many static shapes but only one or two subtle movements during an important action.

## 8. Prop density

Only include props that support location identity, story action, or learning. Decorative clutter increases continuity failures.

## 9. Small-screen reduction

Check whether key objects merge into the background when reduced. Increase separation through shape, value, spacing, or controlled color.

## 10. Motion texture review

Textures that appear stable in a still image may flicker during video. Review the actual moving clip for crawling patterns, shimmer, and unstable edges.

- character facial simplicity;
- prop density;
- background detail level;
- maximum moving background elements;

Reduce detail according to the shot’s educational and emotional purpose.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Add lots of beautiful detail, realistic textures, patterned clothing, moving flowers, sparkling particles, and complex shadows everywhere.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- No visual hierarchy exists.
- Fine detail may disappear or flicker.
- Patterns may reduce character readability.
- Moving decoration competes with learning and participation.
- Realistic textures conflict with the selected cut-paper medium.
- Complex shadows may obscure faces and important objects.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Use subtle large-scale paper texture on characters and scenery. Keep Lumi’s clothing mostly solid and preserve simple facial features.

For the participation shot, show only the red ball, red basket, blue basket, Lumi, and Pip as active visual elements. Keep flower beds simplified and static. Use soft shallow shadows and no particles, glitter, patterned clothing, moving decoration, or high-frequency texture.

The red ball and two baskets must remain readable at thumbnail size.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Using the same detail level for every shot.
2. Adding texture because flat areas feel empty.
3. Allowing fine texture to flicker.
4. Overdecorating clothing.
5. Obscuring the face with shadow or props.
6. Treating all background elements as equally important.
7. Reviewing still images without checking video.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

What is visual hierarchy?

### Answer

The planned order in which the viewer notices elements.
### Question 2

Why use larger subtle texture?

### Answer

It remains stable and does not compete on small screens.
### Question 3

Should all shots use equal detail?

### Answer

No. Learning and participation shots need less competition.
### Question 4

Why keep mouths unobstructed?

### Answer

For emotion and speaking-video clarity.
### Question 5

Why review moving texture?

### Answer

Still textures may flicker or crawl in video.
