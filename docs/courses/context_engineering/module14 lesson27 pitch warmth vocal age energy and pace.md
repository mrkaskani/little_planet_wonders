## Lesson 27 of 72 — Pitch, Warmth, Vocal Age, Energy, and Pace

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Define a distinct and comfortable voice identity for each character.
- Separate pitch, vocal age, warmth, energy, and pace.
- Prevent voices from becoming shrill, rushed, or overly theatrical.
- Create a Voice Identity Profile.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

A character’s voice may be more recognizable than the character’s clothing. Voice identity must remain stable across lines, emotions, songs, and episodes.

Children under five need clear pronunciation, comfortable pacing, and emotional readability. A “young” voice should not automatically become extremely high-pitched. A “fun” voice should not become loud, frantic, or exhausting. Each voice needs controlled dimensions that can be reviewed separately.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Pitch

Pitch is how high or low the voice sounds. Define a comfortable range rather than a single exact note. Avoid extreme high pitch for child characters.

## 2. Vocal age

Vocal age is the perceived age quality of the voice. It comes from pitch, resonance, articulation, rhythm, and performance—not pitch alone.

## 3. Warmth

Warmth describes emotional and tonal comfort. A warm voice may use gentle resonance, smooth transitions, and relaxed delivery.

## 4. Energy

Energy is the amount of liveliness in the performance. It can be low, moderate, or high without changing volume dramatically.

## 5. Pace

Pace includes speaking speed, pause spacing, and thought timing. Preschool dialogue normally needs moderate-to-slow delivery with clear word boundaries.

## 6. Articulation

Words should remain understandable without becoming robotic. Consonants are clear, vowels stable, and final words not swallowed.

## 7. Breath and phrasing

Short lines should sound natural. Plan breathing and pauses rather than compressing the entire line into one rush.

## 8. Volume comfort

A character may sound excited without shouting. Establish a maximum loudness and avoid sudden jumps.

## 9. Distinct voices

Main characters should differ through several dimensions, not stereotypes. Lumi is warm and steady; Pip is lighter and slightly quicker; Auntie Noura is calm and lower.

## 10. Voice continuity

Review pitch, pace, warmth, accent, pronunciation, and energy across all approved samples.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Create a Voice Identity Profile for [CHARACTER].

Use the approved Character Bible, age presentation, personality, emotional range, and child-audience requirements.

Define:
- perceived vocal age;
- comfortable pitch range;
- warmth and resonance;
- default and maximum energy;
- speaking pace;
- articulation;
- breath and phrasing;
- default and maximum volume;
- qualities that distinguish the voice;
- forbidden delivery styles.

**Child-audience requirements**  
Clear, calm, comfortable, nonthreatening, and easy to understand. Avoid shrillness, shouting, rushed delivery, caricature, and unstable vocal age.

**Output requirements**  
Produce practical direction for neutral dialogue and later emotional references.

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> Give Pip a super high, hyper, squeaky, very fast voice because he is funny.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Pitch, speed, and energy are all pushed to extremes.
- The voice may become tiring or difficult to understand.
- Humor is being reduced to vocal discomfort.
- The character may sound much younger or less capable than intended.
- No warmth, articulation, or maximum intensity is defined.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Give Pip a light, friendly voice with a comfortable moderately high pitch, gentle playful energy, and a pace slightly quicker than Lumi’s but still clearly understandable.

Use short phrases, clear consonants, and small curious pauses. Keep volume comfortable and avoid squeaking, shouting, breathless delivery, extreme pitch jumps, or frantic rhythm.

Pip’s humor should come from observation and timing, not vocal strain.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Using pitch alone to create age.
2. Making child voices squeaky.
3. Making energy equal loudness.
4. Using the same pace for every emotion.
5. Choosing novelty over clarity.
6. Failing to define maximum intensity.
7. Allowing the voice to change across episodes.

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create Voice Identity Profiles for two characters.

For each:
1. Define vocal age.
2. Define pitch range.
3. Define warmth and resonance.
4. Define default and maximum energy.
5. Define pace and articulation.
6. Define volume limits.
7. List three distinctive and three forbidden qualities.
8. Compare the voices for clear separation.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

Is vocal age the same as pitch?

### Answer

No. It also includes resonance, articulation, rhythm, and performance.
### Question 2

Can excitement be shown without shouting?

### Answer

Yes.
### Question 3

Why define maximum energy?

### Answer

To prevent emotional scenes from becoming overwhelming.
### Question 4

What supports preschool clarity?

### Answer

Moderate pace, clear articulation, and natural pauses.
### Question 5

How should voices be distinguished?

### Answer

Through several controlled dimensions, not caricature.
