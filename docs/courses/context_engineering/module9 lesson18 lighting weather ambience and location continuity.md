**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes

# 1. Learning objective

- Create controlled lighting and weather variations.
- Define ambience identity for recurring locations.
- Track temporary location state across scenes and episodes.
- Complete a Location Bible and Reference Plan.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

A location can remain geometrically consistent and still feel like a different place when lighting, weather, sound, or temporary state changes unpredictably.

For children under five, lighting should preserve facial readability, safe boundaries, and important objects. Weather should be understandable and should not create unnecessary danger or sensory intensity. Ambience should support location identity without masking dialogue.

Continuity requires tracking whether the ground is wet, a gate is open, flowers are blooming, props have moved, or decorations were added. A temporary change should not silently become permanent.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Lighting identity

Define the normal light direction, softness, warmth, and contrast for each location.

## 2. Time-of-day variations

Morning, afternoon, evening, and night should preserve layout and identity. Night scenes remain readable and reassuring.

## 3. Weather as story context

Weather may support learning or mood, but should not automatically create danger. Light rain can support clothing and puddle lessons without thunder or darkness.

## 4. Lighting continuity

Within one scene, direction, shadow softness, exposure, and color temperature should remain stable unless the story shows a clear change.

## 5. Ambience identity

A recurring location should have a recognizable sound bed. Use subtle natural and room sounds rather than constant decorative effects.

## 6. Audio perspective

Ambience changes with camera distance and location. A close indoor shot should not sound like a wide outdoor garden.

## 7. Temporary state

Track open or closed gates, wet or dry ground, moved furniture, decorations, broken or repaired objects, and current weather.

## 8. Approved changed state

When a story permanently changes the location, record it deliberately. Do not treat every generated variation as canon.

## 9. Transition continuity

When moving between scenes, use consistent weather, time, clothing, and ambience unless a clear transition explains the change.

## 10. Comfort review

Check dark shadows, lightning, wind intensity, thunder, sudden ambience changes, and visually unstable light.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Create a lighting, weather, ambience, and continuity plan for [LOCATION].

Use the approved layout and landmarks.

Define:
- normal lighting;
- safe time-of-day variations;
- allowed weather and maximum intensity;
- how weather changes clothing and surfaces;
- base ambience and perspective variations;
- temporary-state fields;
- conditions for approving a permanent location change.

**Elements allowed to change**  
Time, weather, temporary props, wetness, and approved decorations.

**Elements that must remain unchanged**  
Layout, landmarks, scale, safety barriers, location identity, and child-readable visibility.

**Review criteria**  
Faces, learning objects, paths, and boundaries remain readable. Dialogue remains clear. No flicker, sudden darkness, threatening weather, or untracked state change.

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> Make the garden rainy and dramatic with dark clouds, lightning, loud thunder, strong wind, and flashing wet reflections.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Weather intensity is unnecessary.
- Lightning and thunder may frighten young children.
- Flashing reflections may create sensory risk.
- Strong wind complicates motion and continuity.
- Faces, objects, and safety boundaries may become unclear.
- The prompt does not track wet state or clothing.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Show the Rainbow Garden during gentle light rain. Use soft overcast daylight with clear faces, visible paths, and stable landmarks. Add small raindrops, slight wetness on the path, and calm leaf movement.

Characters wear approved rain clothing. Use gentle rain ambience beneath dialogue. Do not include lightning, thunder, strong wind, flashing reflections, dark threatening clouds, camera shake, or rapid lighting changes. Track the wet path and clothing into the next connected scene.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Using weather only to increase drama.
2. Changing light direction between shots.
3. Forgetting wet clothing or surfaces.
4. Adding loud ambience under dialogue.
5. Letting temporary decorations become permanent.
6. Changing time of day without transition.
7. Reviewing visuals without reviewing sound.

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

4. Create base, close, and wide ambience.

6. Define approved permanent change rules.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

Why track wet state?

### Answer

It must remain consistent across connected scenes.
### Question 2

Can night scenes be dark and unreadable?

### Answer

No. They should remain clear and reassuring.
### Question 3

What is ambience identity?

### Answer

The recurring restrained sound environment of a location.
### Question 4

What is approved changed state?

### Answer

A deliberate permanent update to the location context.
### Question 5

Why review audio perspective?

### Answer

Sound should match camera distance and place.
