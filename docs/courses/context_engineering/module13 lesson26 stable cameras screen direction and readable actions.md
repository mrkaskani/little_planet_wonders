# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Camera movement can help the child follow action, but too much movement competes with the action itself. A pan, zoom, orbit, shake, and character movement combined in one short segment may become difficult to understand and difficult to generate consistently.

Screen direction means the visual direction in which characters and objects move. If Lumi walks left-to-right in one shot and suddenly continues right-to-left without a clear change of view, the child may think she turned around. Stable direction supports continuity.

Readable action also requires enough time to see the starting state, movement, result, and reaction.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Locked camera

The camera remains still. It is often best for demonstrations, participation, dialogue, and simple prop actions.

## 2. Gentle pan

The camera turns horizontally to reveal or follow one subject. Use one slow pan with a clear beginning and end.

## 3. Slow push-in

The camera gently moves closer to emphasize emotion or a learning object. Avoid rapid zoom-like movement.

## 4. Slow pull-back

The camera gradually reveals context or a completed result.

## 5. Simple tracking

The camera moves alongside a character at a stable pace. It should not combine with orbiting or abrupt direction changes.

## 6. Screen direction

Track whether a character faces or moves left or right. Preserve the direction across connected shots unless a visible turn or angle change explains it.

## 7. Eyeline continuity

When characters look at one another or an object, their gaze direction should match the geography.

## 8. Complete action

Show the starting state, action, ending state, and result. Do not cut before the object is clearly placed or the gesture is complete.

## 9. Reaction time

Allow a brief moment after the action so children can understand the result and emotion.

## 10. One camera instruction per segment

Use locked, pan, push, pull, or track—not several at once. Simple camera direction improves comfort and generation control.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Lumi places the ball in the basket

**Starting state:** Lumi stands left of the red basket, holding the ball with both hands.

**Camera:** Locked eye-level medium-wide.

**Action:** Lumi takes one step, bends slightly, and places the ball inside.

**Ending state:** Lumi releases the ball and straightens.

**Reaction:** Pip looks into the basket. Lumi says, “It matches.”

The camera does not pan, zoom, orbit, or shake. The complete action remains readable.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Orbit around Lumi while zooming in, panning toward Pip, shaking for excitement, and tracking the rolling ball.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Several camera systems compete.
- The child may lose spatial orientation.
- Motion intensity is excessive.
- Generation may distort characters and background.
- Screen direction becomes unclear.
- The ball action may be harder to understand.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Use a locked eye-level medium-wide camera while Lumi notices the rolling ball. In the next segment, use one slow pan to follow the ball from left to right until it stops at the gate.

Keep Lumi and Auntie Noura visible enough to preserve safety context. Do not orbit, zoom, shake, rotate, or change direction. Hold briefly after the ball stops so the result is clear.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Combining several camera movements.
2. Changing screen direction without explanation.
3. Cutting before action completion.
4. Moving the camera during participation.
5. Using shake for mild problems.
6. Tracking too closely and losing context.
7. Forgetting reaction time.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Complete a Camera Language Guide and plan three segments.

For each:
1. Define starting state.
2. Choose one camera behavior.
3. Define screen direction and eyeline.
4. Define the action.
5. Define ending state.
6. Add reaction time.
7. Review geography and comfort.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

What is the safest default camera?

### Answer

A locked eye-level camera.
### Question 2

What is screen direction?

### Answer

The visual direction a character or object faces or moves.
### Question 3

Why show reaction time?

### Answer

Children need time to understand the result.
### Question 4

How many camera behaviors per segment?

### Answer

Usually one.
### Question 5

When should direction change?

### Answer

Only when a visible turn or clear angle change explains it.
