## Lesson 61 of 72 — Dialogue Editing, Cleanup, and Speaking-Shot Alignment

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Edit approved dialogue without changing its words, identity, or intended performance.
- Align clean dialogue with speaking shots while preserving natural pauses and breaths.
- Recognize when audio timing problems require regeneration rather than stretching or cutting.
- Maintain consistent voice level, tone, and spacing across scenes.
- Create a Dialogue Edit and Alignment Sheet.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

After picture editing, approved dialogue must be placed precisely against the speaking shots. This is not the moment to rewrite the line, replace words, remove meaningful pauses, or force an audio file to fit a clip that was generated from different timing.

For children under five, dialogue must remain clear, comfortably paced, and emotionally readable. A pause before a learning word may help comprehension. A response pause after a question must remain long enough for participation. Excessive trimming can make speech feel rushed, while aggressive stretching can distort the character’s voice and pronunciation.

The editor should treat locked dialogue as the master. The speaking shot should already have been generated from that exact file. Editing normally involves placement, clean trimming of unused silence, gentle transitions, and level continuity—not changing the performance.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Locked dialogue authority

The approved clean dialogue file is the authoritative source for words, pronunciation, pace, emotional delivery, and duration. Editing should preserve it.

## 2. Head and tail cleanup

Remove only unnecessary silence, clicks, or unusable noise before and after the line. Preserve intentional breath and listening space.

## 3. Dialogue-to-picture alignment

Align speech onset with visible mouth movement and performance preparation. The mouth should not begin moving before speech or continue after the audio ends.

## 4. Pause preservation

Differentiate between accidental empty space and intentional pause. Participation, thought, reassurance, and emphasis pauses must be protected.

## 5. Breath preservation

Natural breath may support phrasing. Remove distracting, harsh, or accidental breaths only when doing so does not make the line unnatural.

## 6. No word surgery

Do not remove, rearrange, or combine words from different takes after approval unless the full line is re-reviewed and re-locked.

## 7. Timing mismatch diagnosis

If the shot is shorter than the exact dialogue, the correct repair is usually a new shot or approved visual extension. If the audio is wrong, regenerate audio and then the speaking shot.

## 8. Voice-level continuity

Adjacent lines from the same character should feel like the same voice in the same environment. Check loudness, tonal balance, and emotional continuity.

## 9. Cross-shot dialogue continuity

When a character speaks across cuts, maintain consistent performance, room perspective, and phrase flow.

## 10. Listener space

Leave enough time after important lines for the other character or child audience to react.

## 11. Dialogue separation

Keep dialogue on its own track or clearly separated layer so ambience, Foley, music, and effects can be adjusted independently.

## 12. Approval after edit

The final edited dialogue sequence requires a new review against the script, locked masters, picture, participation timing, and child-comfort rules.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Speaking shot

Lumi asks:

> “Which basket matches the red ball?”

## Correct dialogue edit

- Preserve the exact approved words.
- Start the audio when Lumi begins natural mouth movement.
- Keep the small pause after “basket.”
- Preserve the full response pause after the question.
- End mouth movement with the final word.
- Let Lumi close her mouth and hold a listening pose.
- Keep the clean dialogue separate from music and ambience.

## Incorrect dialogue edit

- Remove the pause to make the scene faster.
- Stretch the last word to fill the shot.
- Add music directly into the dialogue file.
- Cut to Pip before Lumi’s mouth finishes.
- Replace “matches” with another take without reapproval.

The correct version protects clarity, identity, timing, and participation.

- speech and mouth onset;

- pronunciation;
- voice identity;
- level continuity;
- reaction and listening space;

**Important rules**
- Do not alter words.
- Do not time-stretch the voice unnaturally.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Make the audio fit the shot by cutting pauses, speeding it up, and stretching any words that are too short.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Intentional pauses may be destroyed.
- Speed changes may reduce comprehension.
- Time-stretching may distort the voice.
- The picture may have been generated from different timing.
- Word clarity and pronunciation may be damaged.
- Participation space may disappear.
- The correct source of the mismatch is not diagnosed.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Use the locked dialogue at its approved timing. Align the speech onset and ending with the generated mouth performance.

Preserve the small pause after “basket” and the full participation pause after the question. Remove only accidental silence or noise outside the approved line.

If the shot is too short, extend or regenerate the picture. If the audio wording or performance is incorrect, regenerate and relock the audio, then regenerate the speaking shot. Do not speed up, stretch, or rewrite the approved line.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Cutting pauses for speed.
2. Stretching speech to fill picture.
3. Combining words from unapproved takes.
4. Moving audio without checking mouth onset.
5. Leaving the mouth moving after speech.
6. Mixing music into dialogue too early.
7. Failing to review level continuity.
8. Fixing a picture problem by damaging audio.

The locked dialogue master.

### Answer

No.

### Answer

Extend or regenerate the picture.

### Answer

No.

So other audio layers can be controlled independently.
