# 1. Learning objective

- Write motion prompts that describe change between approved boundaries.
- Direct one action, one camera behavior, speed, path, and completion.
- State allowed changes and protected elements.
- Use negative constraints based on shot-specific risks.
- Create a Motion Prompt Template.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

A motion prompt should not redescribe the entire world from the beginning. The first and last frames already contain much of the visual context. The prompt’s main job is to describe how the approved starting state becomes the approved ending state.

A strong motion prompt names one action, movement path, speed, body mechanics, prop mechanics, camera behavior, emotional intensity, and completion. It also states what must not change.

For children under five, movement should remain readable and comfortable. Rapid acceleration, abrupt direction changes, camera shake, and excessive secondary motion can reduce comprehension.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Boundary-led prompting

Use the approved first and last frames as authoritative start and end states.

## 2. Motion verb

Choose one clear verb: turn, reach, pick up, place, walk, open, close, pour, point, or look.

## 3. Movement path

Describe where the body part or prop moves: down toward the ball, upward to waist height, left to right along the path.

## 4. Speed

Use slow, gentle, steady, or moderate in relation to the action. Avoid vague “dynamic” motion.

## 5. Acceleration and stopping

Define a smooth start and stable stop. Preschool action should not snap or overshoot.

## 6. Body mechanics

State balance, weight shift, hand contact, foot stability, and prop support.

## 7. Camera behavior

Use one behavior. Locked is the default for learning and controlled action.

## 8. Secondary motion

Allow only small supporting motion: clothing settles, scarf moves gently, leaves respond slightly. It should not become the focus.

## 9. Emotion

Emotion guides the performance but should not introduce extra gestures or actions.

## 10. Completion

State how the action visibly ends and how long the end state holds.

## 11. Protected elements

Identity, wardrobe, location, lighting, camera, prop design, and unrelated body parts remain unchanged.

## 12. Risk-based negatives

Prevent known failures such as lip movement, extra steps, hand distortion, prop duplication, camera drift, or background movement.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Motion prompt — Pick up the ball

Beginning from the approved first frame, Lumi bends slowly at the knees and waist while keeping both feet stable.

Her hands move together toward the red ball. Both hands make clear contact on opposite sides. She lifts the ball smoothly to waist height and returns to a balanced upright posture.

End exactly on the approved last frame and hold briefly.

Camera remains locked. Mouth remains closed. Keep identity, wardrobe, lighting, garden, baskets, and ball design unchanged.

No extra step, pointing, speech, head turn, prop duplication, floating, camera movement, or background change.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Write one motion prompt between an approved first frame and approved last frame.

Include:
- one primary action;
- movement beginning;
- path;
- speed;
- acceleration and stop;
- body mechanics;
- prop mechanics;
- limited secondary motion;
- emotion;
- exact completion;
- final hold;
- one camera behavior;
- allowed changes;
- protected elements;
- shot-specific negative constraints.

**Child-audience requirements**  
Movement must be readable, safe, smooth, and free from unnecessary speed or visual competition.

**Output requirements**  
Produce one concise motion prompt that does not introduce new story actions.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Make the image move smoothly and realistically into the last frame.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- The primary action is missing.
- Movement path is undefined.
- Body and prop mechanics are missing.
- Camera behavior is missing.
- “Realistically” may conflict with the stylized medium.
- Protected elements are absent.
- No completion or negative constraints are included.

Beginning from the approved first frame, Lumi slowly bends toward the red ball while both feet remain stable.

Keep the camera locked and the mouth closed. Preserve cut-paper style, identity, wardrobe, garden, lighting, baskets, and ball design.

---

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Redescribing the whole scene.
2. Using vague motion words.
3. Adding new actions.
4. Leaving body mechanics unstated.
5. Using several camera instructions.
6. Allowing excessive secondary motion.
7. Forgetting final hold.
8. Using generic negative lists.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Write four Motion Prompts:

1. Turn head toward object.
2. Walk two steps.
3. Pick up object.
4. Place object in basket.

For each, define boundaries, path, speed, mechanics, camera, protected elements, negatives, and completion.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

What should lead the motion prompt?

### Answer

The approved first and last frames.
### Question 2

How many primary actions?

### Answer

One.
### Question 3

What is the camera default?

### Answer

Locked.
### Question 4

Why define completion?

### Answer

To ensure the action reaches a stable usable ending.
### Question 5

What are risk-based negatives?

### Answer

Constraints targeting likely failures in the current shot.
