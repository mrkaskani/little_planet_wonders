## Lesson 17 of 72 — Designing Readable Recurring Locations

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Create recurring locations with stable layout and clear purpose.
- Design landmarks, entrances, exits, safe zones, and learning zones.
- Plan camera-friendly reference views.
- Create a Location Design Template.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Young children benefit from familiar environments. A recognizable gate, table, shelf, bench, or path helps them understand where characters are and where important actions occur.

For production, a recurring location must be more than one attractive image. It needs a stable layout that can support wide shots, reverse angles, action shots, close demonstrations, entrances, exits, and continuity across episodes.

If a location changes unpredictably, characters may appear to teleport, props may move, doors may switch sides, and safety barriers may disappear.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Location purpose

Define what kinds of stories and learning naturally belong there. The Cozy Workshop supports shape, sorting, craft, and demonstration scenes.

## 2. Stable layout

Record spatial relationships: entrance, central activity area, storage, windows, exits, and safety boundaries.

## 3. Landmarks

Use three to five memorable features. Too many landmarks create clutter. Each landmark should have a recognizable shape, color, or function.

## 4. Entry and exit points

Characters should move through consistent doors, paths, gates, or openings. Screen direction should support this geography.

## 5. Learning zones

Create a clear place where important objects can be shown without background competition.

## 6. Safe and restricted zones

Mark tool storage, high shelves, water boundaries, adult-only areas, and walking paths.

## 7. Camera-friendly views

Plan exterior, wide, reverse, entrance, activity, close demonstration, and safety-boundary views.

## 8. Background complexity

Keep the main action area simple. Decorative detail may exist in deeper background but should not compete with the learning object.

## 9. Location scale

Furniture, doors, shelves, and props should maintain consistent scale relative to characters.

## 10. Reference package

A Location Reference Plan should include layout diagram, approved views, palette, lighting, ambience, and continuity notes.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Design a readable recurring preschool location using the approved World Bible.

Define:
- story and learning purpose;
- stable layout;
- primary action area;
- three to five landmarks;
- entrances and exits;
- safe and supervised zones;
- recurring props and storage;
- character scale;
- background-complexity limits;
- approved camera views;
- temporary and permanent elements.

**Child-audience requirements**  
Clear orientation, simple action space, no frightening architecture, no visual crowding, and visible safety boundaries.

**Output requirements**  
Create a layout description and a reference-view plan rather than one isolated beauty image.

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> Create a huge magical workshop full of shelves, doors, stairs, machines, moving toys, lights, and hidden rooms.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- The space is too complex for quick orientation.
- Many doors and hidden rooms weaken continuity.
- Machines and moving toys compete with learning action.
- Safety boundaries are unclear.
- No stable layout or camera plan is defined.
- The location may become frightening or overstimulating.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Design the Cozy Workshop as one readable room with a rounded entrance on the left, low activity table in the center, organized storage on the back wall, rounded windows on the right, and one adult-supervised tool area.

Use three memorable landmarks: the shape shelf, sun-pattern rug, and green materials cabinet. Keep the center clear for demonstrations. Preserve layout, scale, and landmark positions in every view. Avoid hidden rooms, moving decorations, industrial machines, and crowded shelves.

---

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Creating only one wide image.
2. Adding too many landmarks.
3. Moving doors and shelves between angles.
4. Making backgrounds as detailed as the learning object.
5. Ignoring character scale.
6. Removing barriers for visual convenience.
7. Failing to plan reverse views.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create one detailed Location Design Template.

1. Define story and learning purpose.
2. Describe layout in five sentences.
3. Choose five landmarks.
4. Mark entrances, exits, safe, and restricted zones.
5. Place recurring props and storage.
6. Define scale references.
7. Plan six camera views.
8. List permanent and temporary elements.
9. Perform a geography and small-screen readability review.

---

# 14. Expected exercise result

A complete Location Design Template and Reference Plan.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

Why use landmarks?

### Answer

They support orientation and continuity.
### Question 2

What is a primary action area?

### Answer

The uncluttered zone where important story or learning action occurs.
### Question 3

Why plan reverse views?

### Answer

To preserve geography across shot direction changes.
### Question 4

Should safety barriers move for composition?

### Answer

No.
### Question 5

Why define scale references?

### Answer

To keep furniture and space consistent relative to characters.
