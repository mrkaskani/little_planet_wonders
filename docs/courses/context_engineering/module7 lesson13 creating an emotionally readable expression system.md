# Module 7

## Lesson 13 of 72 — Creating an Emotionally Readable Expression System

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Design a controlled expression range for preschool characters.
- Differentiate mild emotions without extreme facial distortion.
- Create an Expression Guide usable for image and video generation.
- Review expressions for clarity, comfort, and continuity.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Children under five often rely on faces to understand emotion. If an expression is too subtle, the child may miss it. If it is too extreme, the scene may feel frightening or chaotic.

AI generation may exaggerate eyes, mouths, tears, teeth, or asymmetry. A controlled expression system gives the production approved references for neutral, happy, curious, thinking, listening, mildly concerned, reassuring, celebrating, and asking for help.

Expression continuity also matters across first frames, last frames, speaking shots, and chained segments. Emotional changes should be visible but gradual.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Expression family

A set of approved expressions derived from the same neutral face. Every expression should preserve identity and proportions.

## 2. Neutral-friendly base

The neutral base should feel emotionally open rather than blank or unhappy. It becomes the reference from which other expressions change.

## 3. Emotion components

Expressions are built through eyebrows, eyes, mouth, cheeks, head angle, and posture. Change only what is needed.

## 4. Mild concern versus fear

Mild concern uses small eyebrow lift, gentle mouth change, and stable body. Fear may involve wide eyes, open mouth, trembling, and retreat. The example series normally uses mild concern, not fear.

## 5. Thinking and listening

Thinking may use a slight head tilt and gaze shift. Listening uses stable eye contact, relaxed mouth, and minimal movement. These are important participation expressions.

## 6. Reassuring expression

Use softened eyebrows, gentle eye contact, relaxed mouth, and open posture. Avoid exaggerated smiling during another character’s difficulty.

## 7. Celebration intensity

Celebration should be warm and readable, not explosive. A smile, small arm lift, or gentle hop may be enough.

## 8. Expression transitions

Plan beginning and ending expressions. A change from concern to relief may require a separate segment if the performance is important.

## 9. Mouth compatibility

For speaking video, expression must allow natural mouth movement. Extreme smiles or clenched teeth can interfere with lip synchronization.

## 10. Cultural and individual respect

Do not assume one facial display applies identically to every person or culture. Use clear story context and body language, and require human review.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Pip’s mild concern

**Starting expression:** neutral-friendly.

**Changes:**
- Inner eyebrows lift slightly.
- Eyes look toward the misplaced ball.
- Beak remains gently closed, then opens for the line.
- Head tilts a little.
- Wings remain relaxed.
- Body does not tremble or retreat.

**Line:** “This ball is not in a basket.”

The expression communicates uncertainty without panic.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Create an expression reference sheet for [CHARACTER] using the approved Character Bible and visual identity.

Include:
- neutral-friendly;
- happy-gentle;
- curious;
- thinking;
- listening;
- mildly concerned;
- reassuring;
- asking for help;
- calm celebration.

For each expression, define:
- eyebrow change;
- eye direction and openness;
- mouth shape;
- head angle;
- body posture;
- maximum intensity.

**Elements allowed to change**  
Expression components and slight head angle.

**Elements that must remain unchanged**  
Identity, proportions, face structure, colors, hair or fur, clothing, and art style.

**Child-safety requirements**  
Avoid distorted mouths, extremely wide eyes, aggressive teeth, excessive tears, trembling, threatening anger, or frightening facial transformation.

**Output requirements**  
Create a consistent expression sheet from the same character reference.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Make Lumi extremely shocked, terrified, and dramatic so children understand she is concerned.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Concern is being expressed as terror.
- Extreme shock may distort identity and frighten young viewers.
- The prompt does not define individual facial components.
- “Dramatic” may exaggerate mouth, eyes, and body movement.
- The expression may interfere with speaking performance.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Show Lumi with mild concern. Keep her identity and face proportions unchanged. Raise the inner eyebrows slightly, direct her eyes toward the object, soften the mouth into a small uncertain curve, and tilt the head a little.

Keep shoulders relaxed, hands close to the body, and breathing calm. Do not widen the eyes excessively, open the mouth dramatically, add tears, show teeth, or create trembling. The expression should communicate, “I noticed a small problem,” not danger.

---

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Changing the entire face for every emotion.
2. Using huge eyes for all reactions.
3. Adding tears too easily.
4. Making reassurance look like laughter.
5. Using an extreme smile during lip sync.
6. Ignoring body posture.
7. Generating each expression independently without the same reference.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create nine expression descriptions for one character.

For each:
1. Start from the neutral face.
2. Change eyebrows.
3. Change eye direction or openness.
4. Change mouth.
5. Add head and body direction.
6. Define maximum intensity.
7. Mark whether it is suitable for speaking.
8. Compare all expressions for identity consistency.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

Why use a neutral base?

### Answer

It keeps all expressions connected to the same identity.
### Question 2

What separates mild concern from fear?

### Answer

Smaller facial changes, relaxed body, and absence of panic signals.
### Question 3

Why include body posture?

### Answer

Emotion is communicated by the whole performance, not only the face.
### Question 4

Why limit celebration?

### Answer

Young children need readable, comfortable intensity.
### Question 5

Why review speaking compatibility?

### Answer

Extreme mouth shapes may interfere with natural lip synchronization.
