# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Sound helps young children understand where they are, what moved, what touched, and what changed. A gentle ball roll, basket placement, footstep, cloth movement, or watering-can pour can make cause and effect clearer.

However, sound becomes confusing when every movement receives an effect or when ambience, Foley, music, dialogue, and decorative sounds compete. Preschool sound design should prioritize essential visible actions and stable location identity.

A recurring object should sound consistent. A soft fabric ball should not sound metallic in one scene and plastic in another. A location should have a recognizable ambience, but it must remain below dialogue and change appropriately with camera distance.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Ambience

Ambience is the continuing environmental sound of a place: leaves, distant birds, room tone, gentle water, or soft indoor activity.

## 2. Foley

Foley is sound connected to visible body or object movement: footsteps, clothing rustle, picking up a ball, placing a basket, or opening a drawer.

## 3. Sound effect

A sound effect may represent a visible event, learning cue, magical moment, transition, or recurring signal. It should have a clear purpose.

## 4. Essential versus decorative sound

Essential sound helps the child understand action or place. Decorative sound adds style but may be removed without losing meaning. Essential sounds receive priority.

## 5. Location ambience profile

Each location needs a stable base ambience, close perspective, wide perspective, weather variation, and quiet participation version.

## 6. Prop sound identity

Material, size, weight, and action determine sound. The red fabric ball rolls softly and lands with a muted sound. The wooden basket has a gentle wooden placement sound.

## 7. Character Foley

Character movement should reflect size and material. Lumi’s boots have soft garden footsteps. Pip’s hops are light. Auntie Noura’s movement is calm and grounded.

## 8. Sound perspective

A close object sounds clearer and more detailed. A distant action sounds softer. Sound should match camera distance and location.

## 9. Sound continuity

Ambience should continue naturally across cuts. Sudden changes require a visible location, weather, or time transition.

## 10. Sound and participation

During a question, remove unnecessary Foley and effects. Keep only quiet ambience unless silence is more effective.

## 11. Child-safety limits

Avoid sudden loud sounds, harsh metal impacts, alarms, screams, aggressive animal sounds, high-pitched repeated effects, and large volume jumps.

## 12. Layer priority

Dialogue first, important visible-action sound second, ambience third, decorative effects last. Music is planned separately and should not mask these layers.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Sound identity for the red-ball sequence

### Location ambience

- Soft garden leaves
- Very distant birds
- No constant insect buzzing
- No loud wind

### Prop Foley

- Ball rolling: soft fabric roll
- Ball pickup: gentle cloth contact
- Ball placed in basket: soft fabric against light wood

### Character Foley

- Lumi walking: quiet yellow-boot footsteps
- Pip hopping: two light taps
- Clothing movement: subtle, only when visible

### Participation pause

- Music stops
- No footsteps or prop movement
- Garden ambience becomes very quiet

The sound design clarifies action without filling every frame.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Add a sound effect to every movement, make footsteps loud, use cartoon boings for Pip, and fill the garden with birds, insects, wind, music, and magical sparkles.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Every action becomes sonically emphasized.
- Loud footsteps may make movement feel heavy or threatening.
- Cartoon boings may reduce Pip to a noisy joke.
- Several ambience layers compete with dialogue.
- Magical sounds have no defined story purpose.
- No sound perspective or material continuity is provided.
- The result may become tiring and difficult to understand.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Use only essential sound. Keep the Rainbow Garden ambience soft: gentle leaves and distant birds.

Give Lumi quiet boot footsteps, Pip two light hop taps, the red fabric ball a soft roll, and the wooden basket a gentle placement sound. Add no effect when an action is already clear and silent.

Reduce ambience during dialogue. Make the participation pause nearly silent. Do not use loud footsteps, boings, alarms, constant sparkle effects, dense insect noise, or effects on every gesture.

---

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Adding sound to every movement.
2. Using the wrong material sound.
3. Making footsteps too loud.
4. Changing ambience between matching shots.
5. Using effects as constant comedy.
6. Forgetting camera distance.
7. Stacking ambience, Foley, effects, and music at equal levels.
8. Using harsh failure sounds.
