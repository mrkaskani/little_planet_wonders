# 1. Learning objective

- Build the complete input package for a speaking-character shot.
- Use an approved image, locked exact dialogue, and clear performance direction.
- Separate speaking input from music, ambience, Foley, and effects.
- Create a Speaking-Shot Prompt.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

A speaking-character video has three essential creative inputs:

1. An approved character image or first frame.
2. Locked exact dialogue audio.
3. Performance direction.

If the image is inconsistent, the video begins from the wrong identity or pose. If the audio is temporary, the mouth cannot match the final line. If the performance direction is vague, the face, eyes, head, and hands may move excessively.

The clean dialogue determines duration and lip timing. Music and effects are added later. This separation improves clarity, review, and repair.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Approved image

The image establishes identity, wardrobe, expression, camera, lighting, location, and starting pose.

## 2. Locked exact audio

The audio contains the final words with approved pronunciation, emotion, timing, pauses, and duration.

## 3. Performance direction

Defines face, mouth, eyes, head, hands, posture, listening, and emotional intensity.

## 4. Shot purpose

The performance should support one purpose: explain, ask, reassure, repeat, sing, or listen.

## 5. Starting performance state

Define what the character is doing immediately before speech begins.

## 6. Ending performance state

Define where the face, gaze, hands, and emotion settle after the line.

## 7. Lip-sync source

Use dialogue only. Do not include music, ambience, Foley, success cues, or other voices.

## 8. Gesture budget

Limit the number and size of gestures. One small gesture may be enough for a short line.

## 9. Eye direction

State whether the character looks at the audience, an object, another character, or shifts once between them.

## 10. Head movement

Use small nods, tilts, or turns. Avoid constant bobbing or unrelated movement.

## 11. Performance continuity

The speaking shot should match the previous and next shot’s pose, prop state, emotion, screen direction, and camera.

## 12. Reviewability

The package should make it possible to determine whether failure came from image, audio, or direction.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Speaking shot

**Line:** “Which basket matches the red ball?”

### Approved image

- Lumi holds the red ball at chest height
- Red and blue baskets visible
- Eye-level medium-wide
- Curious expression
- Mouth gently closed
- Camera locked

### Locked audio

- Exact approved line
- Curious emotional reference
- Medium-slow pace
- Clean dialogue only
- Response pause included after the question

### Performance direction

- Small head turn toward the baskets
- Eyes shift from ball to baskets
- One gentle eyebrow lift
- Hands continue holding the ball
- No extra pointing
- Mouth movement natural and restrained
- After speech, mouth closes and Lumi listens

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Create one speaking-character video using:

**Approved image**  
[Image ID and state]

**Locked exact dialogue audio**  
[Audio ID, line, duration, emotion]

**Shot purpose**  
[Purpose]

**Performance direction**
- face;
- natural mouth movement;
- eye direction;
- head movement;
- hands or wings;
- body posture;
- listening pause;
- ending state.

**Elements allowed to change**  
Mouth movement, one planned gaze shift, small head motion, one approved gesture, and subtle expression change.

**Elements that must remain unchanged**  
Identity, wardrobe, body proportions, prop, holder, camera, framing, location, lighting, background, and audio wording.

**Negative constraints**  
No extra words, no unrelated gesture, no camera movement, no prop change, no identity drift, no excessive mouth movement, no shouting expression, and no background sound in the lip-sync source.

**Output requirements**  
One complete speaking shot matching the locked audio duration.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Animate Lumi talking with this image and make her expressive.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- The exact audio is not identified.
- The line and duration are missing.
- “Expressive” may create excessive motion.
- Eye, head, hands, body, and listening state are undefined.
- Camera and prop continuity are unprotected.
- Mixed audio may be used.
- The shot purpose and ending state are missing.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Use the approved medium-wide image of Lumi holding the red ball and the locked clean dialogue audio for: “Which basket matches the red ball?”

Preserve the exact image identity, wardrobe, camera, lighting, location, baskets, and ball position.

Use gentle curiosity. Let Lumi’s eyes shift once from the ball to the baskets, raise the eyebrows slightly, and make one small head turn. Keep both hands on the ball. Use natural restrained mouth movement.

After the line, close the mouth, keep the camera locked, and hold an attentive listening pose during the response pause. No extra gesture, no pointing, no prop movement, no music, and no added words.

---

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Using temporary dialogue.
2. Using mixed soundtrack audio.
3. Writing “be expressive” without limits.
4. Adding several gestures.
5. Moving the prop during speech unintentionally.
6. Forgetting the listening pose.
7. Allowing camera movement.
8. Failing to specify ending state.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create two Speaking-Shot Packages.

1. A participation question.
2. A reassurance line.

For each:
- select approved image;
- attach locked dialogue;
- state purpose;
- direct face, mouth, eyes, head, hands, body, and pause;
- define allowed and unchanged elements;
- add forbidden performance;
- create review criteria.
