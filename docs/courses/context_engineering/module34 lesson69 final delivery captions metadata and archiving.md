## Lesson 69 of 72 — Final Delivery, Captions, Metadata, and Archiving

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Prepare the approved episode for delivery without changing creative intent.
- Create readable captions and subtitle timing from the exact final script.
- Package picture, sound, review reports, and metadata clearly.
- Archive masters, source assets, versions, prompts, references, and continuity records.
- Create a Final Delivery and Archive Checklist.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

A finished episode is not complete until it is delivered and archived in a way that preserves quality and future continuity. Poor captions may paraphrase learning words, appear too quickly, or obscure important visual information. Missing metadata can make versions difficult to identify. Weak archiving can separate the final episode from its approved script, references, and review history.

Delivery should preserve the exact dialogue and approved mix. Accessibility materials should be created carefully. Archive structure should make it possible to repair the episode, reuse approved assets, and begin the next episode without guessing.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Delivery master

The final approved picture and sound version identified by a stable name and version.

## 2. Caption fidelity

Captions should match the exact spoken words. Do not simplify, paraphrase, or remove target vocabulary without approved reason.

## 3. Caption timing

Text should appear with the spoken phrase and remain long enough to read. Avoid rapid flashing captions.

## 4. Caption placement

Keep captions away from important faces, mouths, learning objects, and safety information.

## 5. Speaker identification

When needed, identify the speaker clearly and consistently without clutter.

## 6. Sound description

Important nonverbal sounds may be described when they carry meaning, but avoid captioning every minor sound.

## 7. Language and translation control

Translated captions and audio require new linguistic, educational, cultural, and timing review.

## 8. Metadata

Record title, episode number, learning goal, audience, language, duration, version, approval status, and review date.

## 9. Review-package inclusion

Keep final child-safety, educational, continuity, audio-picture, and mix approvals with the delivery record.

## 10. Source archive

Archive scripts, audio masters, picture masters, references, prompts, keyframes, segment outputs, edit project, mix project, cue sheets, and review reports.

## 11. Version discipline

Separate draft, review, approved, and superseded files clearly.

## 12. Future repair

The archive should allow one line, shot, caption, or cue to be revised without reconstructing the entire production.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Prepare a Final Delivery and Archive Checklist for a completed preschool episode.

Include:
- final master identification;
- caption and transcript fidelity;
- caption timing and placement;
- target vocabulary preservation;
- important sound descriptions;
- metadata;
- child-safety, educational, continuity, lip-sync, picture, and mix approval records;
- complete source archive;
- version separation;
- backup and recovery check.

**Important rules**
- Captions must match approved dialogue.
- Translations require new review.
- Delivery files must not replace editable source archives.
- No episode is ready when approval records or critical masters are missing.

**Output requirements**
Produce a complete delivery package manifest and archive checklist.

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> Export the final video, add automatic captions, and delete old files to save space.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Automatic captions may mishear names and learning words.
- Caption timing and placement are not reviewed.
- Review reports may be lost.
- Deleting old files may remove approved sources and repair capability.
- Version authority becomes unclear.
- Metadata and archive structure are missing.
- Translations and accessibility are not controlled.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Create the final approved delivery master and a separate complete source archive.

Generate captions from the exact approved script, then review every word, target term, timing, reading duration, speaker label, and placement. Preserve review reports and metadata.

Archive editable picture, audio, script, reference, keyframe, prompt, continuity, and review assets. Mark drafts, superseded files, and approved masters clearly. Do not delete source material required for repair or future continuity.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Trusting automatic captions.
2. Paraphrasing target vocabulary.
3. Covering faces or learning objects.
4. Keeping only the final video.
5. Mixing drafts and approved files.
6. Deleting rejected-version evidence too early.
7. Forgetting review reports.
8. Failing to test recovery.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create a Final Delivery and Archive Checklist.

1. Define the master filename and version.
2. Create a caption-review plan.
3. Add metadata.
4. Add all required review reports.
5. List every source asset category.
6. Define approved, draft, and superseded storage.
7. Define backup and recovery checks.
8. Mark any missing delivery blockers.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

Should automatic captions be trusted without review?

### Answer

No.
### Question 2

What should captions match?

### Answer

The exact approved spoken dialogue.
### Question 3

Why archive source assets?

### Answer

To support repair, continuity, reuse, and future episodes.
### Question 4

Can translated captions use the same approval?

### Answer

No. They require new review.
### Question 5

What is a delivery blocker?

### Answer

A missing critical master, caption review, metadata item, or approval record.
