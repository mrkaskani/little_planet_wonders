# 1. Learning objective

- Design a first frame as the exact visual starting state of a shot or segment.
- Combine approved references into one clean composition.
- Control identity, camera, lighting, emotion, props, and safety.
- Create a First-Frame Prompt and approval checklist.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

The first frame is not merely an attractive image. It is the precise starting state from which motion begins. If the pose, prop position, camera, expression, or background is wrong, the generated action must either correct the mistake unnaturally or continue from an incorrect state.

A strong first frame gives the video process a stable identity, composition, and physical arrangement. It also helps reviewers decide whether the planned action is safe, readable, and possible.

For children under five, the first frame should make the main subject easy to find and should not contain confusing extra action.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. First-frame purpose

The frame establishes who is present, where they are, what they hold, how they feel, where they look, and what movement can begin next.

## 2. Composition

Choose shot size, camera height, character placement, object placement, negative space, and background complexity.

## 3. Action readiness

The pose should naturally lead into the intended movement. A character preparing to pick up a ball should be near it, balanced, and looking at it.

## 4. Prop state

Record holder, hand, location, orientation, material, color, and condition.

## 5. Character state

Record body orientation, feet, hands, head, eyes, mouth, expression, and wardrobe.

## 6. Camera state

The first frame locks perspective, framing, screen direction, and lens feel for the segment.

## 7. Lighting state

Light direction, softness, color, and shadow must be approved before motion.

## 8. Background state

Landmarks, barriers, furniture, storage, and weather must match the Location Bible.

## 9. One future action

The frame should support one primary next action. Do not pose the character halfway through several different actions.

## 10. Safety review

Check posture, distance from hazards, tool use, adult supervision, and imitable behavior.

## 11. Small-screen clarity

Reduce the frame and confirm that character, learning object, and action direction remain readable.

## 12. Approval lock

Once approved, the first frame becomes a controlled source. Do not replace it casually with a prettier but inconsistent image.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## First frame for Lumi picking up the red ball

- Eye-level medium-wide shot
- Locked-camera perspective
- Lumi stands left of the ball
- Feet stable
- Hands empty and relaxed
- Torso slightly turned toward the ball
- Eyes on the ball
- Mouth gently closed
- Expression: curious
- Ball centered clearly on the path
- Red and blue baskets visible in the background but not dominant
- Soft warm daylight
- No other characters moving
- No extra props

This frame gives the next segment one clear action: bend and pick up the ball.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Create one approved first frame for a preschool video segment.

**Permanent context**  
Use the approved character, wardrobe, expression, pose, location, prop, visual, color, lighting, camera, and safety references.

**Current context**  
[Episode, scene, shot, segment]

**Next primary action**  
[One action]

Define exact:
- character position and orientation;
- feet, hands, head, eyes, mouth, and expression;
- wardrobe;
- prop location, holder, orientation, and state;
- location landmarks and safety boundaries;
- shot size, camera height, perspective, and screen direction;
- lighting and color;
- allowed variation;
- unchanged elements.

**Child-audience requirements**  
One clear focus, safe posture, readable face, visible learning object, simple background, no threatening angle, and no unnecessary motion implied.

**Output requirements**  
One single scene frame, not a reference collage.

**Review criteria**  
The frame must be ready for exactly one next action.

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> Create a cinematic image of Lumi near the ball, looking dynamic and ready to do many things.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- “Near” does not define position.
- “Dynamic” may create an unstable or extreme pose.
- Several possible next actions are implied.
- Hands, eyes, mouth, prop, and wardrobe state are unclear.
- Camera and lighting are undefined.
- Location continuity and safety boundaries are missing.
- The output may prioritize beauty over action readiness.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Create one first-frame image for a controlled pickup segment.

Lumi stands left of the red fabric ball on the Rainbow Garden path. Her feet are stable, hands empty, torso turned slightly toward the ball, eyes focused on it, mouth gently closed, and expression curious.

Use an eye-level medium-wide locked-camera composition with soft warm daylight. Keep the approved yellow gate, blue watering station, red and blue baskets, path layout, clothing, character proportions, and ball identity unchanged.

No running pose, no reaching yet, no extra gesture, no mouth movement, no extra objects, and no camera tilt. The frame must clearly prepare for one next action: bend and pick up the ball.

---

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Choosing a beautiful pose that cannot lead into the action.
2. Leaving hands undefined.
3. Starting halfway through the movement.
4. Allowing the mouth to move in a non-speaking shot.
5. Using the wrong camera angle.
6. Changing the location layout.
7. Adding decorative props.
8. Approving without thumbnail review.

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

For each:
- define character state;
- define prop state;
- define location state;
- define camera and lighting;
- define the next action;
- list allowed and unchanged elements;
- complete safety and small-screen review.

What does a first frame control?

### Answer

The exact visual starting state of the segment.

### Answer

The next movement should begin naturally.

### Answer

No.
### Question 4

What happens after approval?

### Answer

The first frame becomes a controlled source.

### Answer

To confirm subject, learning object, and action remain readable.
