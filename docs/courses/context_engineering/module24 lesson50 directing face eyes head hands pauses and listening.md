# Module 24

## Lesson 50 of 72 — Directing Face, Eyes, Head, Hands, Pauses, and Listening

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Direct each part of a speaking performance with controlled intensity.
- Coordinate mouth, eyes, head, hands, and body around the exact dialogue.
- Create readable listening and participation pauses.
- Prevent excessive motion, frozen faces, and unwanted gestures.
- Create a Character Performance Direction Sheet.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Lip synchronization alone does not create a convincing speaking performance. A character may pronounce every word correctly but still feel unnatural if the eyes wander, the head moves constantly, the hands gesture without purpose, or the face remains frozen.

For children under five, performance must be emotionally readable and calm enough to follow. The character should not perform every word with a new gesture. One gaze shift, one small head movement, and one meaningful hand gesture may be enough.

Listening is also a performance. After asking a question, the character should stop speaking, close the mouth, remain attentive, and give the child time to respond.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Face direction

Define the starting expression, emotional change, and ending expression. Keep changes gradual and proportional.

## 2. Mouth direction

Mouth movement follows the locked audio. Avoid exaggerated opening, constant smiling, visible teeth, or movement before and after speech.

## 3. Eye direction

Eyes communicate attention. Limit gaze shifts and ensure they match the object, audience, or other character.

## 4. Blinking

Blink naturally and infrequently. Excessive blinking can look nervous or unstable.

## 5. Head movement

Use a small turn, tilt, or nod when it supports meaning. Avoid continuous bobbing.

## 6. Hand or wing gesture

Use one gesture for one idea. Keep hands away from the mouth when possible and preserve prop grip.

## 7. Body posture

The torso and feet should remain stable unless the line requires a clear movement.

## 8. Speech preparation

A character may take a small breath or look toward the listener before speaking.

## 9. Listening pose

After speech, the mouth closes, expression softens, eyes remain attentive, body becomes calm, and major movement stops.

## 10. Participation pause

The pause should contain enough life to feel attentive but not enough motion to distract.

## 11. Singing performance

Singing may use broader breath, rhythm, and gesture, but still requires controlled facial movement, comfortable intensity, and exact audio.

## 12. Performance continuity

Start and end states must match neighboring shots. Track prop holder, gaze, expression, hand position, and body orientation.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Performance direction for Lumi’s question

> “Which basket matches the red ball?”

### Before speech

- Lumi looks at the ball.
- Mouth closed.
- Both hands hold the ball.
- Gentle curious expression.

### During “Which basket”

- Eyes shift once toward the two baskets.
- Head turns slightly in the same direction.
- Eyebrows lift gently.

### During “matches the red ball”

- Eyes return briefly to the ball.
- Hands stay still.
- Mouth movement remains natural.

### After speech

- Mouth closes.
- Eyes move toward the baskets.
- Head remains still.
- Lumi holds an attentive listening expression.
- No gesture or body movement during the response pause.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Make Lumi move naturally while talking, use lots of facial expression and hand gestures, and keep her active during the pause.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- “Naturally” is undefined.
- “Lots” encourages excessive movement.
- Hand gestures may conflict with the held prop.
- Active pause contradicts listening.
- Eye, head, mouth, and body direction are missing.
- Gesture budget is undefined.
- Ending state and continuity are absent.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Begin with Lumi looking at the red ball, mouth closed, both hands holding it, and a gentle curious expression.

During the first phrase, shift the eyes once toward the baskets and make one small head turn. Raise the eyebrows slightly. Keep the hands still and preserve the ball grip.

Use natural mouth movement only while speaking. After the line, close the mouth and hold an attentive listening pose. Keep the camera and body stable during the pause.

Maximum: one gaze shift, one head turn, no hand gesture, no body step, no extra blink sequence, and no prop movement.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Directing every word with a gesture.
2. Allowing eyes to wander.
3. Keeping the mouth moving after speech.
4. Using constant head nodding.
5. Moving hands while holding a prop.
6. Making the pause visually busy.
7. Adding blinks as repeated animation.
8. Failing to define ending state.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

What should mouth movement follow?

### Answer

The locked exact dialogue audio.
### Question 2

How many gestures are usually needed for a short line?

### Answer

Often zero or one.
### Question 3

What is a listening pose?

### Answer

A calm attentive state after speech with the mouth closed.
### Question 4

Why limit gaze shifts?

### Answer

To preserve clarity and natural attention.
### Question 5

What must performance ending state match?

### Answer

The next shot or segment continuity.
