## Lesson 28 of 72 — Pronunciation, Emotional Range, and Maximum Intensity

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Create pronunciation rules for names, repeated vocabulary, and educational terms.
- Define a controlled emotional voice range.
- Protect character identity across emotional delivery.
- Complete a Character Voice Bible.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Incorrect pronunciation can confuse a learning goal, change a character’s name, or create inconsistency across episodes. A pronunciation guide is especially important for invented names, culturally specific names, repeated educational words, songs, and words with several accepted pronunciations.

Emotion also changes voice. A happy line may become faster and higher; concern may become quieter; reassurance may slow down. Without limits, emotional delivery can change the apparent identity of the voice.

The Character Voice Bible records both pronunciation and emotional boundaries.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Pronunciation guide

Record difficult names and words in a readable sound guide, syllable breakdown, stress pattern, and approved example.

## 2. Name consistency

Character and location names should sound the same across dialogue, narration, songs, and episodes.

## 3. Learning-word clarity

Target vocabulary must be especially clear. Do not swallow, stretch, or stylize the word so much that children cannot recognize it.

## 4. Accent consistency

If a character has an accent, define it respectfully and consistently. Do not exaggerate it for comedy.

## 5. Neutral-friendly reference

The baseline emotional delivery against which all other emotions are compared.

## 6. Happy-gentle

Slightly brighter tone and energy, but stable pace and volume.

## 7. Curious

A small upward contour, clear pause, and attentive timing. Avoid constant question-like speech.

## 8. Mild concern

Slightly softer or slower delivery, not trembling, crying, or panic.

## 9. Reassuring

Warm, stable, clear, slower, and supported by gentle breath and pauses.

## 10. Maximum intensity

Define the strongest allowed vocal emotion. The example series avoids screaming, threatening anger, prolonged crying, and explosive excitement.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Create a Character Voice Bible using the approved Voice Identity Profile.

Include:
- pronunciation table for character names, location names, recurring vocabulary, and learning terms;
- respectful accent rules if relevant;
- emotional direction for neutral-friendly, happy-gentle, curious, mild concern, reassuring, asking for help, and calm celebration;
- maximum pitch, pace, energy, and volume;
- forbidden delivery;
- learning-word clarity rules;
- continuity review.

**Elements allowed to change**  
Emotion, pause placement, and limited emphasis.

**Elements that must remain unchanged**  
Voice identity, vocal age, accent, pronunciation, core pitch range, and comfort.

**Output requirements**  
Practical direction for voice generation, performance review, and continuity.

---

# 7. Weak prompt

> Make the voice sound completely different for every emotion so children can tell how the character feels.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Character identity would be lost.
- Extreme pitch and speed changes may become uncomfortable.
- Emotion should not replace pronunciation clarity.
- No maximum intensity is defined.
- The prompt may encourage caricature, crying, or shouting.
- Continuity across scenes becomes weak.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Preserve Lumi’s warm childlike identity in every emotional state. Adjust only a small number of features.

For curiosity, add a gentle upward contour and thoughtful pause. For mild concern, soften the volume slightly and slow the phrase. For reassurance, use warm stable tone, clear articulation, and a slower pace.

Do not change vocal age, accent, core pitch, or pronunciation. Avoid screaming, trembling, sobbing, breathless delivery, aggressive emphasis, or extreme pitch jumps.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Changing vocal age with emotion.
2. Using a different accent in songs.
3. Pronouncing names inconsistently.
4. Overacting mild concern.
5. Stretching learning words unnaturally.
6. Allowing excitement to become shouting.
7. Skipping pronunciation review.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Complete a Character Voice Bible.

1. Create a pronunciation table with at least ten entries.
2. Define accent rules if relevant.
3. Define seven emotional states.
4. Set pitch, pace, energy, and volume limits.
5. List forbidden delivery.
6. Record learning-word clarity rules.
7. Compare two emotional directions for identity consistency.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

Why create a pronunciation guide?

### Answer

To keep names and learning terms consistent and clear.
### Question 2

Should voice identity change with emotion?

### Answer

No. Emotion modifies delivery within the same identity.
### Question 3

What is maximum intensity?

### Answer

The strongest approved pitch, pace, energy, and volume.
### Question 4

Can accent be exaggerated for humor?

### Answer

No.
### Question 5

Why protect learning words?

### Answer

They carry the educational purpose and must remain understandable.
