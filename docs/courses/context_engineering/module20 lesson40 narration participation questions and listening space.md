# Module 20

## Lesson 40 of 72 — Narration, Participation Questions, and Listening Space

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Use narration only when it improves understanding.
- Differentiate narration from character dialogue and visible action.
- Write one clear participation question at a time.
- Create listening space through pauses, silence, and restrained editing.
- Complete a Narration Script and Participation-Pause Plan.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Narration can help orient the child, connect scenes, name an unseen time change, or clarify a concept. It can also become unnecessary when it merely describes what the child already sees.

Over-narration fills quiet space and may compete with character performance. A child needs time to observe expressions, watch actions, and respond to questions. Participation questions should be prepared visually and followed by a genuine pause.

Narration, dialogue, and silence should work together. They should not all occupy the same moment.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Narrator role

Define whether the narrator welcomes, orients, connects scenes, asks questions, names learning, or recaps. The narrator should not explain every action.

## 2. Show versus tell

If Lumi visibly places the ball in the basket, narration does not need to say, “Lumi places the ball in the basket,” unless the wording teaches an important concept.

## 3. Orientation narration

A short line may establish time, place, or activity when the visual alone is not enough.

## 4. Transition narration

Narration may connect a clear time or location change without requiring another dialogue scene.

## 5. Learning narration

Use narration to name or reinforce a concept only when it adds clarity beyond character dialogue.

## 6. Question design

Ask one short question tied to visible choices. Avoid combining identification, explanation, and prediction into one line.

## 7. Question preparation

Show the objects, explain the task simply, and ensure the child understands what to look for before asking.

## 8. Listening pose

During the pause, characters remain attentive. The camera stays stable. Music and effects reduce. No important action occurs.

## 9. Pause length by task

Repeating one word may need less time than choosing between objects or making a prediction. Human review determines whether the pause feels sufficient for the central audience.

## 10. Confirmation

After the pause, confirm gently and show the answer. Do not judge the child’s speed or correctness.

## 11. Narration voice identity

The narrator needs a stable voice profile: warmth, pace, pronunciation, emotional range, and relationship to the audience.

## 12. Listening-space continuity

Editing, music, ambience, and character movement must preserve the planned pause. A pause written in the script can disappear during editing if not tracked.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Over-narrated version

**Narrator:** “Lumi walks toward the ball. Lumi bends down. Lumi picks up the red ball. Pip watches. Lumi turns toward the baskets.”

The narration repeats visible action and leaves no quiet observation time.

## Improved version

Lumi walks toward the ball in silence.

A soft ball-pickup sound is heard.

**Narrator:** “Lumi found a red ball.”

Lumi shows the two baskets.

**Lumi:** “Which basket matches?”

The camera stays still. Music stops. Lumi and Pip wait.

**Narrator after the pause:** “The red basket matches the red ball.”

The narration adds naming and confirmation without describing every movement.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Write narration and participation pauses for a preschool episode.

Use the approved dialogue, Episode Breakdown, learning goal, emotional goal, and audio bibles.

For narration:
- identify where narration is genuinely needed;
- remove lines that only describe visible action;
- use short concrete wording;
- preserve narrator identity;
- avoid overlap with important dialogue;
- support orientation, transition, learning, or recap.

For participation:
- show choices first;
- ask one clear question;
- define listening pose;
- keep camera stable;
- reduce music, ambience, Foley, and effects;
- define intended response;
- confirm gently;
- show the answer;
- protect the pause during editing.

**Output requirements**  
Produce an exact Narration Script and Participation-Pause Plan.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Have the narrator explain everything, ask several questions quickly, keep music playing, and let the characters move during the pause.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Narration competes with visible storytelling.
- Several questions increase processing load.
- Music competes with the child’s response.
- Character movement may distract from the choices.
- The pause is not a genuine listening moment.
- Narration and dialogue roles are undefined.
- Editing may remove the child’s response time.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Use narration only for orientation, one key learning label, transition, and recap.

Before the participation question, show the red and blue baskets clearly. Let Lumi ask, “Which basket matches the red ball?”

Keep the camera locked. Let Lumi and Pip appear to listen. Stop music and remove unnecessary Foley. After a human-reviewed pause, confirm, “The red basket matches,” and show Lumi placing the ball inside.

Do not ask several questions, narrate visible movement, or shorten the pause without review.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Narrating every action.
2. Using narration to replace visual clarity.
3. Asking multiple questions.
4. Playing dense music during pauses.
5. Letting characters perform unrelated movement.
6. Confirming too quickly.
7. Using the narrator as an authority that judges the child.
8. Cutting pauses during final editing.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create a Narration Script and Participation-Pause Plan.

1. Review one scene and remove unnecessary narration.
2. Add only orientation, learning, transition, or recap narration.
3. Write one participation question.
4. List visible choices.
5. Define listening poses.
6. Define camera and audio behavior.
7. Define the response type.
8. Write confirmation.
9. Add editing-protection notes.
10. Read the full scene aloud with pauses.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

When is narration useful?

### Answer

When it adds orientation, transition, learning clarity, or recap.
### Question 2

Should narration describe every visible action?

### Answer

No.
### Question 3

How many main questions should a participation moment ask?

### Answer

One.
### Question 4

What should happen to music during a response pause?

### Answer

It becomes very quiet or stops.
### Question 5

Why protect pauses during editing?

### Answer

The child needs real processing and response time.
