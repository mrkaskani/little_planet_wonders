# Module 6

## Lesson 11 of 72 — Designing a Readable Preschool Character

**Estimated study time:** 15 minutes  
**Estimated practice time:** 15 minutes

---

# 1. Learning objective

- Design a character whose identity remains readable on a small screen.
- Use silhouette, shape language, color, face, clothing, and accessories intentionally.
- Separate permanent identity features from temporary story changes.
- Create a Character Identity Sheet and Character Reference Plan.

---

# 2. Why it matters for children under five

Young children often recognize a character before they understand the dialogue. A clear silhouette, stable color pattern, readable face, and consistent clothing help the child know who is present and what the character is feeling.

AI-generated character images can drift. Hair may change length, clothing may change color, accessories may disappear, body proportions may shift, or the face may become more detailed from one shot to another. A strong Character Identity Sheet reduces this risk by defining what the character always looks like and what may change temporarily.

Readability is especially important on phones, tablets, and small televisions. Fine costume details, tiny facial features, complex patterns, and similar colors can disappear or merge. Preschool character design should therefore prioritize recognition over decorative complexity.


## Recurring example series

All examples continue the same evolving preschool series:

# Lumi and Pip’s Little Garden

- **Lumi:** a curious four-year-old child with warm brown skin, a dark curly bob, coral overalls, a cream shirt, yellow boots, and a leaf-shaped hair clip.
- **Pip:** a small rounded teal-blue bird with a cream belly, orange beak, sky-blue scarf, large expressive eyes, gentle hops, and short calm flights.

- **Audio identity:** warm voices, short sentences, restrained music, clear ambience, gentle Foley, and protected quiet space.

# 3. Core concepts

## 1. Character readability

A readable character can be recognized quickly by shape, color, and movement. The child should not need a close-up or long explanation to know who the character is.

## 2. Silhouette

A silhouette is the outer shape of the character. It should remain recognizable even when shown as a dark shape. Lumi’s rounded hair, compact body, overalls, and boots create a different silhouette from Auntie Noura’s taller body, apron, and low bun.

## 3. Shape language

Rounded shapes usually feel gentle and approachable. Sharp, narrow, or heavily angular shapes may feel more intense. Shape language should support personality without stereotyping or making any body type seem “good” or “bad.”

## 4. Face design

Preschool faces should communicate emotion clearly. Large readable eyes, simple eyebrows, a simple mouth, and enough space between features help expressions remain understandable. Avoid excessive facial detail that changes unpredictably across generations.

## 5. Color identity

Use a small, stable character palette. Lumi’s coral overalls, cream shirt, and yellow boots are easier to track than clothing with many patterns and colors. Character colors should remain distinct from the background.

## 6. Clothing as identity

Core clothing may function like a uniform. It supports recognition and continuity. Variations should be planned rather than improvised. If Lumi wears rain clothing, the new outfit should still preserve her identity colors and silhouette.

## 7. Accessories

An accessory should have a clear purpose. Lumi’s leaf hair clip helps recognition. Pip’s scarf adds silhouette and motion. Too many accessories increase continuity failures and visual clutter.

## 8. Proportion consistency

Record approximate head-to-body ratio, body width, limb length, hand size, eye size, and height relative to other characters. These relationships matter more than exact measurement.

## 9. Permanent and temporary features

Permanent features include identity colors, face structure, body proportions, hair, and core clothing. Temporary features may include dirt, wetness, a raincoat, a held prop, or a specific expression.

## 10. Small-screen test

Reduce the character image until it is approximately thumbnail size. Check whether the silhouette, main colors, face direction, and current action remain readable.

# 4. Practical example from the ongoing series

## Weak Lumi design

- Highly detailed floral overalls
- Several bracelets and pins
- Tiny eyes
- Complex shoe laces
- Hair changing between loose curls and braids
- Different shirt colors in every image
- Background colors similar to clothing

This design creates continuity and readability problems.

## Improved Lumi design

- Stable dark curly bob
- One leaf-shaped hair clip
- Coral overalls with large simple shapes
- Cream shirt
- Yellow boots
- Large brown eyes and simple eyebrows
- Rounded silhouette
- Clear color contrast from the environment

The improved design can be recognized in a wide shot, close-up, and moving scene.

# 6. Reusable prompt template

Create a readable preschool character design using the approved Series Bible, audience profile, visual direction, and safety rules.

**Permanent context**  
The series uses rounded 2D cut-paper styling, simple facial features, warm colors, stable proportions, clear silhouettes, and uncluttered backgrounds.

**Character role**  
[Role]

**Personality function**  
[How the character supports learning and emotion]

Define:
- silhouette;
- shape language;
- body proportions;
- face structure;
- hair, fur, or body surface;
- stable color palette;
- core clothing;
- one or two identity accessories;
- height relative to other characters;
- features that may change temporarily;
- features that must never change.

**Child-audience requirements**  
The character must remain readable in wide shots and on small screens. Avoid frightening detail, excessive accessories, sharp visual aggression, and unstable complexity.

**Output requirements**  
Produce a front view, side view, three-quarter view, full-body view, neutral expression, and a concise Character Identity Sheet.

**Review criteria**  
The character should remain recognizable without relying on fine detail.

# 7. Weak prompt

> Design Lumi as a very detailed fashionable child with many accessories, complex patterns, different hairstyles, and a new outfit in every episode.

---

# 8. Diagnosis of the weak prompt

- Too many changing features weaken identity.
- Fine detail disappears on small screens.
- Different hairstyles and clothing complicate continuity.
- Accessories may distract from facial emotion and action.
- The prompt does not define silhouette, proportions, or permanent features.
- The design may become visually crowded for children under five.

---

# 9. Improved prompt

Design Lumi as a readable four-year-old character with a stable rounded silhouette, dark curly bob, one leaf-shaped hair clip, coral overalls, cream shirt, and yellow boots.

Keep the face simple: large brown eyes, clear eyebrows, small nose, and a flexible simple mouth. Use a consistent head-to-body relationship and compact childlike proportions. Avoid tiny patterns, jewelry, extra accessories, or hairstyle changes.

Allow only expression, pose, temporary dirt, wetness, or approved weather clothing to change. Preserve identity colors, face, hair, body proportions, and core clothing in every reference and shot.

# 12. Common mistakes

1. Designing only a beautiful close-up.
2. Using too many fine patterns.
3. Changing hair or clothing without story reason.
4. Relying on an accessory as the only identity cue.
5. Making every character the same shape and height.
6. Allowing visual style to become more detailed from shot to shot.
7. Failing to define forbidden visual changes.

---

# 13. Fifteen-minute practical exercise

Create a Character Identity Sheet for one main character.

1. Describe the silhouette in one sentence.
2. Choose three dominant identity colors.
3. Define face, hair, proportions, and clothing.
4. Select no more than two identity accessories.
5. List five permanent features.
6. List five temporary features.
7. List five forbidden changes.
8. Sketch or describe six reference views.
9. Perform a small-screen readability review.

---

# 14. Expected exercise result

A complete Character Identity Sheet and Character Reference Plan for one main character.

# 15. Short quiz and answers

### Question 1

What is silhouette?

### Answer

The outer shape that helps a character remain recognizable.
### Question 2

Why limit accessories?

### Answer

They increase clutter and continuity failures.
### Question 3

What belongs in permanent identity?

### Answer

Core face, hair, body proportions, colors, and main clothing.
### Question 4

Why test at thumbnail size?

### Answer

To check whether identity and action remain readable on a small screen.
### Question 5

Can weather clothing change?

### Answer

Yes, when approved and when identity colors and silhouette remain recognizable.
