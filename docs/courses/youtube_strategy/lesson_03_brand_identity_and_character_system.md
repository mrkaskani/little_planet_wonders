# LittlePlanetWonders Growth Course

## Lesson 3 of 16: Brand Identity and Character System

**Study time:** 15 minutes  
**Practice time:** 15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Define a recognizable identity for LittlePlanetWonders.
- Create three characters with different educational functions.
- Build a character bible for AI consistency.
- Assign voice, behavior, and visual rules.
- Decide when a character should be used or avoided.

---

## 2. Brand identity is more than a logo

For a children’s channel, identity is the repeated experience viewers recognize across episodes.

It includes:

- Character appearance and personality.
- Voice and pronunciation.
- Color and lighting.
- Music and sound style.
- Teaching rhythm.
- Story structure.
- Thumbnail composition.
- The emotional feeling of the channel.

A logo may help adults recognize the channel, but children are more likely to remember a character, phrase, song, or recurring adventure structure.

---

## 3. Recommended brand identity

### Brand purpose

> Help young children discover beginner English through safe, gentle adventures.

### Brand personality

- Curious
- Caring
- Optimistic
- Patient
- Playful
- Trustworthy

### Visual direction

- Rounded shapes rather than sharp, threatening forms.
- Clear silhouettes that remain readable on small screens.
- Warm natural environments with controlled accent colors.
- Moderate detail so objects remain easy to identify.
- Consistent scale between characters.
- Calm backgrounds during language instruction.

### Audio direction

- Clear voices with stable volume.
- Moderate speaking speed.
- Short pauses after questions.
- Music that supports rather than competes with speech.
- Limited sound effects with a clear purpose.

---

## 4. Why start with only three main characters

Too many characters create several problems:

- Children may not remember their names or roles.
- AI visual consistency becomes harder.
- Voice production becomes more expensive.
- Thumbnails become crowded.
- Stories require more explanation.
- The channel’s identity becomes unclear.

Begin with three permanent characters. Add new recurring characters only after the first group is recognizable.

---

## 5. Recommended character roles

### Character 1: The explorer

Purpose:

- Introduces the adventure.
- Asks genuine questions.
- Invites the child to participate.

Example:

**Luna the Fox**

- Personality: Curious, brave, kind.
- Educational role: Leads vocabulary and discovery episodes.
- Weakness: Sometimes overlooks clues.
- Child interaction: “Can you help me find it?”

### Character 2: The learner

Purpose:

- Makes understandable mistakes.
- Gives the child a chance to correct someone.
- Shows that mistakes are part of learning.

Example:

**Milo the Bear**

- Personality: Funny, gentle, enthusiastic.
- Educational role: Pronunciation, repetition, and guessing.
- Weakness: Confuses similar words.
- Child interaction: “Did I say it correctly?”

### Character 3: The real-life connector

Purpose:

- Connects fantasy adventures to everyday routines and feelings.
- Models sentences and positive behavior.

Example:

**Mia**

- Type: Human child character.
- Personality: Patient, observant, helpful.
- Educational role: Daily routines, emotions, classroom language.
- Child interaction: “What should we do next?”

Do not make one character always perfect and another always foolish. Each should have strengths, limitations, and moments of learning.

---

## 6. The character bible

A character bible is the single source of truth for generation, writing, voice, editing, and review.

### Identity fields

- Name
- Species or human identity
- Apparent age
- Height relative to other characters
- Body proportions
- Face shape
- Eye shape and color
- Hair or fur color
- Clothing
- Accessories
- Dominant and accent colors

### Personality fields

- Three primary traits
- One safe weakness
- Main motivation
- Typical emotional reactions
- What the character never does
- Relationship with other characters

### Educational fields

- Teaching function
- Types of episode led by the character
- Typical question style
- Vocabulary difficulty
- Common participation pattern
- Recurring phrase

### Voice fields

- Approximate age impression
- Pitch range
- Speaking speed
- Accent choice
- Emotional intensity
- Pronunciation standard
- Words or sounds requiring special review

---

## 7. AI consistency rules

For every recurring character, maintain:

1. A neutral front reference.
2. A neutral side reference.
3. A three-quarter reference.
4. Happy, sad, surprised, worried, and thinking expressions.
5. Standard clothing and color values.
6. Approved size relationships.
7. Approved indoor and outdoor lighting references.
8. Voice samples for neutral, excited, quiet, and questioning delivery.

Before approving a generated shot, compare:

- Fur, skin, or hair color.
- Eye color and shape.
- Clothing and accessories.
- Number of fingers, limbs, ears, and tails.
- Relative height.
- Voice identity.
- Personality and behavior.

## 8. Character continuity matrix

| Rule | Luna | Milo | Mia |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main function | Explorer | Learner | Daily-life connector |
| Primary color | Orange/rust | Warm brown | Teal or green clothing |
| Voice | Bright and curious | Warm and playful | Calm and clear |
| Typical question | “Can you find…?” | “Did I get it right?” | “What should we do next?” |
| Leads | Vocabulary adventures | Guessing and phonics | Routines and emotions |
| Must never do | Mock mistakes | Act unintelligent | Speak like an adult lecturer |

You may change the final names and colors, but keep each role distinct.

---

## 9. Character use rules

### Use a character when

- The character creates a clear question or problem.
- The character demonstrates the target word or action.
- The character gives the child a reason to respond.
- The character helps connect several episodes into a series.

### Avoid unnecessary characters when

- The screen is already visually complex.
- A clear object demonstration is enough.
- Dialogue would delay the learning activity.
- The character has no educational or story function.

Characters should support learning, not compete with it.

## 11. Character test

Before approving a character, ask:

1. Can a child recognize the silhouette without seeing the face?
2. Can the character be described in one sentence?
3. Does the character have a clear educational role?
4. Can the AI expert reproduce it consistently?
5. Can the voice remain consistent for many episodes?
6. Does the design remain readable in a small thumbnail?
7. Does the character behave safely and respectfully?

A character that fails several of these questions should be simplified before production.

---

## 12. Quiz

1. Why are three main characters enough at the beginning?
2. What is the purpose of the learner character?
3. Name four items that belong in a character bible.
4. Why should each character have a safe weakness?
5. When should a character be removed from a scene?

---

## 13. Quiz answers

1. Three characters are easier for children to remember and easier for the team to produce consistently.
2. To make understandable mistakes and create opportunities for children to answer or correct.
3. Any four of: appearance, colors, proportions, personality, role, voice, behavior rules, expressions, clothing, recurring phrase.
4. It makes the character relatable and creates natural learning situations.
5. When it has no educational or narrative purpose or makes the scene visually confusing.
