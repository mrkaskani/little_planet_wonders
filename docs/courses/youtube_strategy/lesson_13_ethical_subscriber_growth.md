# LittlePlanetWonders Growth Course

## Lesson 13 of 16: Ethical Subscriber Growth

**Study time:** 15 minutes  
**Practice time:** 15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Build subscribers through useful recurring content.
- Adapt growth tactics to made-for-kids feature restrictions.
- Use series, playlists, channel organization, and adult trust.
- Design safe calls to action.
- Grow parent, teacher, school, and partner relationships.
- Measure subscriber quality instead of chasing an empty number.

---

## 2. What a valuable subscriber means

A useful subscriber is someone whose household, classroom, or organization genuinely wants more LittlePlanetWonders content.

A subscriber is valuable when they:

- Watch more than one episode
- Recognize a recurring series
- Return for future lessons
- Share the channel appropriately
- Use a worksheet or classroom resource
- Recommend the channel to another adult

Do not buy subscribers or use exchange groups. Artificial subscribers may not watch, learn, return, or support the channel.

---

## 3. Made-for-kids constraints

LittlePlanetWonders is primarily directed to young children, so accurate audience designation is essential.

Current official YouTube guidance states that made-for-kids content may lose or restrict features such as:

- Comments
- Notifications
- Cards
- End screens
- Personalized advertising
- Save to playlist or Watch Later for viewers
- Posts on a channel set as made for kids

The exact list can change. Verify current YouTube Help documentation.

The subscribe function may still exist, but the notification bell may not. Therefore, the channel must create return behavior through recognition and adult habits rather than notifications.

---

## 4. The ethical subscriber-growth system

### Step 1: Deliver a complete learning result

Do not ask for a subscription before the child or adult receives value.

### Step 2: Show a recognizable continuation

> “Next time, Luna visits the ocean.”

### Step 3: Use a gentle spoken CTA

> “Parents and teachers can follow LittlePlanetWonders for a new English adventure every week.”

### Step 4: Maintain a predictable schedule

Example:

> New complete lesson every Friday, with two practice Shorts during the week.

### Step 5: Create series paths

- Luna’s Word Adventures
- Milo’s Sound Safari
- Mia’s Little Life Stories
- Sing and Repeat

### Step 6: Build adult trust outside the watch page

- Worksheets
- Teacher notes
- Parent-facing social posts
- School partnerships
- Email for adults, when managed lawfully

---

## 5. Series-based growth

A viewer should understand what to watch next.

### Weak channel library

- Random color song
- Advanced grammar
- Unrelated science animation
- One phonics clip

### Strong channel library

- Farm Animals Word Adventure
- Ocean Animals Word Adventure
- Forest Animals Word Adventure
- Animal Sounds Practice Short
- Animal Vocabulary Compilation

Series create recognition and make subscription value obvious.

## 6. Channel-home organization

Create clear sections:

1. Start Learning English
2. Luna’s Word Adventures
3. Milo’s Sound Safari
4. Mia’s Little Life Stories
5. Songs and Chants
6. Ages 2–3
7. Ages 3–5
8. Ages 5–6

Even when some viewer playlist features are limited, the channel can still organize its own public content into useful viewing paths, subject to current platform features.

### Channel trailer for adults

Create a short parent-and-teacher-facing trailer that explains:

- Target age
- Learning method
- Teacher review
- Publishing schedule
- Main series
- Calm and child-safe approach

---

## 7. Safe calls to action

### End-of-video CTA

> “Today we learned cow, sheep, horse, pig, and chicken. Parents and teachers can follow LittlePlanetWonders for our next English adventure.”

### Description CTA

> Subscribe to LittlePlanetWonders for one new teacher-reviewed preschool English lesson each week.

### Social CTA

> Save this activity for your next preschool English practice, and find the complete lesson on LittlePlanetWonders.

### Avoid

- “Subscribe or Luna will be sad.”
- “Only good children subscribe.”
- Fake countdowns
- Rewards that require subscription
- Repeated pressure throughout the episode
- Asking children to comment personal information

A trusted character should not emotionally manipulate a child.

---

## 8. Adult lead magnets

A lead magnet is a free resource offered to an adult audience.

Examples:

- Farm-animal matching worksheet
- Five-day vocabulary practice sheet
- Preschool phonics checklist
- Parent viewing guide
- Teacher lesson plan
- Printable emotion cards

### Privacy rule

Collect adult contact information only through a system designed for lawful adult use. Do not ask children to submit names, email addresses, photos, school details, or location information.

COPPA and other child-privacy laws can apply when online services collect personal information from children. Legal requirements depend on audience, service design, location, and data practices.

---

## 9. Collaboration system

Appropriate collaborators:

- Preschool teachers
- ESL teachers
- Speech and language educators
- Children’s librarians
- Family-safe educational creators
- Schools and tutoring centers
- Children’s book authors or publishers

### Collaboration examples

- Teacher reviews an episode structure
- Joint vocabulary worksheet
- Guest pronunciation activity
- Classroom feedback pilot
- Library English-story event
- Cross-reference between two complementary adult audiences

Evaluate collaborators for child safety, educational quality, rights, and brand fit.

---

## 10. Partnership ladder

### Level 1: Feedback

Ask five teachers to test a lesson.

### Level 2: Resource exchange

Provide a worksheet and receive structured classroom feedback.

### Level 3: Co-created activity

Produce a teacher guide or short adult-facing demonstration.

### Level 4: Institutional partnership

Work with a school, library, tutoring center, or educational app.

Relationships should grow from useful work rather than immediate promotional demands.

---

## 11. Subscriber metrics

Track:

- Subscribers gained per video
- Subscribers gained per 100 views
- Long videos versus Shorts subscriber contribution
- Returning viewers
- Casual and regular viewers when available
- Views per viewer
- Number of viewers watching more than one related video
- External sources producing subscribers

### Example

Video A:

- 1,000 views
- 5 subscribers
- 0.5 subscribers per 100 views

Video B:

- 300 views
- 6 subscribers
- 2 subscribers per 100 views

Video B reaches fewer people but converts a more relevant audience.

---

## 12. Four-week subscriber experiment

### Week 1: Series promise

End with a preview of the next Luna adventure.

### Week 2: Adult CTA

Add one calm parent-and-teacher subscription sentence.

### Week 3: Resource value

Publish a related worksheet through adult-facing channels.

### Week 4: Collaboration

Ask a teacher community for structured feedback.

Compare:

- Subscribers per 100 views
- Returning viewers
- External traffic
- Worksheet interest
- Qualitative teacher feedback

Do not change all four tactics in the same video.

---

## 13. Stop, improve, or scale

### Stop

- A CTA pressures or manipulates children.
- A partnership creates safety or trust concerns.
- An external resource collects inappropriate data.
- Subscriber growth comes from unrelated viral content.

### Improve

- Viewers watch but do not understand the series promise.
- Shorts gain subscribers who never watch another video.
- Adult resources are useful but difficult to access.
- Channel sections are confusing.

### Scale

- A series repeatedly gains subscribers and returning viewers.
- Teachers voluntarily share a resource.
- A collaboration produces relevant watch behavior.
- Adults begin requesting related lessons.

## 16. Quiz answers

1. They genuinely watch, return, use, or recommend the content.
2. Made-for-kids content can have those features restricted or unavailable.
3. The viewer should receive a complete educational value first.
4. They build parent and teacher trust and create a relationship outside child-directed engagement features.
5. Subscribers gained per 100 views.
