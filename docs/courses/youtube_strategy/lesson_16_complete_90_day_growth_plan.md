# LittlePlanetWonders Growth Course

## Lesson 16 of 16: Complete 90-Day Growth Plan

**Study time:** 15 minutes  
**Practice time:** 15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Combine all previous lessons into one operating plan.
- Organize 13 weeks of long videos, Shorts, and social outputs.
- Assign work to two teachers and one AI expert.
- Use weekly experiments and stage gates.
- Protect the $100 monthly budget.
- Finish 90 days with evidence for the next strategy.

---

## 2. Starting position

| Item | Current state |
|---|---|
| Channel | LittlePlanetWonders |
| Child audience | Ages 2–6; primary focus ages 3–5 |
| Adult decision-makers | Parents, teachers, schools, caregivers |
| Subscribers | 2 |
| Average views | About 10 |
| Weekly capacity | 1 long video and 2 Shorts |
| Monthly budget | About $100 |
| Team | 2 teachers and 1 AI expert |
| Style | AI-created animal and human characters |
| Priority | Views and genuine subscribers |

The channel is in the discovery stage. The purpose of the next 90 days is to discover repeatable audience value.

---

## 3. Ninety-day outcomes

These are working targets, not promises.

### Production

- 13 long videos
- 26 Shorts
- 3 recognizable series
- 1 consistent character bible
- 1 reusable worksheet system
- 1 weekly analytics dashboard

### Learning

Identify:

- Strongest topic cluster
- Strongest lead character
- Strongest opening
- Best working length
- Most useful Short format
- Best adult-facing resource
- Most valuable traffic source

### Audience

- Move toward the first 50–150 genuine subscribers as an aspirational range
- Create returning-viewer behavior
- Produce at least two videos that perform clearly above the channel baseline
- Build initial parent and teacher trust

### Partnership

- Receive feedback from at least five educators
- Create one sponsor-safe sample
- Prepare a one-page media kit
- Contact a small number of suitable partners only after readiness review

## 5. Team operating model

### Teacher 1: Learning designer

- Choose objective
- Define target vocabulary
- Design participation
- Create worksheet activity

### Teacher 2: Language and safety reviewer

- Verify pronunciation and grammar
- Review age suitability
- Approve final script and video
- Check captions and claims

### AI expert: Production lead

- Maintain character consistency
- Generate and edit assets
- Mix audio
- Create Shorts and social adaptations
- Manage thumbnails and files
- Maintain rights and version records

### Shared weekly decision

Choose:

- One thing to repeat
- One thing to improve
- One experiment

## 6. Monthly budget

| Category | Monthly range |
|---|---:|
| Video and visual generation | $35–50 |
| Voice, music, and sound | $25–35 |
| Storage, licensed assets, or design | $10–20 |
| Experiment reserve | $10–20 |

Do not spend most of the budget on ads during the discovery stage.

Track cost per:

- Long video
- Short
- Approved minute
- Reusable asset

Pause a format when it repeatedly costs too much relative to learning and performance value.

## 7. Days 1–30: Foundation and testing

### Week 1: Farm Animals

**Long video:** Help Luna Find Five Farm Animals  
**Shorts:** Which animal says moo? / Find the sheep  
**Social:** Farm vocabulary Reel, three pins, teacher post  
**Metric:** First-30-second retention  
**Experiment:** Mystery opening  
**Partnership action:** Ask one preschool teacher for feedback  
**Sponsor action:** None  
**Expected learning:** Whether Luna’s search format is understandable

### Week 2: Colors in the Magic Garden

**Long video:** Help Luna Bring Back Five Colors  
**Shorts:** Find something red / What color is the flower?  
**Social:** Color carousel and printable card  
**Metric:** CTR and average percentage viewed  
**Experiment:** Question thumbnail versus character portrait  
**Partnership action:** Share a free color activity with one teacher community  
**Sponsor action:** None  
**Expected learning:** Whether visual color mysteries improve appeal

### Week 3: Letter B Adventure

**Long video:** Milo’s Sound Safari: Letter B  
**Shorts:** What begins with B? / B or S?  
**Social:** Phonics tip Reel and B-word pin set  
**Metric:** Retention during sound practice  
**Experiment:** Sound-first opening  
**Partnership action:** Ask an ESL teacher to review pronunciation pacing  
**Sponsor action:** Begin a list of suitable educational brands  
**Expected learning:** Whether phonics fits the current audience

### Week 4: How Does Mia Feel?

**Long video:** Mia’s Little Story: Happy, Sad, Worried, Excited  
**Shorts:** Happy or sad? / How does Mia feel?  
**Social:** Emotion cards and parent activity  
**Metric:** Average view duration and subscriber gain  
**Experiment:** Story opening versus direct vocabulary opening  
**Partnership action:** Collect structured feedback from two educators  
**Sponsor action:** None  
**Expected learning:** Compare story and vocabulary formats

### Day-30 review

Select:

- Strongest pillar
- Strongest character
- Strongest opening
- One weak format to pause or redesign

Do not scale before this review.

## 10. Weekly analytics scorecard

| Area | Metric | Weekly question |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | Impressions and feed appearances | Did distribution opportunities grow? |
| Appeal | CTR and chose-to-view behavior | Did suitable viewers select the content? |
| Engagement | AVD, APV, early retention | Did the lesson hold attention? |
| Continuation | Returning viewers, views per viewer | Did viewers come back or continue? |
| Conversion | Subscribers per 100 views | Did the video build relevant audience? |
| Adult value | Saves, downloads, teacher feedback | Did adults find it useful? |
| Operations | Cost and production time | Can the team sustain it? |
| Trust | Errors, safety issues, corrections | Did quality remain high? |

## 11. Stage gates

### Gate 1: After four weeks

Continue only formats that are:

- Educationally clear
- Visually stable
- Affordable
- Showing some audience evidence

### Gate 2: After eight weeks

Scale only patterns that:

- Succeed more than once
- Produce useful subscribers or returning viewers
- Can become a meaningful series

### Gate 3: After thirteen weeks

Choose the next quarter based on:

- Top two pillars
- Top two topic clusters
- Best lead character
- Best opening
- Best long-video length
- Best Short format
- Best adult resource
- Sustainable budget

---

## 12. Risk controls

### AI mass-production risk

Every episode must have a distinct objective, story, teaching sequence, and teacher contribution.

### Character inconsistency

Use reference images, voice references, and approval checks.

### Copyright risk

Maintain a rights log for music, sound, fonts, images, voices, and AI tools.

### Child-privacy risk

Do not collect personal information from children. Keep newsletters and resources adult-focused.

### Sponsorship risk

Use legal review, clear disclosure, safe categories, and editorial independence.

### Overproduction risk

Protect the one-long-plus-two-Shorts rhythm. Pause secondary social outputs before reducing core lesson quality.

- Seasonal effects

- Two primary pillars
- Two main characters
- One long-video rhythm
- Two Short formats
- Two adult social platforms
