# LittlePlanetWonders Growth Course

## Lesson 15 of 16: Sponsorships and Educational Partnerships

**Study time:** 15 minutes  
**Practice time:** 15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Decide when LittlePlanetWonders is ready for sponsor outreach.
- Identify suitable and unsuitable sponsor categories.
- Build a one-page media kit.
- Offer realistic sponsorship packages.
- Write outreach and follow-up messages.
- Protect children’s trust and editorial independence.
- Report campaign performance professionally.

---

## 2. Sponsorship is not the first milestone

With two subscribers and about ten average views, LittlePlanetWonders should first build proof of quality.

Recommended readiness evidence:

- 12–15 strong long videos
- Three recognizable series
- Stable character and audio quality
- Consistent weekly publishing
- Teacher-review process
- Clear audience positioning
- Analytics dashboard
- At least one parent or teacher resource
- A sponsor-safe sample campaign

A small channel can still form partnerships, but the offer should focus on educational quality, custom assets, teacher expertise, and adult-facing resources rather than promising large reach.

---

## 3. Appropriate sponsor categories

Potential categories:

- Educational apps
- Children’s book publishers
- Learning-toy companies
- School-supply brands
- Family-safe technology companies
- Language-learning platforms
- Tutoring services
- Schools and educational institutions
- Libraries and learning centers
- Printable-resource companies
- Child-safe audio or reading tools

Every partner must be reviewed for:

- Child safety
- Product quality
- Privacy practices
- Educational relevance
- Legal suitability
- Reputation
- Advertising methods

---

## 4. Excluded or high-risk categories

Do not work with:

- Age-restricted products
- Gambling
- Alcohol, nicotine, or recreational drugs
- Unsafe supplements or health claims
- Weapons
- Adult entertainment
- Predatory data-collection services
- Misleading learning products
- Products that pressure children to purchase
- Brands with unsafe child-labor, privacy, or exploitation concerns

Be cautious with products involving:

- In-app purchases
- Behavioral tracking
- Artificial scarcity
- Loot-box-style mechanics
- Unverified educational or medical claims

---

## 5. Safer sponsorship locations

The safest early sponsorship options do not require a trusted character to sell directly to children.

Use:

- Parent-facing descriptions
- Teacher worksheets
- Adult newsletters
- Website resource pages
- Parent-focused Instagram posts
- Teacher training clips
- School partnership materials
- Sponsor-supported free printables

Example:

> “This teacher guide was supported by [Partner]. The lesson objective and teaching decisions remained under LittlePlanetWonders editorial control.”

---

## 6. Disclosure and legal review

Current YouTube guidance requires creators to identify paid product placements, sponsorships, endorsements, or other relevant commercial relationships using the paid-promotion setting.

YouTube also states that creators and brands are responsible for local legal disclosure obligations. Its official guidance warns that, in some UK and EU contexts, videos classified as children’s programmes may face restrictions or prohibitions on sponsorships or paid product placements.

Because LittlePlanetWonders may reach audiences in Germany, the EU, the UK, and other countries:

- Obtain jurisdiction-specific legal advice before integrating sponsorship into child-directed episodes.
- Use clear adult and child-understandable disclosures where required.
- Do not assume YouTube’s automatic disclosure is the only legal requirement.
- Record payment, free products, affiliate relationships, and editorial influence.

This lesson is a planning framework, not legal advice.

---

## 7. What sponsors evaluate

Sponsors may examine:

- Audience fit
- Countries and languages
- Child age positioning
- Views and watch time
- Returning viewers
- Subscriber growth
- Teacher and parent engagement
- Brand safety
- Production quality
- Publishing reliability
- Resource downloads
- Campaign reporting ability
- Usage rights
- Exclusivity requirements

A sponsor may value 100 highly relevant teachers more than thousands of unrelated viewers.

### Brand summary

> Teacher-reviewed English adventures for beginner children aged 3–5, using gentle AI-created animal and human characters.

### Audience

- Primary child age
- Parents and teachers as decision-makers
- Main countries when reliable data exists
- Language-learning level

### Content system

- One long lesson weekly
- Two Shorts weekly
- Four recurring content pillars

### Trust evidence

- Two-teacher review
- Child-safe design
- Original characters
- Rights and compliance process

### Performance

- Monthly views
- Average view duration
- Returning viewers
- Subscribers
- Top videos
- Worksheet downloads
- Social reach

### Partnership options

- Adult-facing resource sponsorship
- Teacher guide
- Branded educational sample
- School or library collaboration

### Contact

- Adult business email
- Website or professional profile

Do not invent or inflate statistics.

## 9. Three starting sponsorship packages

Prices are experimental planning ranges, not guaranteed market rates.

### Package 1: Teacher Resource Pilot — $75–150

Includes:

- One co-branded parent or teacher worksheet
- One adult-facing social post
- Partner acknowledgement
- Basic campaign report

Best for:

- Small educational brands
- Local schools
- Independent publishers

### Package 2: Educational Partner — $150–300

Includes:

- Two teacher or parent resources
- Two adult-facing social posts
- One short adult-facing demonstration
- Campaign report

Best for:

- Learning apps
- Tutoring services
- Educational-resource companies

### Package 3: Original Learning Asset — $300–600

Includes:

- One custom educational mini-video for adult-facing or partner-owned channels
- One worksheet
- Defined usage rights
- One revision round
- Campaign report

Best for:

- Publishers
- Family-safe technology companies
- Educational institutions

Do not include unlimited revisions, permanent global usage, or exclusivity without separate pricing.

## 10. Professional sponsor outreach email

### Subject

Partnership idea: Teacher-reviewed preschool English resources

### Message

Hello [Name],

I’m contacting you from LittlePlanetWonders, a children’s English-education channel creating gentle, teacher-reviewed animated lessons for beginner learners aged 3–5.

Our team includes two teachers and an AI production specialist. We are building recurring vocabulary, phonics, and story-based series for children, supported by parent and teacher resources.

We believe [Brand] may be a strong fit because [specific reason connected to the brand’s educational value]. We would like to explore a small pilot focused on an adult-facing worksheet, teacher resource, or educational content sample rather than direct purchase messaging to children.

The pilot could include:

- [Deliverable 1]
- [Deliverable 2]
- [Campaign reporting]

Editorial decisions, language accuracy, child safety, and disclosure would remain clearly controlled and documented.

I would be happy to share our one-page media kit and a sample concept tailored to [Brand].

[Adult representative name]  
LittlePlanetWonders  
[Business contact information]

## 11. Follow-up email

### Subject

Following up: LittlePlanetWonders educational pilot

### Message

Hello [Name],

I’m following up on my message about a possible educational partnership between [Brand] and LittlePlanetWonders.

A simple pilot could begin with one parent- or teacher-facing resource connected to a preschool English topic such as animals, routines, phonics, or emotions. This would allow both teams to evaluate educational fit and audience response before considering a larger campaign.

Please let me know whether this type of collaboration is relevant to your current plans. If not, I will not continue following up.

Best regards,

[Adult representative name]  
LittlePlanetWonders

Send one thoughtful follow-up. Do not repeatedly contact a brand that does not respond.

## 12. Negotiation checklist

Before agreeing, document:

- Deliverables
- Publication dates
- Approval process
- Number of revisions
- Payment amount and schedule
- Taxes and invoicing
- Usage rights
- Usage period
- Countries and platforms
- Paid advertising rights
- Editing rights
- Exclusivity
- Competitor restrictions
- Disclosure language
- Product claims
- Child-safety responsibilities
- Data collection
- Reporting metrics
- Cancellation terms
- Ownership of characters and source assets

Never transfer permanent ownership of core characters without deliberate legal and business review.

---

## 13. Editorial-independence rules

LittlePlanetWonders must retain control over:

- Learning objective
- Vocabulary and pronunciation
- Character behavior
- Safety
- Story outcome
- Teacher approval
- Honest product description
- Whether the product is included at all

Reject requests to:

- Hide sponsorship
- Make false claims
- Pressure children
- Create fear of missing out
- Present an advertisement as an independent lesson
- Collect child data
- Remove necessary safety information

---

Include:

### Campaign summary

- Partner
- Dates
- Objective
- Deliverables

### Distribution

- Platforms
- Publication links or identifiers
- Impressions or reach
- Views

### Engagement

- Watch time
- Average view duration
- Completion behavior
- Saves and shares

### Audience value

- Worksheet downloads
- Teacher inquiries
- Parent feedback
- Relevant traffic sources

### Channel value

- Subscribers gained
- Returning viewers
- Related-video viewing

### Learning and recommendations

- What worked
- What underperformed
- What should change
- Suggested next campaign

Protect audience privacy and do not provide personal information that the sponsor is not lawfully entitled to receive.

2. Which sponsorship locations are safer for a child-directed brand?
3. What must be disclosed to YouTube?
4. Why must local law be checked separately?
5. What should never be transferred casually in a contract?

---

## 17. Quiz answers

1. The channel first needs evidence of quality, consistency, trust, and audience fit.
2. Parent-facing descriptions, teacher resources, newsletters, websites, and adult social posts.
3. Paid product placements, sponsorships, endorsements, and relevant commercial relationships through the platform’s paid-promotion setting.
4. Platform disclosure does not replace jurisdiction-specific advertising and children’s-media requirements.
5. Ownership of core characters, source assets, or broad permanent usage rights.
