**Study time:** 15 minutes

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Describe the current position of LittlePlanetWonders.
- Separate strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and risks.
- Choose realistic goals for the first 90 days.
- Define the minimum production system needed for weekly publishing.
- Avoid judging a new channel using large-channel expectations.

---

## 3. What a channel audit means

A channel audit is a structured examination of:

1. The audience you serve.
2. The educational value you provide.
3. The content you can produce consistently.
4. The resources available to you.
5. The risks that could reduce trust or platform suitability.
6. The data you need before scaling.

A useful audit does not only identify problems. It helps you decide what to do next.

---

## 4. LittlePlanetWonders SWOT audit

### Strengths

- Two teachers can review vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, pacing, and age suitability.
- An AI expert can build a repeatable visual-production pipeline.
- The channel has a clear educational purpose.
- Animal and human characters can support storytelling and recurring series.
- One long video plus two Shorts per week is a sustainable starting rhythm.

### Weaknesses

- The channel has almost no audience data yet.
- Ages 2–6 is too broad for one learning level.
- AI-generated characters may change appearance between episodes.
- A $100 monthly budget limits advertising, premium voices, and complex animation.
- The brand does not yet have proven recurring characters or series.

### Opportunities

- Parents and teachers continually search for beginner vocabulary, phonics, routines, emotions, and listening practice.
- One long lesson can be repurposed into Shorts, Reels, worksheets, and Pinterest posts.
- Teacher review can become a major trust signal.
- A calm, educational style can differentiate the channel from chaotic children’s content.
- Recurring stories can combine language learning with social and emotional education.

### Risks

- Nearly identical AI-generated videos may feel mass-produced.
- Incorrect pronunciation or visual errors can damage educational trust.
- Broad topic selection can make the channel difficult to understand.
- Copyright or licensing mistakes can affect music, sound effects, voices, and visual assets.
- Content directed at young children requires careful audience settings, privacy awareness, and child-safe design.

## 5. The first-stage growth model

Do not think:

> “How can we go viral?”

Think:

> “How can we repeatedly produce videos that a specific child enjoys and a parent or teacher trusts?”

Your first growth loop is:

1. Choose one clear learning objective.
2. Build one understandable story or activity around it.
3. Package it with an honest title and thumbnail.
4. Publish it consistently.
5. Measure where viewers enter, continue, and leave.
6. Produce a better sequel based on evidence.

This creates sustainable improvement instead of random experimentation.

---

## 6. Recommended 90-day goals

These are working targets, not guarantees.

### Production goals

- Publish 12–13 long videos.
- Publish 24–26 Shorts.
- Establish three recognizable series.
- Use no more than three permanent main characters at first.
- Create one reusable worksheet format.

### Learning goals

Discover:

- Which topic earns the most impressions.
- Which thumbnail style earns more clicks.
- Which opening retains viewers best.
- Which character receives the strongest response.
- Which video length produces the best completion behavior.
- Which videos generate subscribers rather than only temporary views.

### Audience goals

A reasonable early target is to move from 2 subscribers toward the first 50–150 genuine subscribers. Results may be lower or higher, so your main success measure should be the quality of what you learn.

## 7. Minimum production system

### Teacher 1: Learning designer

Responsibilities:

- Select the learning objective.
- Choose age-appropriate vocabulary.
- Define repetition and participation moments.
- Check that the lesson is educationally meaningful.

### Teacher 2: Language and safety reviewer

Responsibilities:

- Review grammar and pronunciation.
- Check sentence difficulty.
- Identify confusing or unsafe scenes.
- Approve the final lesson before publication.

### AI expert: Production lead

Responsibilities:

- Maintain character consistency.
- Generate visual assets and animation.
- Produce and synchronize voices.
- Edit long videos and Shorts.
- Create thumbnails and production templates.
- Maintain asset and licensing records.

## 8. Recommended $100 monthly budget

| Use | Suggested amount |
|---|---:|
| Voice, music, or sound tools | $25–35 |
| Visual or video generation | $35–50 |
| Licensed assets or storage | $10–20 |
| Experiment reserve | $10–20 |

Do not spend most of the budget on advertisements yet. With only a few videos, advertising may produce traffic without teaching you whether the content naturally satisfies viewers.

---

## 9. Stop, improve, or scale rules

### Stop temporarily when

- A format repeatedly performs worse than the channel baseline.
- Production cost is too high for the learning value.
- Children cannot understand the objective without adult explanation.
- The AI output requires excessive correction.

### Improve when

- The topic receives impressions but viewers do not click.
- Viewers click but leave during the opening.
- Retention is good but few viewers watch another episode.
- Parents or teachers understand the lesson, but the child participation is weak.

### Scale when

- A topic performs strongly more than once.
- A recurring character improves retention or repeat viewing.
- A format can be produced reliably within the weekly schedule.
- A video attracts both views and subscribers.

## 11. Quiz

1. Why should the first 90 days focus on learning rather than revenue?
2. What is the main risk of targeting ages 2–6 with one lesson level?
3. What should the two teachers review?
4. Why should advertising receive little or no budget initially?
5. When should a content format be scaled?

---

## 12. Quiz answers

1. Because the channel lacks enough audience and content data to know which formats work reliably.
2. Children in that range have very different attention, language, and cognitive abilities.
3. Learning objectives, vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, pacing, age suitability, safety, and final quality.
4. Paid traffic can hide whether the content naturally earns attention and satisfaction.
5. When it performs strongly more than once and can be produced sustainably.
