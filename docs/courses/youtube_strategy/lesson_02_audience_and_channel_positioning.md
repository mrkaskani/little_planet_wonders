# LittlePlanetWonders Growth Course

## Lesson 2 of 16: Audience and Channel Positioning

**Study time:** 15 minutes  
**Practice time:** 15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will define:

- The primary child audience.
- The secondary learning levels.
- The adult decision-maker audience.
- The educational and emotional promises.
- A clear positioning statement for LittlePlanetWonders.

---

## 2. Why audience positioning matters

A channel cannot teach every child aged 2–6 using the same vocabulary, pacing, story structure, and video length.

### A two-year-old commonly needs

- Single words and very short phrases.
- Concrete objects and actions.
- Slow repetition.
- Simple movement and songs.
- Short viewing experiences.

### A five- or six-year-old can often handle

- Short stories.
- Questions and answers.
- Phonics activities.
- Complete beginner sentences.
- Simple sequencing and problem-solving.

Therefore, LittlePlanetWonders should have one primary age group and clearly separated secondary levels.

---

## 3. Recommended primary audience

> **Children aged 3–5 who are beginning to learn English.**

This group is suitable because they can:

- Follow a short animated story.
- Recognize recurring characters.
- Repeat words and short sentences.
- Answer simple questions.
- Learn through songs, stories, movement, and visual demonstrations.

---

## 4. The three-level audience system

### Level 1: Little Learners

**Age:** 2–3  
**English level:** First exposure

Suitable content:

- Animals
- Colors
- Numbers 1–5
- Body parts
- Greetings
- Basic actions

Example language:

> “Cat.”  
> “A red ball.”  
> “Clap your hands.”

Recommended video length: **2–4 minutes**

### Level 2: Wonder Explorers

**Age:** 3–5  
**English level:** Beginner

Suitable content:

- Themed vocabulary
- Daily routines
- Emotions
- Short stories
- Simple phonics
- Questions and answers

Example language:

> “Where is the cat?”  
> “The cat is under the table.”

Recommended video length: **4–6 minutes**

This should be your main level during the first 90 days.

### Level 3: Ready for School

**Age:** 5–6  
**English level:** Beginner to early elementary

Suitable content:

- Phonics
- Listening comprehension
- Complete beginner sentences
- Story sequencing
- Classroom vocabulary
- Simple grammar patterns

Example language:

> “First, Mia puts on her shoes. Then she goes to school.”

Recommended video length: **5–8 minutes**

---

## 5. The two-audience system

LittlePlanetWonders has two different audiences.

### Child audience

The child needs:

- Friendly characters.
- Clear speech.
- Visual examples.
- Repetition with variation.
- Time to answer questions.
- Safe and understandable stories.

### Adult audience

Parents, teachers, schools, and caregivers need evidence that the content is:

- Educational.
- Safe.
- Age-appropriate.
- Professionally reviewed.
- Calm rather than overstimulating.
- Useful at home or in a classroom.

The child-facing video should create joy and learning. Adult-facing descriptions and social posts should explain the educational value and how to use the content.

---

## 6. Channel positioning

A positioning statement explains:

1. Who the channel serves.
2. What it helps them accomplish.
3. How the experience feels.
4. Why adults should trust it.

### Recommended positioning statement

> **LittlePlanetWonders creates gentle, teacher-reviewed English adventures for children aged 3–5, using friendly animal and human characters to teach useful words, sounds, routines, and social skills.**

### Short brand line

> **Small adventures. Big English discoveries.**

---

## 7. Educational promise

Every episode should include:

1. One clear learning objective.
2. A limited number of new words or expressions.
3. Correct pronunciation.
4. Meaningful repetition.
5. At least one participation moment.
6. A short recap.

### Strong objective

> By the end of this episode, children can recognize and say five farm-animal names.

### Weak objective

> Children will learn English.

The strong objective can be reviewed and measured. The weak objective is too broad.

---

## 8. Emotional promise

LittlePlanetWonders should feel:

- Warm
- Curious
- Gentle
- Safe
- Playful
- Encouraging

It should not feel:

- Chaotic
- Frightening
- Aggressive
- Commercial
- Random
- Exhausting

The emotional promise should influence voices, music, editing speed, color, dialogue, and character behavior.

---

## 9. Example episode positioning

### Weak idea

> Animals for Kids

Problems:

- Too broad.
- No story.
- No specific age or learning objective.
- No participation promise.

### Stronger idea

> Help Luna Find the Five Missing Farm Animals

Learning objective:

- Cow
- Sheep
- Horse
- Pig
- Chicken

Story problem:

> The animals are hiding around the farm, and Luna needs help finding them.

Participation:

> “Can you find the sheep?”

Adult value:

> Children practise listening, animal vocabulary, and simple location words.

## 11. Quiz

1. Why should ages 2–6 not receive exactly the same lesson structure?
2. What is the primary age group recommended for the first 90 days?
3. Who are the adult decision-makers?
4. What is the difference between a topic and an educational objective?
5. Name three emotions the brand should consistently communicate.

---

## 12. Quiz answers

1. Because children in this range have different language abilities, attention spans, and cognitive needs.
2. Ages 3–5.
3. Parents, teachers, schools, and caregivers.
4. A topic describes the subject; an objective describes exactly what the child should be able to understand or do.
5. Any three of: warm, curious, gentle, safe, playful, encouraging.
