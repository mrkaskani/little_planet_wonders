# LittlePlanetWonders Growth Course

## Lesson 8 of 16: Titles and Child-Safe Thumbnails

**Study time:** 15 minutes  
**Practice time:** 15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Write titles that communicate age, topic, and experience.
- Design honest, readable, child-safe thumbnails.
- Separate child appeal from parent and teacher search intent.
- Create three packaging options for every episode.
- Diagnose whether a weak result comes from topic, title, or thumbnail.

---

## 2. Packaging has two jobs

A title and thumbnail must:

1. Help the right adult or viewer understand the educational topic.
2. Make the child-facing experience visually appealing without deception.

The packaging should accurately promise what the video delivers.

Official YouTube policies prohibit harmful or deceptive thumbnail practices and misleading metadata. Platform rules may change, so verify the current official thumbnail, spam, and child-safety policies.

---

## 3. The title formula

A useful title combines two or three elements:

- Learning outcome
- Theme or story
- Character or series
- Age or level when helpful

### Formula A: Story plus learning

> Help Luna Find Five Farm Animals | English for Kids

### Formula B: Learning question

> Can You Name These Five Farm Animals?

### Formula C: Series plus topic

> Luna’s Word Adventure: Farm Animals

Do not place every keyword variation into one long title.

---

## 4. Child-facing and adult-facing meaning

### Child-facing meaning

The child sees:

- A familiar character.
- An emotion.
- A mystery, object, or challenge.

### Adult-facing meaning

The adult reads:

- English learning.
- Topic.
- Appropriate difficulty.
- Educational format.

Example:

> **Help Luna Find Five Farm Animals | Beginner English for Kids**

The story attracts attention, while the second part explains educational value.

---

## 5. Title rules

Use:

- Clear everyday language.
- The actual learning subject.
- Numbers only when they matter.
- Character or series names after they become recognizable.
- Natural search phrases.

Avoid:

- “You won’t believe…”
- False emergencies.
- Unsupported claims such as “Become fluent instantly.”
- Excessive capital letters.
- Repeated keywords.
- Topics that are not actually taught.

---

## 6. Thumbnail composition

A strong LittlePlanetWonders thumbnail should usually contain:

- One primary character.
- One clear emotion.
- One learning subject or mystery.
- A simple background.
- Strong separation between character and object.
- Little or no text.

### Recommended hierarchy

1. Character face or action
2. Target object or clue
3. Small supporting element
4. Optional short text

### Example

For a farm-animal video:

- Luna on the left, looking curious.
- A cow partly hidden behind a red barn.
- Two other small animal clues.
- Optional text: “WHO IS HIDING?”

---

## 7. Thumbnail text

For preschool-oriented thumbnails:

- Use 0–4 words.
- Use large, readable letters.
- Do not repeat the full title.
- Prefer a question or key word.
- Ensure the image still works without text.

Possible text:

- FIND THEM!
- WHICH ANIMAL?
- FIVE COLORS
- LETTER B
- HOW DOES MIA FEEL?

Adults may read the title, while children may respond mainly to the image.

---

## 8. Safe emotional expression

Useful expressions:

- Curious
- Happy
- Gently surprised
- Thinking
- Proud
- Concerned without panic

Avoid:

- Terrified faces.
- Implied injury.
- Threatening villains.
- Fake crying used only to gain clicks.
- Disturbing transformations.
- Images unrelated to the educational content.

The thumbnail should create curiosity without exploiting fear.

---

## 9. Three-option packaging system

For every long video, create three title and thumbnail directions before choosing one.

### Episode: Farm Animals

#### Option 1: Story

Title:

> Help Luna Find Five Farm Animals | English for Kids

Thumbnail:

> Luna holding an empty animal checklist while a cow hides behind the barn.

#### Option 2: Question

Title:

> Can You Name These Five Farm Animals?

Thumbnail:

> Three animal silhouettes and Luna pointing toward them.

#### Option 3: Series

Title:

> Luna’s Word Adventure: Farm Animals

Thumbnail:

> Luna in the center with five clearly separated animals.

Recommended first test: **Option 1**, because it combines a story problem with a clear educational subject.

## 10. Packaging examples for four pillars

### Word Adventures

Titles:

- Help Luna Find Five Colors
- Learn Fruit Names with Milo’s Picnic
- What Is Hiding in the Red Backpack?

Thumbnail concept:

- Character plus three target objects and a visual question.

### Sound Safari

Titles:

- The Letter B Adventure: Bear, Ball, Banana
- Can You Hear the S Sound?
- Find Three Words That Begin with M

Thumbnail concept:

- Large letter, character listening, two or three matching objects.

### Little Life Stories

Titles:

- Mia’s First Day at Preschool
- How Does Luna Feel Today?
- Milo Learns to Share

Thumbnail concept:

- Character emotion plus one clear situation.

### Sing and Repeat

Titles:

- Clap, Jump, Turn Around! | Action Song
- Hello and Goodbye | Gentle English Song
- Five Happy Animals | Count and Sing

Thumbnail concept:

- One physical action, musical cue, and limited characters.

## 11. Diagnose packaging performance

### Low impressions

Possible causes:

- Topic has limited demand.
- Channel lacks history in the topic.
- Video needs more time or stronger related content.

Do not assume the thumbnail is the only problem.

### Impressions but low CTR

Possible causes:

- Thumbnail is unclear.
- Title does not communicate value.
- Topic appears too general.
- Packaging looks similar to stronger alternatives.

Action:

> Test a clearer title or thumbnail without changing the video itself.

### Strong CTR but weak retention

Possible causes:

- Packaging promises something the opening delays or does not deliver.
- The story setup is too long.
- The child cannot understand the activity.

Action:

> Improve the opening and alignment between packaging and content.

### Strong CTR and retention

Action:

> Produce a related sequel using the same packaging logic.

---

## 12. Thumbnail review checklist

### Clarity

- Can the topic be understood at a small size?
- Is there one primary focal point?
- Are the objects visually recognizable?

### Honesty

- Does the scene or object appear in the video?
- Is the emotion proportional to the story?
- Does the title match the lesson?

### Child safety

- No frightening imagery.
- No dangerous imitation prompt.
- No humiliating or manipulative expression.
- No inappropriate adult theme.

### Brand consistency

- Character appearance is correct.
- Colors match the series.
- Font and layout are consistent.
- The thumbnail is distinguishable from previous episodes.

---

## 14. Quiz

1. What are the two jobs of title and thumbnail packaging?
2. How many focal elements should a thumbnail usually have?
3. Why should thumbnail text remain short?
4. What does strong CTR but weak retention suggest?
5. Why should three packaging options be created before publication?

---

## 15. Quiz answers

1. Explain the educational topic to the right viewer or adult and create honest child-friendly curiosity.
2. Usually one main character, one primary object or mystery, and at most a small supporting element.
3. Small-screen readability is limited, and young children may respond more to the image.
4. The packaging attracts clicks, but the video does not deliver the promise quickly or clearly enough.
5. It forces the team to compare different strategic directions rather than accept the first idea.

- [ ] CTR is interpreted with impressions and retention.
