# LittlePlanetWonders Growth Course

## Lesson 5 of 16: Educational Episode Planning

**Study time:** 15 minutes  
**Practice time:** 15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Convert a broad topic into a measurable learning objective.
- Select an age-appropriate vocabulary load.
- Build a simple educational story.
- Plan participation, repetition, and assessment moments.
- Create an episode brief that the teachers and AI expert can use.

---

## 2. Start with learning, not visuals

A common AI-production mistake is to generate attractive scenes before defining what the child should learn.

The correct order is:

1. Choose the child and level.
2. Define the learning objective.
3. Select the target language.
4. Decide how the child will practise it.
5. Build the story around that practice.
6. Design shots and animation.

Visuals must make the meaning clearer, not merely decorate the video.

---

## 3. Topic versus learning objective

### Topic

> Farm animals

### Measurable objective

> By the end of the episode, a beginner child aged 3–5 can recognize and attempt to say cow, sheep, horse, pig, and chicken.

A strong objective specifies:

- Who is learning.
- What they will learn.
- How many items or skills are involved.
- What observable response is expected.

---

## 4. Vocabulary-load rules

Recommended starting ranges:

| Age/level | New items in one lesson | Typical language |
|---|---:|---|
| Ages 2–3 | 2–4 | Single words and short phrases |
| Ages 3–5 | 4–7 | Words plus simple repeated sentences |
| Ages 5–6 | 5–8 | Words, sentences, sound patterns, sequencing |

Do not treat every spoken word as a target word. The script can contain familiar supporting language while concentrating practice on a small target set.

---

## 5. The five-stage educational episode

### Stage 1: Need to know

Create an immediate reason to learn.

Example:

> Luna must find the five animals before the farm gate closes.

### Stage 2: First model

Introduce the word clearly with matching audio and visual evidence.

> “A cow. This is a cow.”

### Stage 3: Guided repetition

Ask for a manageable response.

> “Say it with me: cow.”

Leave enough silence for the child to respond.

### Stage 4: Recognition test

Present choices or a new context.

> “Which one is the cow?”

### Stage 5: Recap and transfer

Review the items and use at least one in a simple sentence.

> “The cow is near the red barn.”

---

## 6. Repetition with variation

Bad repetition:

> “Cow. Cow. Cow. Cow. Cow.”

Meaningful repetition:

1. See the cow.
2. Hear “cow.”
3. Repeat “cow.”
4. Choose the cow from two animals.
5. Hear “The cow says moo.”
6. Recall the cow in the recap.

The word repeats, but the child performs a different mental action each time.

---

## 7. Participation design

Use several types of participation:

### Repeat

> “Say ‘yellow’.”

### Point or choose

> “Which balloon is yellow?”

### Predict

> “What do you think is behind the tree?”

### Move

> “Jump like a frog.”

### Remember

> “Which animal did we find first?”

### Correct a character

> “Milo said ‘horse.’ Is he right?”

Do not ask questions and reveal the answer immediately. Include a visible and audible pause.

---

## 8. Story complexity rules

For ages 3–5, use:

- One main problem.
- One clear goal.
- Few locations.
- A small cast.
- Limited emotional intensity.
- A positive resolution.

Avoid:

- Several unrelated subplots.
- Complex villains.
- Long explanations.
- Sudden frightening danger.
- Moral lessons that overwhelm the language objective.

---

## 9. Example episode brief

### Working title

Help Luna Find Five Farm Animals

### Audience

Ages 3–5, beginner English

### Learning objective

Recognize and attempt to say cow, sheep, horse, pig, and chicken.

### Review language

- Hello
- Where is…?
- I see…
- Here it is!

### Story problem

Five animals are hiding around the farm.

### Learning sequence

1. Cow near the barn.
2. Sheep behind a fence.
3. Horse beside a tree.
4. Pig near a puddle.
5. Chicken inside the coop.

### Participation

- Repeat each name.
- Choose between two animals.
- Predict the next location.
- Remember all five during the recap.

### Ending

The animals return, Luna thanks the child, and the next adventure is teased.

### Related Shorts

1. Which animal says moo?
2. Can you find the sheep?

## 11. Teacher-review checklist

### Educational review

- Is the objective measurable?
- Is the vocabulary load suitable?
- Are examples accurate?
- Is repetition meaningful?
- Does the child have enough response time?
- Does the recap test the objective?

### Language review

- Is pronunciation correct and consistent?
- Are sentences natural and simple?
- Are instructions understandable?
- Is any word ambiguous for international viewers?

### Child-safety review

- Is the problem non-frightening?
- Are character behaviors respectful?
- Is the pacing calm enough?
- Are sounds and visual changes controlled?
- Does the story avoid pressure, shame, or manipulation?

---

## 12. Stop, improve, or approve

### Stop production when

- The objective is still vague.
- The script teaches too many new items.
- Visual generation has started before the educational sequence is approved.
- The story requires long explanation.

### Improve when

- The child only watches and never participates.
- The word appears only once.
- The recap introduces new material.
- The characters distract from the target language.

### Approve when

- A child can understand the goal visually.
- Each target item is modelled, practised, and reviewed.
- Both teachers approve the language and age fit.
- The AI expert can produce the episode consistently.

- Red
- Blue

- Green
- Purple

- Two recognition tests.
- One memory question.

## 14. Quiz

1. What should be defined before visual production begins?
2. How many new items are recommended for ages 3–5?
3. What is repetition with variation?
4. Why is response time important after a question?
5. What are the five stages of the educational episode?

---

## 15. Quiz answers

1. The audience, objective, target language, and learning sequence.
2. Approximately 4–7 new items.
3. Repeating the same target through different actions such as seeing, saying, choosing, applying, and recalling.
4. The child needs time to process and answer rather than only hear the character answer.
5. Need to know, first model, guided repetition, recognition test, recap and transfer.
