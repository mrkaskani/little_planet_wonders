# LittlePlanetWonders Growth Course

## Lesson 9 of 16: Search and Topic Research

**Study time:** 15 minutes  
**Practice time:** 15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Research topics that parents and teachers are already looking for.
- Separate adult search intent from child-facing creative appeal.
- Build topic clusters instead of publishing isolated ideas.
- Identify useful competitor gaps without copying competitors.
- Score and prioritize ideas for LittlePlanetWonders.
- Build a twelve-week topic bank.

---

## 2. Search has two audiences

LittlePlanetWonders serves two audiences at the same time.

### The adult searches

A parent or teacher may search for:

- English for preschool children
- Farm animals in English
- Phonics letter B
- Daily routines in English for kids
- English feelings vocabulary
- Calm educational videos for toddlers

The adult wants to understand the educational value, age, level, and safety of the video.

### The child chooses emotionally

The child may respond to:

- A familiar character
- A mystery
- An animal
- A clear emotion
- A colorful object
- A simple challenge

Therefore, the title should explain the learning value, while the story and thumbnail create child-friendly curiosity.

### Example

Adult search need:

> Farm animals in English for preschoolers

Child-facing concept:

> Luna must find five missing farm animals before bedtime.

Possible title:

> Help Luna Find Five Farm Animals | Beginner English for Kids

## 3. Six reliable topic sources

### Source 1: Your teachers

Ask the two teachers to record:

- Words children commonly confuse
- Topics repeatedly requested by parents
- Classroom activities children enjoy
- Pronunciation problems
- Concepts that require visual demonstration
- Seasonal curriculum needs

Teacher knowledge is valuable because it is based on real learning problems, not only popularity.

### Source 2: YouTube Studio search terms

When the channel receives enough search traffic, review the search terms that already bring viewers to your videos.

Look for:

- Repeated topics
- Unexpected phrases
- Age-related wording
- Countries or languages appearing in traffic
- Search phrases that lead to strong retention

Do not build conclusions from one or two views. Record patterns across several weeks.

### Source 3: YouTube search suggestions

Type a broad phrase manually and record relevant suggestions.

Examples:

- English for kids...
- Farm animals...
- Phonics for preschool...
- Feelings in English...
- Bedtime routine...

Suggestions can reveal common wording, but they do not guarantee that a topic is easy to rank for or suitable for your channel.

### Source 4: Parent and teacher communities

Study questions in:

- Preschool teacher groups
- Homeschooling communities
- ESL and EFL communities
- Parent forums
- School newsletters
- Language-learning communities

Do not spam links. Observe recurring needs and answer helpfully when participation is permitted.

### Source 5: Curriculum and developmental needs

Build a stable topic bank from beginner learning categories:

- Greetings
- Colors
- Numbers
- Animals
- Food
- Body parts
- Clothing
- Weather
- Family
- Emotions
- Daily routines
- Classroom language
- Phonics
- Simple location words

These topics may not always look fashionable, but they remain useful to parents and teachers.

### Source 6: Competitor gaps

Study successful channels to understand:

- Which ages they serve
- Which topics they repeat
- Which videos recently performed well
- Which questions remain unanswered
- Which formats feel too loud, long, advanced, or generic

The goal is not to copy a story, character, title, song, or thumbnail. The goal is to find an unmet audience need.

## 4. Search-intent categories

### Learn a word set

Examples:

- Animals in English
- Fruit names for kids
- Colors for preschool

Best format:

> Story-based vocabulary adventure

### Practise a sound

Examples:

- Letter B sound
- Beginning sounds for preschool
- Phonics S words

Best format:

> Sound hunt or clue game

### Understand a routine

Examples:

- Morning routine in English
- Bedtime vocabulary
- Getting ready for school

Best format:

> Short daily-life story

### Solve a parenting or teaching need

Examples:

- Calm English learning video
- Five-minute ESL activity
- Preschool listening practice

Best format:

> Clearly structured lesson with an adult-facing description

### Seasonal or event-based need

Examples:

- Back-to-school English
- Winter clothing vocabulary
- Spring animals
- Holiday greetings

Use seasonal topics only when they remain culturally appropriate for the target audience.

## 5. Build topic clusters

A topic cluster is a connected group of videos that can recommend and reinforce one another.

### Example cluster: Farm English

1. Farm Animals Word Adventure
2. What Sounds Do Farm Animals Make?
3. Letter C: Cow, Cat, Carrot
4. Where Is the Pig? In, On, and Under
5. Farm Animals Sing and Repeat
6. Farm Vocabulary Compilation

One successful topic can therefore become several distinct lessons without repeating the same video.

### Cluster rule

Each follow-up must add a new learning outcome.

Do not simply change one animal or color while keeping the same script, visuals, and outcome.

## 6. The LittlePlanetWonders topic score

Score every idea from 1 to 5.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Audience fit | Is it useful for beginner children aged 3–5? |
| Search clarity | Can an adult easily describe or search for it? |
| Child appeal | Can it become a visual mystery, action, or story? |
| Educational value | Is there a clear measurable learning objective? |
| Series potential | Can it produce useful follow-up episodes? |
| Production feasibility | Can the team produce it within one week? |
| Brand fit | Does it match calm, teacher-reviewed English learning? |

Maximum score: **35**

### Suggested interpretation

- **29–35:** Prioritize
- **23–28:** Useful, but improve the concept
- **17–22:** Keep in the idea bank
- **Below 17:** Pause or redesign

This is an internal decision tool, not a prediction of views.

---

## 7. Topic-versus-format testing

Do not confuse a topic problem with a format problem.

### Test a topic

Keep these stable:

- Luna as the main character
- Four-to-six-minute length
- Word Adventure structure
- Similar thumbnail layout

Change only the theme:

- Farm animals
- Fruit
- Clothing
- Weather

### Test a format

Keep the topic similar but change the teaching method:

- Search adventure
- Guessing game
- Short story
- Song and movement

Changing one major variable makes the result easier to interpret.

## 9. Search-friendly description structure

Use descriptions for adults, not keyword lists.

### Template

> In this teacher-reviewed English adventure, children aged 3–5 help Luna find five farm animals: cow, sheep, horse, pig, and chicken. The episode supports beginner vocabulary, listening, pronunciation, and simple participation. Parents and teachers can pause after each question to give children more answering time.

Then add:

- Recommended age
- Learning objective
- Target words
- Related series or lesson
- Necessary sponsorship disclosure
- Appropriate rights or credit information

Avoid repeating the same phrase unnaturally many times.

## 10. Competitor analysis without copying

Create a spreadsheet with these columns:

- Channel
- Primary age
- Main topic
- Format
- Video length
- Title pattern
- Thumbnail pattern
- Opening mechanism
- Character system
- Most-viewed topics
- Recent successful topics
- Parent/teacher positioning
- Missing need
- Original opportunity for LittlePlanetWonders

### Example inference

Observation:

> Several competitors teach colors through songs, but few use a calm story and participation pauses.

Original opportunity:

> Create a gentle mystery story where Luna restores colors to a garden and children answer before each reveal.

That is differentiation, not copying.

---

## 11. When to stop, improve, or scale a topic

### Stop temporarily

- Three similar episodes receive little interest and weak retention.
- The topic is difficult to explain visually.
- It does not suit the target age.
- Production effort is much higher than its learning value.

### Improve

- Search impressions appear, but viewers do not click.
- Adults click, but children leave early.
- The concept is useful, but the story is confusing.

### Scale

- More than one related video performs above your channel baseline.
- Search terms clearly match the topic.
- The topic gains both watch time and subscribers.
- Teachers can extend it into meaningful lessons.

## 13. Quiz

1. Why does LittlePlanetWonders need both adult search value and child appeal?
2. What is the difference between a topic and a topic cluster?
3. Why should competitor research focus on gaps rather than copying?
4. What is the maximum LittlePlanetWonders topic score?
5. Why should only one major variable be changed during a test?

---

## 14. Quiz answers

1. Adults usually search for and approve the content, while children need a clear, enjoyable experience.
2. A topic is one subject; a cluster is a connected group of distinct lessons around that subject.
3. Gaps reveal unmet needs while preserving originality and trust.
4. 35.
5. So the team can identify which change most likely influenced the result.
