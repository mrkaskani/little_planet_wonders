# LittlePlanetWonders Growth Course

## Lesson 14 of 16: Analytics and Content Testing

**Study time:** 15 minutes  
**Practice time:** 15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Build a weekly analytics dashboard.
- Interpret performance for a very small channel.
- Separate reach, appeal, engagement, continuation, and business value.
- Identify retention dips and spikes.
- Run one-variable content experiments.
- Decide when to stop, improve, or scale a format.

---

## 2. Analytics answer questions

Do not collect metrics only because YouTube displays them.

Every metric should answer a decision question.

| Question | Useful evidence |
|---|---|
| Did YouTube show the video? | Impressions, Shorts feed appearances |
| Did suitable viewers choose it? | CTR, chose to view versus swiped away |
| Did the opening work? | First-30-second retention, early Short behavior |
| Did the lesson hold attention? | Average view duration, average percentage viewed |
| Did viewers return? | Returning, casual, and regular viewers; views per viewer |
| Did the video build the channel? | Subscribers gained, related-video viewing |
| Did adults value it? | Worksheet downloads, shares, teacher inquiries |
| Did it create business interest? | Sponsor or partnership inquiries |

## 4. Small-channel warning

LittlePlanetWonders currently has very few views. One viewer can dramatically change a percentage.

Example:

- 10 viewers
- 6 complete most of the video
- 60% apparent completion behavior

If one additional viewer leaves early, the result changes significantly.

Therefore:

- Do not declare a winner from one upload.
- Group similar videos.
- Review several weeks.
- Combine quantitative and qualitative evidence.
- Record uncertainty.

---

## 5. Internal diagnostic bands

These ranges are planning aids, not universal YouTube benchmarks.

### Long-video average percentage viewed

| Result | Internal interpretation |
|---|---|
| Above 55% | Strong for review; examine what worked |
| 40–55% | Promising; improve weak moments |
| 30–40% | Needs structural review |
| Below 30% | Concerning; examine topic, opening, clarity, and length |

### First-30-second retention

| Result | Internal interpretation |
|---|---|
| Above 75% | Opening likely communicates clearly |
| 60–75% | Usable; investigate small losses |
| 45–60% | Opening may be slow or confusing |
| Below 45% | Strong redesign candidate |

These bands must be interpreted with video length, traffic source, topic, age, sample size, and channel history.

For Shorts, compare each Short with your own typical performance and current Shorts analytics rather than applying long-video percentages.

## 6. Retention graph diagnosis

Official YouTube Analytics provides video-level audience-retention reports and may compare videos with content of similar length.

### Early cliff

Possible causes:

- Long logo
- Misleading title or thumbnail
- Slow explanation
- Child cannot understand the mission
- Loud or uncomfortable audio

### Gradual decline

Normal attention loss may be occurring, but review:

- Repetitive teaching
- Weak story progress
- Excessive lesson length

### Sharp dip

Possible causes:

- Confusing scene
- Repeated word with no variation
- Audio problem
- Frightening or strange visual
- Adult-focused explanation inside child content

### Spike

Possible causes:

- Viewers replay a difficult word
- A funny or surprising moment
- A useful answer reveal
- A confusing section that required rewatching

A spike is not automatically positive. Teachers should inspect the moment.

## 8. One-variable testing

Choose one primary variable:

- Topic
- Hook
- Title
- Thumbnail
- Length
- Character
- Lesson structure
- Publishing time
- Short format

Keep other major elements as stable as practical.

### Example thumbnail test

Stable:

- Same episode
- Same title
- Same audience
- Same video

Change:

- Character expression and visual question

### Example hook test

Stable:

- Farm vocabulary
- Luna
- Five target words
- Similar length

Change:

- Missing-animals mystery versus direct teaching opening

---

## 9. Experiment card

### Hypothesis

> A visual mystery in the first three seconds will improve first-30-second retention compared with a spoken greeting.

### Primary metric

> First-30-second retention

### Secondary metric

> Average percentage viewed

### Controlled elements

- Topic
- Character
- Length
- Vocabulary load
- Thumbnail style

### Decision rule

> Use the mystery opening in the next two related episodes if it performs better and teacher review confirms clarity.

### Risks

- Sample too small
- Traffic sources differ
- Topic interest differs

---

## 10. Weekly review meeting

Keep the meeting to approximately 30 minutes.

### Part 1: Facts

- What was published?
- What traffic appeared?
- Where did viewers leave?
- Which output created subscribers or adult value?

### Part 2: Interpretation

- What are the likely causes?
- What is uncertain?
- What did teachers observe?

### Part 3: Decision

Choose only:

- One thing to stop
- One thing to improve
- One thing to repeat
- One experiment for the next week

---

## 11. Eight-week testing queue

| Week | Variable | Test |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | Mystery versus greeting |
| 2 | Topic | Animals versus colors |
| 3 | Thumbnail | Question composition versus character portrait |
| 4 | Length | Four minutes versus six minutes |
| 5 | Participation | One-second versus three-second answer pause |
| 6 | Character | Luna lead versus Milo lead |
| 7 | Short opening | Question versus character mistake |
| 8 | Adult resource | Worksheet versus parent activity guide |

Do not treat these as perfectly controlled laboratory experiments. They are structured production tests.

---

## 12. Stop, improve, or scale matrix

### Stop temporarily

- Three comparable episodes perform below baseline.
- Teacher review finds weak learning value.
- Production cost cannot be sustained.
- The format attracts unrelated traffic.

### Improve

- Impressions are present but CTR is weak.
- CTR is strong but opening retention is weak.
- Retention is strong but subscriber conversion is weak.
- Shorts gain views but do not create returning viewers.

### Scale

- A pattern succeeds in more than one episode.
- It remains educationally strong.
- Production is repeatable.
- It builds returning viewers, subscribers, or adult trust.

## 14. Practice cases

### Case A

- Impressions increase.
- CTR is low.
- Viewers who click watch most of the lesson.

Decision:

> Improve title and thumbnail before changing the lesson structure.

### Case B

- CTR is high.
- Most viewers leave in the first 15 seconds.

Decision:

> Align the opening with the packaging and start the activity sooner.

### Case C

- A Short receives many views.
- It gains few subscribers.
- Long-video traffic does not increase.

Decision:

> Strengthen character and series connection; verify that the Short attracts the intended audience.

### Case D

- A video has moderate views.
- It gains more subscribers per 100 views than other videos.
- Teachers report strong classroom use.

Decision:

> Create a related sequel even though raw views are not the highest.

## 16. Quiz

1. Why should every metric answer a decision question?
2. Why are percentages unstable for a very small channel?
3. What can a sharp retention dip indicate?
4. What is the purpose of one-variable testing?
5. When should a format be scaled?

---

## 17. Quiz answers

1. Metrics are useful only when they help the team decide what to stop, improve, or scale.
2. One or two viewers can significantly change the result.
3. Confusion, repetition, audio problems, uncomfortable visuals, or a mismatch in audience focus.
4. To make the likely cause of a performance difference easier to identify.
5. After it succeeds more than once, remains educationally strong, and can be produced sustainably.
