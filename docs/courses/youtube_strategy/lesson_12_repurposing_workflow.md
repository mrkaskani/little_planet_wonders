# LittlePlanetWonders Growth Course

## Lesson 12 of 16: Repurposing Workflow

**Study time:** 15 minutes  
**Practice time:** 15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Turn one long lesson into a complete weekly content package.
- Separate reusable assets from platform-specific outputs.
- Repurpose without creating repetitive or low-value content.
- Assign tasks to the teachers and AI expert.
- Create a manageable production board.
- Build an archive that reduces future production cost.

---

## 2. Repurposing is adaptation, not duplication

Duplication means publishing the same material everywhere without considering context.

Repurposing means extracting different useful outcomes from one educational source.

### Source lesson

> Help Luna Find Five Farm Animals

### Distinct outputs

- A long listening and vocabulary adventure
- A one-word guessing Short
- A five-word recall Short
- A parent-facing teaching Reel
- A teacher activity carousel
- A vocabulary worksheet
- Three Pinterest pins
- A campaign sample for future sponsors

Every output has a different job.

---

## 3. The master lesson package

Before editing social outputs, create one organized source package.

### Folder structure

```text
farm_animals_episode/
├── 01_brief/
├── 02_script/
├── 03_voice/
├── 04_character_references/
├── 05_backgrounds/
├── 06_shots/
├── 07_music_sfx/
├── 08_long_video/
├── 09_shorts/
├── 10_social/
├── 11_printables/
├── 12_thumbnails/
└── 13_rights_and_approvals/
```

The exact tool does not matter. The important rule is that the source, approvals, and usage rights remain traceable.

---

## 4. Asset inventory

After completing the long video, record reusable assets.

| Asset | Reuse possibility |
|---|---|
| Character close-ups | Thumbnails, Reels, Shorts |
| Clean character poses | Future episodes and worksheets |
| Object images | Vocabulary cards and pins |
| Backgrounds | Related episodes, with meaningful variation |
| Voice words | Pronunciation review, not automatic reuse without context |
| Music | Only where licence and mood permit |
| Sound effects | Similar actions, with volume review |
| Story clips | Shorts and social previews |
| Teacher notes | Captions, worksheets, lesson plans |

Do not reuse an asset when doing so creates visual inconsistency, incorrect meaning, or repetitive mass production.

---

## 5. One-to-many workflow

### Step 1: Produce the long lesson first

The long lesson contains the complete educational structure.

### Step 2: Mark extraction moments

During review, label:

- Best opening
- Strongest question
- Best pronunciation model
- Most visually clear object
- Strongest emotional moment
- Best recap

### Step 3: Create two Shorts

- Discovery question
- Reinforcement test

### Step 4: Create adult-facing outputs

- Teaching-tip Reel
- Carousel
- Facebook post
- Teacher resource

### Step 5: Create evergreen assets

- Pinterest pins
- Printable worksheet
- Vocabulary card

### Step 6: Archive successful elements

Record what worked so the next episode can reuse the method, not merely duplicate the media.

## 6. Required weekly output package

From one long lesson, create:

| Output | Quantity | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| YouTube long lesson | 1 | Complete learning experience |
| YouTube Shorts | 2 | Discovery and reinforcement |
| Instagram Reels | 2 | Parent/teacher discovery |
| Instagram carousel | 1 | Saveable learning resource |
| Facebook post | 1 | Adult explanation and community reach |
| Pinterest pins | 3 | Evergreen search |
| Teacher resource | 1 | Trust and practical use |
| Email/newsletter item | 1 | Parent/teacher relationship, when available |

At the beginning, you may skip the email item until you have a lawful adult-focused mailing system.

## 8. Platform adaptation checklist

For every output, change at least one of these according to the platform:

- Opening
- Aspect ratio
- Caption
- Text size
- Duration
- Adult or child perspective
- Call to action
- Educational depth
- Search wording

### Example

Child-facing Short:

> “Where is the sheep?”

Adult-facing Reel:

> “Pause for two seconds after the question to give preschool learners time to answer.”

Same source, different value.

## 9. Team responsibilities

### Teacher 1: Learning extraction

- Select target words.
- Identify the best participation moment.
- Create worksheet tasks.
- Write parent/teacher guidance.

### Teacher 2: Quality review

- Verify pronunciation and grammar.
- Check that clips remain understandable outside the long video.
- Review captions and printables.
- Approve child-safety and learning claims.

### AI expert: Production and adaptation

- Maintain the asset library.
- Export required aspect ratios.
- Edit Shorts and Reels.
- Create thumbnails, pins, and carousel layouts.
- Maintain filenames and version history.
- Store rights and tool-usage records.

---

## 12. Quality-control gates

### Gate 1: Educational accuracy

- Correct word and meaning
- Correct pronunciation
- Suitable vocabulary load
- Clear participation

### Gate 2: Visual continuity

- Correct character design
- Correct object
- No strange anatomy
- Stable clothing and colors

### Gate 3: Audio quality

- Speech is understandable
- Music does not cover speech
- Volume is stable
- No missing or duplicated words

### Gate 4: Rights and compliance

- Music and assets are licensed
- AI tool terms allow intended use
- Sponsorship is disclosed
- No child personal data is collected

### Gate 5: Platform adaptation

- Correct aspect ratio
- Readable text
- Useful opening
- Appropriate caption and CTA

---

## 13. Avoid low-value repurposing

Do not:

- Upload the same Short repeatedly with tiny changes.
- Replace only one word in an otherwise identical video.
- Create dozens of automatic slideshows.
- Publish clips that make no sense without the long episode.
- Use generic AI visuals with no teacher-designed learning value.
- Flood platforms with the same caption and link.

YouTube’s current monetization and spam guidance emphasizes original, authentic value and warns against mass-produced, repetitive, or generic AI content. Verify the current official wording before scaling automation.

---

## 14. Archive for future compilations

Tag each approved segment with:

- Topic
- Age
- Learning level
- Character
- Target word
- Location
- Format
- Duration
- Rights status
- Performance result

Example:

```text
topic: farm_animals
age: 3-5
word: cow
character: luna
format: recognition_question
rights: cleared
performance: strong_pause_response
```

This makes it possible to create a future compilation with a coherent educational sequence rather than random clips.

## 16. Quiz

1. What is the difference between duplication and repurposing?
2. Why should the long lesson usually be produced first?
3. What should be marked during the long-video review?
4. Why must clips be reviewed outside their original context?
5. What is the difference between a process template and repetitive content?

---

## 17. Quiz answers

1. Duplication republishes the same material; repurposing adapts the source into distinct useful outputs.
2. It contains the complete learning structure and strongest source material.
3. Strong openings, questions, pronunciation, visual moments, emotions, and recap segments.
4. A clip may become confusing, inaccurate, or incomplete when separated from the full episode.
5. A process template standardizes quality, while repetitive content makes the viewer experience interchangeable and low value.
