# LittlePlanetWonders Growth Course

## Lesson 11 of 16: Social Media Strategy

**Study time:** 15 minutes  
**Practice time:** 15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson objective

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Choose social platforms that match the team’s capacity.
- Market primarily to parents, teachers, schools, and caregivers.
- Adapt one idea to different platform behaviors.
- Create captions, search terms, and calls to action.
- Protect child privacy and avoid inappropriate underage targeting.
- Build a realistic weekly distribution schedule.

---

## 2. The social-media audience

LittlePlanetWonders videos are created for children, but social-media marketing should primarily speak to adults.

Target:

- Parents
- Preschool teachers
- ESL and EFL teachers
- Homeschooling families
- Schools
- Libraries
- Tutoring centers
- Educational partners

Do not create engagement systems that ask young children to provide personal details, send direct messages, join external groups, or operate accounts against platform age rules.

---

## 3. Platform priority

With one AI expert and two teachers, do not attempt to dominate every platform.

### Tier 1: Primary

- YouTube
- YouTube Shorts
- Instagram

### Tier 2: Evergreen and community

- Pinterest
- Facebook
- Teacher and homeschooling communities

### Tier 3: Optional experiment

- TikTok, where account operation, content, audience, and local rules make it appropriate

Focus on consistent quality before increasing platform count.

---

## 4. Platform strategy matrix

| Platform | Primary audience | Recommended content | Typical duration | Starting frequency | Primary purpose |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| YouTube long-form | Children with adult selection | Complete lessons and stories | 4–7 min | 1/week | Learning and repeat viewing |
| YouTube Shorts | Children and adults | One-word tests and mini lessons | 20–40 sec | 2/week | Discovery and practice |
| Instagram Reels | Parents and teachers | Lesson clips and teaching tips | 15–45 sec | 2/week | Adult discovery and trust |
| Instagram carousel | Parents and teachers | Vocabulary cards and activity steps | 5–8 slides | 1/week | Saves and educational value |
| Pinterest | Parents and teachers | Worksheets, vocabulary pins, lesson previews | Static or short video | 3 pins/week | Evergreen search |
| Facebook | Parents, schools, communities | Educational explanation and resource | Short clip or post | 1/week | Community distribution |
| TikTok | Adult educators and parents | Fast teaching idea or lesson clip | 15–45 sec | 1–2/week test | Additional discovery |
| Teacher communities | Teachers | Resource, activity, or case study | Platform-dependent | 1 useful contribution/week | Trust and partnerships |

Platform features and recommendation systems change. Verify current posting, age, advertising, music, and disclosure rules in each platform’s official documentation.

---

## 5. Instagram strategy

### Reels

Use a child-safe video clip, but write the caption for adults.

Example opening text:

> A 30-second English activity for preschool learners

Caption structure:

1. Adult problem
2. Learning objective
3. How to use the activity
4. Recommended age
5. Clear YouTube call to action

### Example caption

> Practising farm-animal vocabulary with a preschool learner? Pause after each question and let the child answer before Luna reveals the animal. This activity introduces cow, sheep, horse, pig, and chicken for beginner learners aged 3–5. The complete teacher-reviewed adventure is available on LittlePlanetWonders.

### Carousel idea

Slide 1:

> Five Farm Animal Words

Slides 2–6:

- One animal
- One clear image
- One short sentence

Slide 7:

> Try the complete listening adventure on LittlePlanetWonders.

### Search and hashtags

Use a small set of relevant terms rather than a large block of unrelated hashtags.

Examples:

- preschool English
- English for kids
- ESL activities
- beginner vocabulary
- teacher resources

---

## 6. Pinterest strategy

Pinterest can support evergreen adult search.

Create three pins from each long lesson:

1. Vocabulary reference pin
2. Printable activity pin
3. Video-lesson preview pin

### Pin title example

> Five Farm Animal Words in English for Preschool

### Pin description example

> A beginner English vocabulary activity for children aged 3–5, featuring cow, sheep, horse, pig, and chicken. Use it at home, in preschool, or in an ESL classroom.

### Visual rules

- Vertical layout
- Large readable title
- One clear character or object
- Adult-facing educational benefit
- Consistent branding

Do not use a child’s personal image or information without appropriate permission and legal review.

---

## 7. Facebook strategy

Facebook content should explain how adults can use the lesson.

### Post structure

- What children learn
- Recommended age
- Participation suggestion
- Link or channel direction
- Optional printable resource

### Example

> This week’s LittlePlanetWonders lesson helps beginner learners aged 3–5 practise five farm-animal words. Parents and teachers can pause after each clue and ask the child to point, say, or imitate the animal. The complete teacher-reviewed adventure is now available on YouTube.

### Groups

Before posting in a group:

- Read the rules.
- Contribute useful information.
- Avoid repeated self-promotion.
- Share a resource when links are not allowed.
- Ask administrators before promotional collaborations.

---

## 8. TikTok strategy

Use TikTok only as an adult-operated brand channel and only where appropriate under current platform and local rules.

Possible content:

- One quick preschool English activity
- A teacher explaining how to use a video
- Before-and-after production insight for adults
- A vocabulary game excerpt
- A printable-resource demonstration

Avoid:

- Asking children to duet, message, or reveal information
- Direct purchase pressure toward young viewers
- Reusing music without commercial rights
- Copying trends that conflict with the calm brand

The objective is not to imitate every trend. It is to reach adults who value educational content.

---

## 9. Teacher and school communities

Create value before asking for attention.

Useful contributions:

- A free vocabulary worksheet
- A five-minute classroom activity
- A pronunciation guide
- A lesson plan using one episode
- A short analysis of what children practise

### Partnership message principle

Do not write:

> “Please promote our channel.”

Write:

> “We created a teacher-reviewed farm-animal listening activity for beginner learners aged 3–5. We would value feedback on whether the pacing and vocabulary load work in your classroom.”

Feedback requests can create stronger relationships than immediate promotion requests.

---

## 10. Platform-native adaptation

Do not publish the identical file, caption, and opening everywhere.

### YouTube Short

Child-facing question:

> “Which animal says moo?”

### Instagram Reel

Adult-facing text:

> “A 30-second listening activity for preschool English.”

### Facebook post

Adult explanation:

> “Pause before the answer to increase participation.”

### Pinterest pin

Evergreen title:

> “Farm Animal Vocabulary Activity for Preschool.”

The learning asset is the same, but the context changes.

## 11. Weekly social schedule

| Day | Platform | Output | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Friday | YouTube | Long lesson | AI expert + teacher approval |
| Saturday | YouTube Shorts | Discovery Short | AI expert |
| Sunday | Instagram | Reel from discovery Short | AI expert |
| Monday | Pinterest | Vocabulary pin | AI expert |
| Tuesday | YouTube Shorts | Reinforcement Short | AI expert |
| Wednesday | Instagram | Teacher-facing Reel or carousel | Teacher + AI expert |
| Thursday | Facebook or teacher group | Resource or learning explanation | Teacher |
| Flexible | Pinterest | Two additional pins | AI expert |

This schedule repurposes existing material rather than demanding daily original production.

---

## 12. Social-media metrics

Track by platform:

- Reach or impressions
- Video views
- Average watch behavior where available
- Saves
- Shares
- Profile visits
- Link clicks
- YouTube traffic from external sources
- Worksheet downloads
- Teacher inquiries
- Sponsor or partnership inquiries

Do not treat likes as the only success signal. A smaller post that produces worksheet downloads or teacher inquiries may have more strategic value.

---

## 13. Child-safety and rights checklist

Before publishing:

- The account is operated by an adult organization or team.
- No child personal data is requested.
- No unsafe challenge is demonstrated.
- No direct pressure asks children to purchase something.
- Music and visual usage rights cover the platform.
- Sponsorship is clearly disclosed.
- AI characters do not imitate a real child without permission.
- The caption is appropriate for parents and teachers.
- Links lead to safe, relevant destinations.

## 14. Practice

Take the long episode:

> **Help Luna Find Five Farm Animals**

Create:

1. One Instagram Reel concept and caption
2. One seven-slide Instagram carousel
3. Three Pinterest pin concepts
4. One Facebook post
5. One teacher-community contribution

For each output, define:

- Adult target audience
- Primary value
- Call to action
- Metric to monitor

---

## 15. Quiz

1. Who should social-media marketing primarily target?
2. Why should the same clip be adapted differently for each platform?
3. What are the three recommended Pinterest outputs from one lesson?
4. Why are saves, downloads, and teacher inquiries sometimes more useful than likes?
5. What should be checked before posting in a teacher or parent group?

---

## 16. Quiz answers

1. Parents, teachers, schools, caregivers, and educational partners.
2. Each platform has a different audience context, content format, and user intention.
3. A vocabulary reference pin, printable activity pin, and video-preview pin.
4. They show practical value, deeper interest, or a possible relationship rather than a light reaction.
5. The group rules and whether promotional links are permitted.

---

## 17. Completion checklist

- [ ] Primary and secondary platforms are selected.
- [ ] Social marketing targets adults rather than collecting child data.
- [ ] Every platform has a specific purpose.
- [ ] Captions communicate educational value.
- [ ] One lesson becomes several platform-native outputs.
- [ ] The team follows a realistic weekly schedule.
- [ ] Rights, disclosures, privacy, and platform rules are reviewed.
- [ ] Social traffic is connected back to YouTube and parent/teacher resources.
